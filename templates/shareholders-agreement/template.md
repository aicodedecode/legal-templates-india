# SHAREHOLDERS' AGREEMENT

> **Drafting note:** This agreement governs the relationship among the Company, its Founders and its Investors after a priced round in which the Investors subscribed to CCPS under a share subscription agreement (**SSA**). It assumes that the SSA covers the subscription mechanics, the conditions precedent and the bulk of the business warranties, and that this Agreement covers the ongoing relationship. Defined terms match the term sheet and iSAFE templates in this repository. Before execution, run a consistency check across the SSA, this Agreement and the restated Articles. Under Indian law, investor rights that are not in the Articles may not bind the Company (see clause 2). The Articles should therefore reproduce this Agreement almost word for word, typically as a separate Part B that prevails over the general provisions in Part A.

This Shareholders' Agreement (the **"Agreement"**) is entered into on {{effective_date}} by and among:

1. **{{company_name}}**, a private company incorporated under the Companies Act, 2013, with corporate identity number {{company_cin}}, having its registered office at {{company_address}} (the **"Company"**);

2. **{{founder_1_name}}**, {{founder_1_details}} (**"Founder 1"**);

3. **{{founder_2_name}}**, {{founder_2_details}} (**"Founder 2"**, and together with Founder 1, the **"Founders"**, and each a **"Founder"**);

4. **{{lead_investor_name}}**, {{lead_investor_details}} (the **"Lead Investor"**); and

5. **The persons listed in Part B of Schedule 1** (together with the Lead Investor, the **"Investors"**, and each an **"Investor"**).

The Company, the Founders and the Investors are each a **"Party"** and together the **"Parties"**. Each expression includes the Party's successors and permitted assigns, and, in the case of an individual, his or her heirs, executors, administrators and legal representatives.

## Recitals

A. The Company is engaged in the business of {{business_description}} (the **"Business"**).

B. Under a share subscription agreement dated {{ssa_date}} among the Company, the Founders and the Investors (the **"SSA"**), the Investors have subscribed to the {{ccps_series_name}} on the terms set out in it.

C. The Parties wish to record the terms that govern their relationship as shareholders of the Company, the management and operation of the Company, and their respective rights and obligations in relation to the Company and its Securities.

## 2. Effectiveness and Articles of Association

2.1 This Agreement comes into effect on the Closing Date, except for this clause 2 and clauses 1, 21, 26 and 27, which come into effect on the date of execution.

2.2 On or before the Closing Date, the Shareholders will vote to adopt, by special resolution under section 14 of the Act, restated Articles that incorporate the provisions of this Agreement to the extent they are capable of incorporation. The Company will file Form MGT-14 with the Registrar of Companies within 30 days of the special resolution being passed.

2.3 If any provision of this Agreement conflicts with the Articles, this Agreement will prevail as between the Shareholders. The Shareholders will exercise all voting and other rights available to them to amend the Articles promptly so that they conform to this Agreement.

2.4 The Founders will exercise their voting rights as Shareholders, and will procure that every director nominated by them exercises his or her rights (subject to his or her duties under section 166 of the Act), to give full effect to this Agreement.

> **Drafting note:** In *V.B. Rangaraj v. V.B. Gopalakrishnan* (1992), the Supreme Court held that a restriction on the transfer of shares that is not contained in the articles is not binding on the company or its shareholders. The Delhi High Court applied this reasoning to investor rights under a shareholders' agreement in *World Phone India Pvt Ltd v. WPI Group Inc* (2013) [verify citation]. Other decisions, such as *Messer Holdings Ltd v. Shyam Madanmohan Ruia* (Bombay High Court, 2010) [verify], have enforced shareholder agreements as contracts between the shareholders who signed them. For public companies, the proviso to section 58(2) of the Act makes contracts on the transfer of securities enforceable. Even so, always incorporate the agreement in the Articles. In *Tata Consultancy Services Ltd v. Cyrus Investments Pvt Ltd* (2021), the Supreme Court upheld affirmative voting rights that were contained in the articles, which shows how much weight the courts give to the articles. Check the MGT-14 period against the current rules [verify].

## 3. Board of Directors

3.1 **Management:** the business and affairs of the Company will be managed by the Board, subject to this Agreement, the Articles and the Act.

3.2 **Composition:** the Board will consist of not more than {{board_size}} directors, of whom:

(a) {{founder_director_count}} will be nominated by the Founders acting together, for so long as the Founders hold in aggregate at least {{founder_rights_threshold}} of the share capital and each nominating Founder remains in full-time employment of the Company;

(b) {{investor_director_count}} will be nominated by the Lead Investor, for so long as the Lead Investor holds at least {{investor_rights_threshold}} of the share capital; and

(c) the remaining directors (if any) will be independent directors appointed by the Board with the prior written consent of the Founders and the Investor Director.

3.3 **Appointment and removal:** each nominating Party may, by written notice to the Company, nominate, remove and replace the directors it is entitled to nominate. The Company and the Shareholders will take all steps required under the Act to give effect to each nomination, removal or replacement within 10 Business Days of the notice. This includes appointing the nominee as an additional director under section 161(1) of the Act and regularising the appointment at the next general meeting. No Shareholder will vote to remove a director nominated by another Shareholder, except at the request of the nominating Shareholder.

3.4 **Alternate directors:** each Investor Director may nominate an alternate director under section 161(2) of the Act, and the Board will appoint that alternate.

3.5 **Chairperson:** the chairperson of the Board will be a Founder director, who will not have a second or casting vote.

3.6 **Observer:** the Lead Investor may appoint one non-voting observer to the Board (the **"Observer"**). The Observer may attend all meetings of the Board and its committees, and will receive all notices, agenda, papers and minutes at the same time as directors. The Observer will be bound by the confidentiality obligations in clause 21. The Company may exclude the Observer from any part of a meeting to the extent necessary to preserve legal privilege or to manage a conflict of interest.

3.7 **Meetings and notice:** the Board will meet at least once every calendar quarter, so that not more than 120 days elapse between two consecutive meetings, in accordance with section 173 of the Act. At least 7 days' written notice of each meeting will be given to every director, accompanied by the agenda and supporting papers. A meeting may be held at shorter notice in accordance with section 173(3) of the Act, but no Reserved Matter may be considered at a meeting called at shorter notice unless the Investor Director is present or consents in writing.

3.8 **Participation:** directors may participate in Board meetings through video-conferencing or other audio-visual means, as permitted by the Act and the rules made under it.

3.9 **Quorum:** the quorum for a Board meeting will be the quorum required by section 174 of the Act, which must include the Investor Director, unless the Investor Director has waived this requirement in writing for that meeting. If a quorum is not present within 30 minutes of the scheduled time, the meeting will stand adjourned to the same time and place on the same day of the following week (or, if that is not a Business Day, the next Business Day). At the adjourned meeting, the directors present will constitute a quorum, subject to the Act. However, no Reserved Matter, and no matter not specified in the agenda of the original meeting, may be considered at the adjourned meeting without the prior written consent of the Investor Director.

3.10 **Voting:** subject to clause 5, each director has one vote, and decisions of the Board are taken by a simple majority of the directors present and voting.

3.11 **Circular resolutions:** a resolution may be passed by circulation in accordance with section 175 of the Act. A resolution on a Reserved Matter is not validly passed by circulation unless the Investor Director has voted in favour of it or the Investor Majority has consented to it in writing.

3.12 **Committees:** the Investor Director is entitled to be a member of every committee of the Board, and the quorum provisions of clause 3.9 apply to each committee, with the necessary changes.

3.13 **Subsidiaries:** the composition of the board of each subsidiary of the Company, and the rights of the Parties in relation to it, will mirror this clause 3, with the necessary changes.

3.14 **Non-executive status and liability:** each Investor Director and the Observer is non-executive and will not be responsible for the day-to-day management of the Company. The Company will not identify any Investor Director as an "officer who is in default", a compliance officer or an occupier, or as a person responsible for compliance with any Applicable Law. The Company will nominate a whole-time director or key managerial person for those purposes, and will make all required filings to record this.

3.15 **Insurance and indemnity:** the Company will maintain directors' and officers' liability insurance, with cover and terms acceptable to the Investor Director, from a reputable insurer. The Company will indemnify each Investor Director, alternate director and Observer, to the maximum extent permitted by the Act, against all losses arising from his or her position, other than losses arising from his or her own fraud or wilful default.

3.16 **Expenses and remuneration:** the Company will reimburse the reasonable expenses that the Investor Director, the alternate director and the Observer incur in attending meetings and performing their duties. No director nominated by an Investor will be entitled to sitting fees, unless the Investor so requests.

> **Drafting note:** Section 149(3) of the Act requires at least one director to have stayed in India for at least 182 days in the previous calendar year. Each director needs a Director Identification Number. A new director must give consent in Form DIR-2 and a declaration in Form DIR-8, and the Company must file Form DIR-12 within 30 days of appointment. A nominee director owes fiduciary duties to the Company under section 166 of the Act. A nominee cannot be contractually required to vote as the nominator directs where doing so would breach those duties. The provisions of this Agreement that govern how the Founders' directors vote are therefore expressed as obligations of the Founders to "procure", subject to those duties.

## 4. Shareholders' Meetings

4.1 **Notice:** at least 21 clear days' written notice of every general meeting will be given to each Shareholder, unless shorter notice is approved in accordance with section 101 of the Act. Shorter notice requires the consent of the Investor Majority where any Reserved Matter is to be considered.

4.2 **Quorum:** the quorum for a general meeting will be as required by section 103 of the Act, and must include an authorised representative of the Lead Investor, unless the Lead Investor waives this requirement in writing. If a quorum is not present within 30 minutes, the meeting will stand adjourned on the same terms as in clause 3.9, and the same restrictions on Reserved Matters will apply.

4.3 **Voting:** to the extent permitted by the Act, each Investor will vote on an as-if-converted basis. Where the Act does not allow the CCPS to carry voting rights on a matter, the Founders will exercise their votes on that matter in accordance with the written instructions of the Investor Majority, to the extent necessary to give the Investors the voting influence they would have had on an as-if-converted basis.

4.4 **Voting by poll:** if the Investor Director or the Lead Investor so requests, voting on any resolution at a general meeting will be by poll.

> **Drafting note:** Under section 47(2) of the Act, preference shareholders vote only on resolutions that directly affect the rights attached to their preference shares, and on resolutions for winding up or for repayment or reduction of their share capital. They vote on all resolutions if dividend on their shares has remained unpaid for the prescribed period. Clause 4.3 is therefore an inter se arrangement among the Shareholders. Investors who want direct voting rights commonly hold a small number of Equity Shares as well.

## 5. Reserved Matters

5.1 Notwithstanding anything else in this Agreement or the Articles, neither the Company nor any of its subsidiaries will take any action relating to a Reserved Matter, whether at a meeting of the Board, at a committee meeting, by circular resolution or at a general meeting, without the prior written consent of the Investor Majority.

5.2 The consent of the Investor Majority may be given:

(a) by the affirmative vote of the Investor Director at a Board or committee meeting; or

(b) by a written consent of the Investor Majority delivered before the meeting or resolution concerned.

5.3 The Company will send to the Investors a written request for consent to a Reserved Matter, setting out all information reasonably required to evaluate it. If no Investor responds within 10 Business Days of receiving the request, the Company may send a reminder. If no response is received within 5 Business Days of the reminder, consent to the Reserved Matter will be deemed to have been given. Deemed consent does not apply to the matters in paragraphs 1 to 7 of Schedule 2.

5.4 Any action taken in breach of this clause 5 will be void as between the Parties, and the Founders will take all steps necessary to reverse it.

> **Drafting note:** For founders, keep Schedule 2 to fundamental matters, and resist operational vetoes. A wide set of vetoes may mean the Investor has "control" for the purposes of the Competition Act, 2002 and FEMA's downstream-investment rules, and may make the Investor a "promoter" in a later IPO. Monetary thresholds should be tied to the Business Plan.

## 6. Information and Inspection Rights

6.1 **Information:** for so long as an Investor holds at least {{investor_rights_threshold}} of the share capital, the Company will deliver to that Investor:

(a) within {{mis_days}} days of the end of each month, monthly management information. This includes revenue, gross margin, operating expenditure, burn, cash balance, receivables, headcount and the key performance indicators agreed with the Lead Investor;

(b) within {{quarterly_days}} days of the end of each quarter, unaudited quarterly financial statements with a comparison against the Business Plan and an explanation of material variances;

(c) within {{audited_days}} days of the end of each financial year, audited annual financial statements prepared in accordance with the applicable accounting standards, together with the auditor's report and any management letter;

(d) at least {{budget_days}} days before the start of each financial year, the draft Business Plan for that year;

(e) within 7 days of filing, copies of all material filings with the Registrar of Companies, the Reserve Bank of India and any other Governmental Authority;

(f) within 5 Business Days of becoming aware of it, notice of any material litigation, notice from a Governmental Authority, personal data breach, cyber incident reportable to CERT-In, POSH complaint involving a Key Employee, or other event that has had or could reasonably be expected to have a material adverse effect on the Company; and

(g) any other information reasonably requested by the Investor, including information it needs for its own regulatory, tax, valuation or limited-partner reporting.

6.2 **Inspection:** each Investor entitled to information under clause 6.1, and its authorised representatives, may, on 7 Business Days' notice and during business hours:

(a) inspect the properties, books of account and records of the Company and its subsidiaries;

(b) take copies of them; and

(c) discuss the Company's affairs with its directors, Key Employees and statutory auditors.

The Company bears the cost of inspection only where it reveals a material breach.

6.3 **Business Plan:** the Business Plan for each financial year will be prepared by management, reviewed by the Board and approved in accordance with clause 5.

6.4 **Audit:** the statutory auditor of the Company will be a firm of chartered accountants acceptable to the Investor Majority.

## 7. Pre-emptive Rights

7.1 **Right:** if the Company proposes to issue any Securities (other than in an Exempted Issuance), each Investor will have the right, but not the obligation, to subscribe for up to its pro rata share of those Securities. The pro rata share is its shareholding on a Fully Diluted Basis immediately before the issue. The Investor may subscribe at the same price and on the same terms as the Securities are proposed to be issued.

7.2 **Notice:** the Company will give each Investor a written notice (the **"Issuance Notice"**) setting out:

(a) the number and type of Securities proposed to be issued;

(b) the price and other terms of the issue; and

(c) the identity of any proposed third-party subscriber.

Each Investor may exercise its right within 21 days of receiving the Issuance Notice.

7.3 **Over-allotment:** any Securities not taken up by an Investor will be offered to the Investors that have taken up their full entitlement, pro rata to their shareholdings. Only then may the remaining Securities be offered to third parties, within 90 days, at a price not lower than, and on terms not more favourable than, those in the Issuance Notice.

7.4 **Affiliates and mechanics:** an Investor may nominate an Affiliate to subscribe in its place. The Founders and the Company will pass all resolutions required, under section 62(1)(a) or 62(1)(c) read with section 42 of the Act, as the case may be, to give effect to this clause 7.

## 9. Right of First Refusal

9.1 **Notice:** after the Lock-in Period, if a Founder (the **"Selling Founder"**) wishes to Transfer any Founder Securities to a third party, the Selling Founder will first give written notice (the **"Transfer Notice"**) to the Investors and the Company. The Transfer Notice will set out:

(d) all other material terms, and a copy of the binding offer from the proposed transferee.

9.2 **Exercise:** each Investor may, by written notice within 30 days of receiving the Transfer Notice (the **"ROFR Period"**), elect to purchase all or part of its pro rata share of the Offered Securities at the price and on the terms set out in the Transfer Notice. Any Offered Securities not taken up will be re-offered to the Investors that have elected to purchase their full share, for a further 10 days.

9.3 **Completion:** the purchase of Offered Securities by Investors under this clause 9 will be completed within 30 days of the end of the ROFR Period, or any longer period needed to obtain regulatory approvals.

9.4 **Sale to third party:** if the Investors do not elect to purchase all the Offered Securities, the Selling Founder may, subject to clause 11, Transfer the remaining Offered Securities to the proposed transferee named in the Transfer Notice, within 60 days of the end of the ROFR Period. The Transfer must be at a price not lower than, and on terms not more favourable to the transferee than, those in the Transfer Notice, and the transferee must execute a Deed of Adherence. After that period, the procedure in this clause 9 will apply again.

9.5 **No Competitor:** no Founder may Transfer any Founder Securities to a Competitor.

10.4 **No offer:** if the Founders do not make a ROFO Offer within the 60-day period, the Investors may proceed under clause 12 without further restriction under this clause 10.

## 11. Tag-along Right

11.1 **Right:** if, after complying with clause 9, a Selling Founder proposes to Transfer any Founder Securities to a third party, each Investor will have the right (the **"Tag-along Right"**) to require the proposed transferee to purchase from that Investor, at the same price and on the same terms, a number of Securities determined as follows:

11.3 **Completion:** the Selling Founder will not complete the Transfer unless the proposed transferee purchases the tagged Securities at the same time. The proceeds of any Transfer resulting in a Liquidation Event will be allocated in accordance with clause 14.

11.4 **Limited warranties:** no tagging Investor will be required to give any representation, warranty or indemnity other than as to its title to its Securities, its capacity and its authority.

## 12. Drag-along Right

12.1 **Right:** if no Qualified IPO or other exit acceptable to the Investor Majority has occurred within {{drag_years}} years from the Closing Date, and subject to clause 10, the Investor Majority may require all other Shareholders (the **"Dragged Shareholders"**) to Transfer all their Securities to a bona fide third-party purchaser that is not an Affiliate of any Investor (the **"Drag Purchaser"**), on the terms of this clause 12 (the **"Drag-along Right"**).

12.2 **Conditions:** the Drag-along Right may be exercised only if:

(a) the implied equity value of the Company in the transaction is at least {{drag_valuation_floor}}, unless the Founders consent in writing to a lower value;

(b) the consideration is payable in cash or in freely tradeable listed securities;

(c) the Dragged Shareholders receive the same price per Security and the same form of consideration as the dragging Investors, subject only to clause 14; and

(d) the Dragged Shareholders are not required to give any representation, warranty or indemnity other than as to title to their Securities, capacity and authority. Their aggregate liability is limited to the consideration they receive.

12.3 **Drag Notice:** the Investor Majority will give written notice (the **"Drag Notice"**) to the Dragged Shareholders setting out the identity of the Drag Purchaser, the price and other material terms, and the proposed completion date, which must be at least 30 days after the Drag Notice.

Each Dragged Shareholder irrevocably authorises any director of the Company nominated by the Investor Majority to execute all documents needed to Transfer its Securities to the Drag Purchaser. That authority is coupled with an interest and is given to secure the performance of the Dragged Shareholder's obligations.

12.6 **Costs:** the costs of the transaction will be borne by the selling Shareholders pro rata to the consideration they receive.

> **Drafting note:** A drag-along to which a non-resident is a party must comply with the NDI Rules pricing guidelines. Where the Drag Purchaser is a non-resident buying from residents, the price cannot be below fair value. Where a non-resident Investor sells to a resident Drag Purchaser, the price cannot exceed fair value. The authority in clause 12.5 is contractual. Powers of attorney are listed in the First Schedule to the Information Technology Act, 2000 and cannot be executed electronically, so if a separate power of attorney is needed it should be signed on paper and stamped under State law [verify]. Section 202 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 makes an agency coupled with an interest irrevocable, but courts read such clauses narrowly.

## 13. Anti-dilution Protection

13.1 **Adjustment:** if the Company issues any Securities (other than in an Exempted Issuance) at a price per Equity Share, on an as-if-converted basis, lower than the conversion price of the CCPS then in effect (a **"Dilutive Issuance"**), the conversion price of the CCPS will be adjusted on a broad-based weighted average basis as follows:

CP2 = CP1 × (A + B) ÷ (A + C)

where:

(a) **CP2** is the conversion price in effect immediately after the Dilutive Issuance;

(b) **CP1** is the conversion price in effect immediately before the Dilutive Issuance;

(c) **A** is the number of Equity Shares outstanding on a Fully Diluted Basis immediately before the Dilutive Issuance;

(d) **B** is the number of Equity Shares that the aggregate consideration received by the Company for the Dilutive Issuance would purchase at CP1; and

(e) **C** is the number of Equity Shares issued, or issuable on conversion or exercise of the Securities issued, in the Dilutive Issuance.

13.2 **New conversion ratio:** following an adjustment, each CCPS will convert into the number of Equity Shares equal to its issue price divided by CP2.

13.3 **Exempted Issuance** means any issue of:

(a) Equity Shares or options under the ESOP Plan within the ESOP Pool;

(b) Equity Shares on conversion of the CCPS or any other Security outstanding on the Closing Date;

(c) Securities in a bonus issue, split or consolidation made proportionately to all Shareholders;

(d) Securities in a Qualified IPO; and

(e) Securities that the Investor Majority has approved in writing as an Exempted Issuance.

13.4 **Other adjustments:** the conversion ratio of the CCPS will be adjusted proportionately for any split, consolidation, bonus issue, reclassification or similar event.

13.5 **Alternative steps:** if the adjustment under this clause 13 cannot be fully given effect through the conversion ratio (including because of any limit under Applicable Law), the Company and the Founders will take all steps permitted by Applicable Law to give the Investors the economic benefit of the adjustment. Those steps may include a further issue of Securities at the lowest price permitted by law, a bonus issue, or a Transfer of Founder Securities at the lowest price permitted by law.

> **Drafting note:** For a non-resident Investor, the conversion price after adjustment cannot fall below the fair value determined when the CCPS were issued, because the NDI Rules require the conversion formula to be fixed at that time. A further issue under clause 13.5 to a non-resident must be at fair value on the date of issue, and a Transfer by a Founder to a non-resident must be at or above fair value. Clause 13.5 therefore often has limited room to operate for non-residents. Check the tax implications of any issue or Transfer below fair value.

## 14. Liquidation Preference

14.1 **Waterfall:** on the occurrence of a Liquidation Event, the proceeds, assets or consideration available for distribution to the Shareholders (the **"Proceeds"**) will be distributed as follows:

(a) **first**, to each holder of CCPS, in priority to any payment to the holders of Equity Shares, an amount per CCPS equal to the higher of:

(i) one time (1x) its subscription amount, plus any declared but unpaid dividends; and

(ii) the amount that would be distributed per CCPS if all CCPS had converted into Equity Shares immediately before the Liquidation Event;

(b) **second**, the balance of the Proceeds to the holders of Equity Shares, pro rata to their shareholding. A holder of CCPS that has received an amount under clause 14.1(a)(i) will not participate in this distribution.

14.2 **Liquidation Event** means any of the following:

(a) a voluntary or compulsory winding up, liquidation or dissolution of the Company;

(b) a sale, lease, exclusive licence or other disposal of all or substantially all of the assets, undertaking or intellectual property of the Company;

(c) a merger, demerger, amalgamation, scheme of arrangement or acquisition of Securities as a result of which the Shareholders immediately before that transaction hold less than 50% of the voting share capital of the surviving or acquiring entity; and

(d) any transaction or series of related transactions resulting in a change of Control of the Company.

14.3 **Shortfall:** if the Proceeds are insufficient to pay all the amounts in clause 14.1(a), they will be distributed among the holders of CCPS pro rata to the amounts due to them.

14.4 **Implementation:** where a Liquidation Event takes the form of a sale of Securities or a scheme, the Shareholders will give effect to this clause 14 by allocating the consideration among themselves in accordance with it. Each Shareholder will execute any documents and receive or pay any amounts needed for that purpose.

14.5 **Applicable Law:** this clause 14 operates subject to Applicable Law, including the order of distribution on a winding up under the Act and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, and the NDI Rules.

> **Drafting note:** On a statutory winding up, preference shareholders rank ahead of equity only for return of paid-up capital. A preference over the full subscription amount, including premium, is therefore enforced as a contract among Shareholders. For a non-resident Investor, the waterfall must not guarantee a return, so any reallocation from residents to a non-resident on a sale must still comply with the NDI Rules pricing guidelines. If the waterfall cannot be given full effect, the remedy lies in damages among Shareholders.

> **Drafting note:** The NDI Rules permit "optionality clauses" in favour of a non-resident only where there is no assured return, the Securities have been held for a minimum lock-in period of one year, and the exit price does not exceed the fair value at the time of exit [verify current conditions]. A buy-back or put option that guarantees a return (for example, a put at cost plus a coupon) is not permitted. Clauses 15.3 and 20.2(d) are therefore at Fair Market Value. A buy-back must also comply with section 68 of the Act: it must be paid out of free reserves, securities premium or the proceeds of a fresh issue; it is subject to percentage limits and a debt-equity ratio cap; and there must be a gap of at least one year between buy-backs. Separately, check the tax position on buy-backs, which has changed in recent years.

> **Drafting note:** Under section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, a restraint on trade after employment ends is void, unless it falls within the sale-of-goodwill exception, which rarely applies to a Founder. Restraints during employment and while the Founder is a shareholder are generally enforceable. Clause 16.2 is therefore limited to tenure, and clause 16.3 is a narrow non-solicitation. Non-solicitation clauses are enforced only where they are reasonable and protect a legitimate interest. India has no simple "repurchase at cost" mechanism. A buy-back under section 68 of the Act has limits, and a Transfer to a non-resident must be at fair value. Reverse vesting is therefore an obligation to Transfer to a Board nominee "at the lowest price permitted by Applicable Law". If the transferee is a non-resident, that price will be fair value. Check the tax position of both the Founder and the transferee on a Transfer below fair value.

A foreign Investor may ask for a seat outside India, in which case Part I of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 applies only as provided in the proviso to section 2(2).
