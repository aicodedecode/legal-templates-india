# Employment Agreement

**Category:** Startup Core · **Type:** agreement · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

This is a full employment agreement for a managerial, professional or administrative employee of an Indian company, meaning someone who is not a "worker" under the Industrial Relations Code, 2020. It follows the offer letter and puts the detailed terms in contract form. It is drafted around:

- the four Labour Codes;
- the applicable State Shops and Establishments Act;
- the POSH Act, 2013;
- the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023;
- s.17 and s.19 of the Copyright Act, 1957;
- the restraint-of-trade rule in s.27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872.

## When to use it

- When onboarding full-time employees in managerial, professional, technical or administrative roles, in startups, SMEs or larger companies.
- When replacing an informal "appointment letter" with a proper contract before fundraising due diligence. Investors check IP assignment and confidentiality coverage for key employees.
- As a base for senior hires, adding role-specific terms such as relocation, sign-on bonus clawback or a longer notice period.

## When not to use it

- **Employees who are "workers"** under the IR Code (most shop-floor, clerical and lower-paid supervisory roles). Their terms are governed by standing orders and statutory retrenchment rules. Arbitration of their industrial disputes is generally not available.
- **Directors and KMP.** Whole-time directors, managing directors and key managerial personnel need additional Companies Act provisions (appointment approvals and remuneration limits).
- **Consultants, advisors and gig workers.** Use `consultancy-agreement` or `advisor-agreement` instead. Misclassification risks social security liability.
- **Employees based outside India.** Use local-law contracts for them.

## Key provisions

- **Definitions and order of precedence (cl. 1):** Applicable Law prevails, then this Agreement, then the Offer Letter, then the Policies.
- **Duties, place of work, transfer and remote work (cl. 3–4):** transfer to an Affiliate needs the employee's consent.
- **Working hours (cl. 5):** tied to the State Shops and Establishments Act, with overtime only where the law requires it and safeguards for night work by women.
- **Remuneration (cl. 6, Annexure A):** applies the Code on Wages definition of "wages" and its 50% add-back rule, and caps deductions at the statutory limits.
- **Statutory benefits (cl. 7):** PF, ESI, gratuity (including pro-rata gratuity for fixed-term employees), bonus and maternity, all under the Codes.
- **Leave (cl. 9):** State minimums are a floor.
- **Probation (cl. 10):** includes deemed confirmation.
- **Confidentiality (cl. 12):** includes a prohibition on pasting confidential information into unapproved generative AI tools, and carve-outs for regulators and whistleblowing.
- **IP (cl. 13):** recognises s.17 employer ownership and adds an express s.19-compliant present assignment, a moral-rights undertaking and excluded prior IP.
- **Data protection (cl. 14, Annexure C):** employment legitimate use, a notice summary, proportionate monitoring, and the employee's duty to report breaches (for the CERT-In six-hour window).
- **Restrictive covenants (cl. 15):** a non-compete during employment only, plus narrow post-employment non-solicitation and non-disparagement.
- **Termination (cl. 17):** notice or pay in lieu, incapacity, termination for Cause after a hearing, suspension with subsistence allowance, and retirement.
- **Garden leave (cl. 18):** limited to the notice period.
- **Exit (cl. 19):** return of property, and final dues within the statutory time limits.
- **Optional training-cost recovery (cl. 20).**
- **Disputes (cl. 22):** choose arbitration (Option A), which carves out statutory forums and uses joint appointment, or courts (Option B).

## India-specific notes

- **Non-competes.** Post-employment non-competes are void under s.27 (*Superintendence Co. v. Krishan Murgai*, 1980; *Percept D'Mark v. Zaheer Khan*, 2006). Restraints during employment are valid (*Niranjan Shankar Golikari*, 1967). There is no "12 months / 100 km" safe harbour.
- **Garden leave.** Enforceable while the employment continues and salary is paid. A restraint after termination dressed up as garden leave is likely void.
- **Gratuity.** Payable after five years of continuous service. Fixed-term employees get it pro rata, and current guidance indicates after one year [verify]. Check the notified ceilings for PF, ESI, bonus and gratuity before each hiring cycle.
- **Final settlement.** The Code on Wages requires wages to be paid within two working days of separation [verify]. Update 30–45 day F&F cycles accordingly.
- **Arbitrability.** Industrial disputes of "workers" belong to the IR Code machinery and are not arbitrable (*Vidya Drolia*, 2020). Unilateral appointment of an arbitrator is invalid (*Perkins Eastman*, 2019; *CORE*, 2024). Many employers prefer the courts for employment disputes.
- **POSH.** Every employer with 10 or more employees must constitute an Internal Committee. The agreement makes harassment misconduct and requires the employee to cooperate with inquiries. See `posh-policy`.
- **DPDP.** Employment is a legitimate use under s.7, but give a notice anyway. Check which DPDP Rules are in force on the date of signing.
- **Stamp duty.** Employment agreements are chargeable as agreements under State Stamp Acts, usually at a modest fixed rate. E-stamp before or at execution. An unstamped agreement is not void but is inadmissible until the duty and penalty are paid (*In re Interplay*, 2023). This matters when you need an injunction quickly.
- **E-signature.** Valid under the IT Act, 2000. Preserve the audit trail for the s.63 Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 certificate.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Confirm the employee is not a "worker", or adapt the agreement for standing orders and retrenchment rules.
- [ ] Insert the State Shops and Establishments Act, working hours and leave minimums for the work location, and check any IT/ITES exemption notification.
- [ ] Build Annexure A with payroll: the 50% wages test, PF, ESI applicability, and the gratuity provision.
- [ ] Set the probation length, both notice periods and the retirement age.
- [ ] Set the non-solicitation period (6–12 months is usual) and decide whether to keep non-disparagement.
- [ ] Decide whether to include training-cost recovery (cl. 20) and set its cap.
- [ ] Choose the dispute route: Option A (arbitration) or Option B (courts).
- [ ] Complete Annexure B (role) and Annexure D (excluded IP).
- [ ] Issue or update the Policies: Handbook, POSH, IT/AI use, anti-bribery.
- [ ] Stamp the agreement and execute it.
