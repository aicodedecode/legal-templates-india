# Founders' Agreement

**Category:** Startup Core · **Type:** agreement · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

This agreement is signed by the co-founders of an Indian private limited company, and by the company itself. It records each founder's role, time commitment, shareholding and vesting, and what happens to their shares if they leave. It also covers who owns the intellectual property (IP) built before and after incorporation, confidentiality, non-compete and non-solicitation, how decisions are made, how deadlocks are broken, and the basic rules on transferring shares (lock-in, right of first refusal, tag-along and drag-along).

Founder vesting works as **reverse vesting**. Each founder owns all their shares from day one and has full voting and dividend rights. If a founder leaves, the remaining founders or a Board-designated nominee (for example an ESOP trust) can buy back the founder's unvested shares at face value.

## When to use it

- Two or more founders have incorporated (or are about to incorporate) a private limited company and hold its shares.
- Before any outside investor comes in, so that founder vesting and IP ownership are already in place when investors carry out due diligence.
- When a founder joins later and is issued shares in return for an ongoing commitment.

## When not to use it

- **LLPs and partnership firms.** Use an LLP agreement or a partnership deed. Vesting there works through the capital contribution and profit-sharing provisions.
- **After a priced investment round.** The investor shareholders' agreement (see `shareholders-agreement`) normally replaces this document and resets founder vesting.
- **Founders who are non-residents.** Face-value transfers can breach FEMA pricing guidelines, so take advice first.
- **A founder who is really a consultant or an advisor.** Use the `advisor-agreement` or `consultancy-agreement` template instead.

## Key provisions

- **Roles and time commitment (cl. 3–4):** sets out the founders' titles, their areas of authority, a minimum number of working hours, and an exclusivity obligation that applies only while the founder is involved.
- **Vesting (cl. 6):** monthly vesting with a cliff, counted from a vesting commencement date that can be set before the agreement is signed.
- **Good leaver / bad leaver (cl. 7):** a good leaver keeps their vested shares. For a bad leaver there are two options: they keep their vested shares, or the vested shares can be called at the lower of face value and fair market value. In both cases, unvested shares can be called at face value.
- **Transfer mechanics (cl. 8):** call notice, completion, and a contractual authority that lets a director sign the transfer if the leaver will not. Specific performance is available.
- **Acceleration (cl. 9):** choose between single-trigger and double-trigger acceleration on a change of control.
- **IP (cl. 10):** a present assignment of past and future company IP, worldwide and for the full term of protection, satisfying s.19 of the Copyright Act. It also includes a moral-rights undertaking, a schedule of excluded prior IP, and the transfer of domains and accounts.
- **Decision-making (cl. 13 and Schedule 2):** the Board decides by majority, and reserved matters need a founder supermajority.
- **Deadlock (cl. 14):** escalation, then mediation, then either the status quo continues or a buy-sell exit (shotgun or Texas shoot-out) for fundamental matters.
- **Deed of adherence (Schedule 4):** binds incoming shareholders, nominees and heirs.
- **Transfers (cl. 15):** lock-in, right of first refusal (ROFR), tag-along and drag-along.
- **Disputes (cl. 19):** negotiation, then arbitration before a jointly appointed sole arbitrator, with no unilateral appointment. Seat and venue are stated separately.

## India-specific notes

- **Mirror the transfer provisions in the Articles.** In *V.B. Rangaraj v. V.B. Gopalakrishnan* (1992), the Supreme Court held that restrictions on share transfers that are not in the articles do not bind the company. Clause 2 requires the Articles to be amended, by special resolution filed with the RoC, within a fixed number of days. *World Phone India v. WPI Group* (Delhi HC, 2013) applied the same reasoning to shareholders' agreement rights. Do not skip this step.
- **Why not a buyback?** A buyback under s.68 of the Companies Act, 2013 has these constraints:
  - it must be funded from free reserves or securities premium;
  - it is capped by reference to capital and reserves;
  - it needs a declaration of solvency;
  - it is limited to one buyback per 12 months.

  A capital reduction under s.66 needs the Tribunal's approval. Most startups have losses, so a transfer to the remaining founders or a nominee is the practical route.
- **Tax on leaver transfers.** Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, a transfer at face value below fair market value can create deemed income for the buyer and deemed sale consideration for the seller. Get a valuation and tax advice for each transfer.
- **Non-compete.** Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 makes post-exit non-competes void. This template restricts competition only while the founder is involved, and after exit relies on confidentiality and a short non-solicit.
- **Forfeiture.** Taking back vested shares at a discount may be challenged under s.74 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. Prefer Option A in cl. 7.4(b).
- **Demat.** Check whether the company must hold its securities in demat form under the Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules, 2014. Small companies are exempt.
- **Stamp duty.** The agreement is chargeable under the Stamp Act of the state where it is executed, usually as an "agreement" or "agreement not otherwise provided for". Rates vary, and some states charge more where the agreement covers share transfers or creates rights in shares. Stamp the agreement before or at the time of execution, ideally by e-stamping. Share transfers themselves attract stamp duty on transfer of securities, which is collected through the depository for demat shares.
- **Stamping and enforceability.** An unstamped agreement is not void, but it is inadmissible until the duty and penalty are paid. The arbitration clause survives (*In re Interplay*, SC seven-judge bench, 2023).
- **E-signature and evidence.** E-signature is valid under ss.3A and 5 of the IT Act, 2000. Keep the platform's audit trail so you can furnish the certificate required by s.63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023. A separate power of attorney cannot be e-signed.
- **Arbitration.** A clause letting one side alone appoint the arbitrator is invalid (*Perkins Eastman*, 2019; *CORE*, Constitution Bench, 2024). If you choose courts instead, commercial suits generally need pre-institution mediation under s.12A of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015.
- **Specific performance.** Since the 2018 amendment to the Specific Relief Act, 1963, specific performance is the general rule. This supports the leaver transfer mechanics.
- **NCLT.** Oppression and mismanagement claims (ss.241–242) go to the NCLT and may not be arbitrable.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Number of founders (the template has two). Add party blocks, signature blocks and schedule rows as needed.
- [ ] Vesting commencement date, total vesting period and cliff length.
- [ ] Good leaver triggers, especially resignation after the cliff and the notice period.
- [ ] Option A or Option B for a bad leaver's vested shares (cl. 7.4(b)), and for death or incapacity (cl. 7.5).
- [ ] Single-trigger or double-trigger acceleration, and the percentage that accelerates.
- [ ] Deadlock end-point: status quo (Option A) or buy-sell (Option B, then B1 shotgun or B2 Texas shoot-out).
- [ ] Who can act as Nominee (for example an ESOP trust or a new hire).
- [ ] Reserved matters and the spending threshold.
- [ ] Lock-in period, ROFR period and drag-along threshold.
- [ ] Excluded IP for each founder, listed specifically.
- [ ] Founder remuneration approval route.
- [ ] Arbitration seat and venue, and whether to use an institution.
- [ ] Board resolution approving the agreement, and the special resolution amending the Articles.
- [ ] Stamp duty paid in the state of execution.
