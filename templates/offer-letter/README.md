# Offer Letter

**Category:** Startup Core · **Type:** letter · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

This is the offer of employment an Indian company sends to a selected candidate. It covers:

- the role, reporting line and place of work;
- the joining date;
- a component-wise CTC table built with the "wages" definition in the Code on Wages, 2019 in mind;
- probation and notice;
- an indicative (non-binding) ESOP recommendation;
- conditions precedent such as background verification;
- a DPDP-aligned note on the candidate's personal data;
- a list of documents to bring on joining.

The offer letter is deliberately short. The detailed terms live in the **Employment Agreement**, which the candidate signs on or before joining, and in the Employee Handbook and POSH Policy. Section 9.5 provides that the Employment Agreement prevails if the two conflict.

## When to use it

- To make a formal written offer to a full-time employee in India after the interviews are complete.
- For managerial, professional and administrative roles where the employment agreement in this repository will follow.
- When you want the candidate to accept the commercial terms quickly, before the full agreement is signed.

## When not to use it

- **Consultants, freelancers and advisors.** Use `consultancy-agreement` or `advisor-agreement`. An offer letter is evidence of an employment relationship.
- **Fixed-term employees.** Adapt the letter to state the fixed term, and note that fixed-term employees get statutory benefits on a par with permanent employees, including pro-rata gratuity, under the Code on Social Security, 2020.
- **Shop-floor or "worker" roles in establishments covered by standing orders.** Those terms must match the certified or model standing orders.
- **Internships.** Use a separate internship letter. Stipends and Labour Code coverage need separate analysis.

## Key provisions

- **CTC table (s.4):** separates gross fixed pay, employer contributions and variable pay. The wages clause explains that excluded allowances above 50% of total remuneration are added back to wages.
- **Variable pay:** discretionary and tied to policy, so it does not become a guaranteed entitlement.
- **Probation (s.5):** a fixed period, extendable once, with a shorter notice period during probation.
- **ESOP (s.6):** only a recommendation to the Board. Nothing vests or is promised until a grant letter is issued under the ESOP plan.
- **Conditions precedent (s.7):**
  - background verification;
  - documents;
  - right to work;
  - no conflicting obligations;
  - signing the Employment Agreement.
- **Personal data (s.7.3):** relies on the employment legitimate use under the DPDP Act, gives notice, and provides a grievance route.
- **Acceptance (s.10):** a fixed deadline after which the offer lapses.

## India-specific notes

- **Wages definition.** Under the Code on Wages, 2019, allowances excluded from "wages" (HRA, conveyance, employer PF and similar items) are added back to wages to the extent they exceed 50% of total remuneration. This affects PF, gratuity, bonus and leave encashment. Keep basic pay plus DA at no less than half of remuneration, and confirm the treatment with your payroll provider.
- **Labour Codes.** The four Labour Codes are in force. Check the Central and State rules applicable to the work location for the timing of wage payment, registers and leave.
- **Shops and Establishments Act.** The State Act governs working hours, weekly holidays and leave. The Employment Agreement and Handbook carry the State-specific details.
- **ESOPs.** Only the Board, or its nomination and remuneration committee, can grant options under a plan approved by special resolution under s.62(1)(b) of the Companies Act, 2013. Never promise a percentage stake in an offer letter.
- **Background checks and DPDP.** Background checks process personal data. Employment purposes are a legitimate use under the DPDP Act, but verification agencies act as Data Processors and need a contract with you. Do not make Aadhaar mandatory.
- **Stamp duty.** Treatment varies by State. Offer letters are commonly left unstamped. If you may need to rely on the letter in proceedings (for example, to claw back a joining bonus), stamp it: an unstamped instrument is not void, but it is inadmissible until the duty and penalty are paid (*In re Interplay*, SC 2023).
- **E-signature.** Valid under the IT Act, 2000. Keep the signing audit trail, which will support the certificate under s.63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 if the letter is ever produced in court.
- **Restrictive covenants.** Leave non-solicitation and confidentiality to the Employment Agreement. Never include a post-employment non-compete; it is void under s.27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Confirm the role is genuinely managerial or administrative, or adapt the letter for "worker" roles.
- [ ] Build the CTC table with your payroll provider, checking the 50% wages test, PF wage ceiling elections, ESI applicability and the gratuity provision.
- [ ] Decide the variable pay structure and whether a variable pay policy exists.
- [ ] Set the probation length (3–6 months is typical) and both notice periods.
- [ ] Decide whether to include the ESOP section and the indicative number of options.
- [ ] List the background checks you will run, and confirm consent and notice wording.
- [ ] Set the acceptance deadline and the joining date.
- [ ] Attach or send the Employment Agreement, the Employee Handbook and the POSH Policy.
- [ ] Decide whether to stamp the letter.
