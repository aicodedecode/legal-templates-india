# PREVENTION OF SEXUAL HARASSMENT AT THE WORKPLACE POLICY

> **Drafting note:** This policy implements the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 (the **"Act"**) and the rules made under it in 2013 (the **"Rules"**). Every employer with 10 or more workers must constitute an Internal Committee at each office or administrative unit (section 4 of the Act). Statutory protection under the Act is available only to women. This template extends the policy, as a matter of company policy, to complaints by persons of any gender (paragraph 3.3), which is good practice and helps employers meet their non-discrimination obligations, including under the Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019. The statutory remedies, such as the appeal under section 18, the Local Committee, and recovery through the District Officer, may not be available on a complaint by someone who is not a woman. Paragraph 3.3 says so expressly. Have the Board or the head of the organisation adopt this policy. Display it, translate it into the languages your workforce uses, and reflect it in the employee handbook and standing orders.

## 1. Purpose and Commitment

1.1 {{company_name}} (the **"Company"**) is committed to providing a safe, respectful and inclusive workplace in which every person is treated with dignity. Sexual harassment violates the fundamental rights to equality under Articles 14 and 15 of the Constitution of India, to life and to live with dignity under Article 21, and to practise any profession or carry on any occupation, trade or business under Article 19(1)(g), which includes the right to a safe environment free from sexual harassment.

1.2 The Company has zero tolerance for sexual harassment. This policy (the **"Policy"**):

(a) explains what sexual harassment is;

(b) sets out how complaints can be made and how they will be inquired into and redressed;

(c) describes the duties of the Company, its employees and the Internal Committee; and

(d) records the consequences of sexual harassment and of non-compliance.

1.3 This Policy was approved by {{approving_authority}} on {{effective_date}} and takes effect from that date. It is in addition to, and not in substitution of, the Act, the Rules and any other law.

## 2. Legal Framework

2.1 This Policy is framed under the Act and the Rules, and is guided by the principles laid down by the Supreme Court in *Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan* (1997) and later decisions, including *Aureliano Fernandes v. State of Goa* (2023), in which the Supreme Court directed strict compliance with the Act, including the constitution of Internal Committees and wide publicity of their composition.

2.2 Where conduct amounts to an offence under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 or any other law, the Company will, if the Aggrieved Woman so wishes, assist her in initiating action against the perpetrator, in accordance with section 19(j) of the Act.

> **Drafting note:** Sexual harassment offences were formerly in the Indian Penal Code, 1860 (for example, section 354A). They are now in the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, in force from 1 July 2024. Section 11(1) of the Act still refers to the IPC provision. Read it with the corresponding BNS provision [verify the section mapping before citing a number].

## 3. Scope and Applicability

3.1 **Persons:** this Policy applies to all Employees, as defined in paragraph 4.1. It also applies to every other person who is present at, or interacts with, the Workplace. This includes consultants, interns, trainees, apprentices, volunteers, contract and agency workers, vendors, customers, clients and visitors.

3.2 **Places and times:** this Policy applies to conduct at the Workplace (including the Extended Workplace and remote work, as defined in paragraph 4.1), whether during or outside working hours, and to conduct through any physical or digital medium.

3.3 **Gender-neutral application:** the Act protects women. As a matter of Company policy, the Company will also receive and inquire into complaints of sexual harassment made by persons of any gender, including men and transgender persons, using the procedure in this Policy. Where the complainant is not a woman, the Internal Committee will act under this Policy and the Company's service rules. Remedies that exist only under the Act (including the appeal under section 18 of the Act, recourse to the Local Committee, and recovery of compensation through the District Officer) may not be available. In those cases, the Company will provide an internal review by {{appellate_authority}} instead.

3.4 **Third parties:** where the Respondent is not an Employee of the Company (for example, a client's or vendor's employee), the Internal Committee will inquire into the complaint so far as it is able. The Company will take all reasonable steps available to it, which may include asking the third party's employer to take action under its own policy, and will protect the complainant.

## 4. Definitions

4.1 In this Policy:

**"Aggrieved Woman"** means a woman of any age, whether employed or not, who alleges that she has been subjected to any act of sexual harassment by the Respondent at the Workplace. Under paragraph 3.3, it includes any other person who makes a complaint under this Policy. **"Complainant"** means the Aggrieved Woman, or a person who has filed a complaint on her behalf under paragraph 7.4.

**"District Officer"** means the officer notified under section 5 of the Act for the district in which the relevant Workplace is located.

**"Employee"** means a person employed at the Workplace for any work on a regular, temporary, ad hoc or daily-wage basis, either directly or through an agent (including a contractor), with or without the knowledge of the Company, whether for remuneration or not, or working on a voluntary basis or otherwise, whether the terms of employment are express or implied. It includes a co-worker, a contract worker, a probationer, a trainee, an apprentice, an intern and any person called by any other name (section 2(f) of the Act).

**"Employer"** means the Company, and includes any person responsible for the management, supervision and control of the Workplace (section 2(g) of the Act).

**"Internal Committee"** or **"IC"** means the committee constituted by the Company under section 4 of the Act and paragraph 5 of this Policy.

**"Local Committee"** means the committee constituted by the District Officer under section 6 of the Act.

**"Presiding Officer"** means the presiding officer of the Internal Committee.

**"Respondent"** means the person against whom a complaint has been made under this Policy.

**"Sexual Harassment"** has the meaning given in paragraph 4.2.

**"Workplace"** includes (section 2(o) of the Act):

(a) every office, branch, unit, site, warehouse, store and other premises of the Company;

(b) any place visited by an Employee arising out of or during the course of employment, including transportation provided by the Company for undertaking the journey (the **"Extended Workplace"**), such as client premises, hotels, conferences, off-sites, training venues and Company events, whether on or off premises; and

(c) as a matter of Company policy, any place from which an Employee works remotely or from home, and any virtual space used for work, including email, chat and messaging applications, video-conferencing, collaboration tools and social media groups used for work.

**"Working day"** means a day on which the Company's office at the relevant Workplace is open for business.

4.2 **"Sexual Harassment"** includes any one or more of the following unwelcome acts or behaviour, whether directly or by implication (section 2(n) of the Act):

(a) physical contact and advances;

(b) a demand or request for sexual favours;

(c) making sexually coloured remarks;

(d) showing pornography; or

(e) any other unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature.

4.3 The following circumstances, among others, may amount to Sexual Harassment if they occur or are present in relation to, or connected with, any act or behaviour of Sexual Harassment (section 3(2) of the Act):

(a) an implied or explicit promise of preferential treatment in employment;

(b) an implied or explicit threat of detrimental treatment in employment;

(c) an implied or explicit threat about present or future employment status;

(d) interference with work, or creating an intimidating, offensive or hostile work environment; or

(e) humiliating treatment likely to affect health or safety.

4.4 **Illustrations:** without limiting paragraph 4.2, the following may amount to Sexual Harassment:

(a) unwelcome touching, hugging, patting, brushing against, or blocking a person's path;

(b) sexually suggestive jokes, comments about a person's body, appearance or sex life, or persistent unwelcome invitations;

(c) sending sexually explicit messages, images, memes, videos or emojis by any medium, including workplace chat and personal messaging applications used to contact colleagues;

(d) displaying sexually explicit material, including as screen backgrounds on video calls;

(e) stalking, unwelcome following, or repeated contact outside work after it has been made clear that the contact is unwelcome;

(f) creating, sharing or threatening to share intimate images or synthetic (AI-generated or manipulated) sexual content depicting a colleague; and

(g) making employment decisions, assignments, appraisals or opportunities conditional on sexual favours.

4.5 The intent of the Respondent is not relevant. What matters is whether the conduct was unwelcome and of a sexual nature, assessed from the perspective of a reasonable person in the Complainant's position.

## 5. Internal Committee

5.1 **Constitution:** the Company has constituted an Internal Committee at {{ic_locations}}, consisting of (section 4(2) of the Act):

(a) a **Presiding Officer**, who will be a woman employed at a senior level at the Workplace from among the Employees. If no such woman is available, the Presiding Officer will be nominated from other offices or administrative units of the Workplace. If none is available there either, she will be nominated from any other workplace of the Company, or of another department or organisation;

(b) **not less than two members** from among the Employees, preferably committed to the cause of women, or with experience in social work or legal knowledge; and

(c) **one external member** from a non-governmental organisation or association committed to the cause of women, or a person familiar with issues relating to sexual harassment.

5.2 At least one-half of the members of the Internal Committee will be women.

5.3 The current members of the Internal Committee, and their contact details, are set out in Schedule 2. They will be displayed at a conspicuous place at every Workplace and on the Company's intranet.

5.4 **Term:** each member will hold office for a period not exceeding 3 years from the date of nomination, as specified by the Company (section 4(3) of the Act).

5.5 **Fees:** the external member will be paid the fees or allowances specified in the Rules, or such higher amount as the Company decides, for holding the proceedings of the Internal Committee (section 4(4) of the Act and Rule 4).

5.6 **Removal:** a member, including the Presiding Officer, will be removed, and the vacancy filled by fresh nomination in accordance with section 4(5) of the Act, if he or she:

(a) breaches section 16 of the Act (confidentiality);

(b) has been convicted of an offence, or an inquiry into an offence under any law is pending against him or her;

(c) has been found guilty in any disciplinary proceedings, or a disciplinary proceeding is pending against him or her; or

(d) has abused his or her position so as to render continuance in office prejudicial to the public interest.

5.7 **Conflict of interest:** any member who is the Complainant, the Respondent, a witness, or a relative or close associate of any of them, or who has any other conflict, will recuse himself or herself from that case. The Company will nominate a replacement for that case if the quorum would not otherwise be met.

5.8 **Quorum:** at least three members of the Internal Committee, including the Presiding Officer, must be present when conducting an inquiry (Rule 7(7)).

5.9 **Training:** the Company will provide training to the Internal Committee members on the Act, the Rules, the conduct of inquiries and the principles of natural justice.

> **Drafting note:** Rule 4 explains what "a person familiar with issues relating to sexual harassment" means. It includes a person with social-work experience in the field, or a person familiar with labour, service, civil or criminal law [verify the current text and the prescribed fees]. The external member must not be connected with the Company's management. Constitute a separate Internal Committee at each office or administrative unit with 10 or more workers (section 4(1)). Distributed and remote teams may be covered by a common Internal Committee, as long as it is accessible to every employee. Record the constitution in a written order signed by the head of the organisation. A failure to constitute an Internal Committee attracts a penalty under section 26 (see paragraph 19).

## 6. Responsibilities of the Company

6.1 The Company will (section 19 of the Act):

(a) provide a safe working environment at the Workplace, including safety from persons coming into contact with Employees;

(b) display at a conspicuous place in the Workplace the penal consequences of Sexual Harassment, and the order constituting the Internal Committee;

(c) organise workshops and awareness programmes at regular intervals to sensitise Employees to the provisions of the Act, and orientation programmes for the members of the Internal Committee, in the manner prescribed;

(d) provide necessary facilities to the Internal Committee for dealing with complaints and conducting inquiries;

(e) assist in securing the attendance of the Respondent and witnesses before the Internal Committee;

(f) make available to the Internal Committee any information it requires in relation to a complaint;

(g) provide assistance to the Aggrieved Woman if she chooses to file a complaint in relation to the offence under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 or any other law;

(h) cause the initiation of action under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 or any other law against the perpetrator, or, if the Aggrieved Woman so desires, where the perpetrator is not an Employee, at the Workplace where the incident took place;

(i) treat Sexual Harassment as misconduct under its service rules and initiate action for that misconduct; and

(j) monitor the timely submission of reports by the Internal Committee.

6.2 The Company will also:

(a) include this Policy in the induction of every Employee, and obtain each Employee's acknowledgement in the form in Schedule 3;

(b) incorporate this Policy into its employment contracts, standing orders and contracts with vendors and contractors who provide personnel at the Workplace; and

(c) include in its Board's report a statement that it has complied with the provisions relating to the constitution of the Internal Committee, and any further disclosures required by the Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014.

> **Drafting note:** The Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014 require the Board's report to state that the Company has complied with the provisions relating to the constitution of the Internal Committee. Recent amendments require disclosure of the number of complaints received, disposed of and pending beyond 90 days [verify the current Rule 8(5) text]. Under the Labour Codes, check whether the standing orders for your establishment must list sexual harassment as misconduct.

## 7. Making a Complaint

7.1 **Who may complain:** any Aggrieved Woman may make a written complaint of Sexual Harassment to the Internal Committee (section 9(1) of the Act).

7.2 **Time limit:** the complaint must be made within 3 months from the date of the incident, and, in the case of a series of incidents, within 3 months from the date of the last incident. The Internal Committee may, for reasons to be recorded in writing, extend this time limit by a further period not exceeding 3 months, if it is satisfied that circumstances prevented the Aggrieved Woman from filing the complaint within the original period.

7.3 **Assistance:** if the Aggrieved Woman is unable to make a complaint in writing, the Presiding Officer or any member of the Internal Committee will render all reasonable assistance to her in making the complaint in writing.

7.4 **Complaint by another person:** if the Aggrieved Woman is unable to make a complaint because of physical or mental incapacity, death or otherwise, her legal heir or another person specified in Rule 6 may make the complaint (section 9(2) of the Act and Rule 6). These persons include a relative or friend, a co-worker, an officer of the National or State Commission for Women, or any person who has knowledge of the incident, in each case with the written consent of the Aggrieved Woman where the Rules require it.

7.5 **How to complain:** a complaint may be made:

(a) in the form set out in Schedule 1, or in any other written form, submitted to the Presiding Officer or any member of the Internal Committee in person or at {{ic_email}}; or

(b) through the SHe-Box (Sexual Harassment electronic Box) online portal maintained by the Ministry of Women and Child Development, which forwards complaints to the Internal Committee or Local Committee concerned.

7.6 The Complainant should submit 6 copies of the complaint (or one electronic copy, which the Internal Committee will reproduce), together with supporting documents and the names and addresses of witnesses (Rule 7(1)).

7.7 **Acknowledgement:** the Internal Committee will acknowledge receipt of a complaint in writing within 2 working days.

7.8 **Informal concerns:** an Employee who is unsure whether conduct amounts to Sexual Harassment may seek guidance in confidence from any member of the Internal Committee, without making a formal complaint. Members will explain the options available, but will not discourage anyone from making a complaint.

> **Drafting note:** The Internal Committee has no power to entertain a complaint filed after the extended limitation period. Record the reasons for any extension carefully, because extension decisions are often challenged. A complaint made orally, or to a manager or HR, should be promptly referred to the Internal Committee and reduced to writing with the Complainant's assistance.

## 8. Conciliation

8.1 Before starting an inquiry, the Internal Committee may, **at the request of the Aggrieved Woman**, take steps to settle the matter between her and the Respondent through conciliation (section 10 of the Act).

8.2 **No monetary settlement** may be made as a basis of conciliation.

8.3 Where a settlement is arrived at, the Internal Committee will record it and forward it to the Company to take action as specified in the recommendation. The Internal Committee will provide copies of the settlement to the Aggrieved Woman and the Respondent.

8.4 Where a settlement is arrived at, no further inquiry will be conducted. However, if the Aggrieved Woman informs the Internal Committee that a term or condition of the settlement has not been complied with by the Respondent, the Internal Committee will proceed to make an inquiry into the complaint (section 10(4) of the Act).

8.5 The Internal Committee will not suggest, pressure or encourage the Aggrieved Woman to seek conciliation.

## 9. Inquiry

9.1 **Commencement:** if conciliation is not requested, or does not result in a settlement, or the settlement is not complied with, the Internal Committee will inquire into the complaint. Where the Respondent is an Employee, the inquiry will be conducted in accordance with the Company's service rules and this Policy. Where there are no applicable service rules, the inquiry will be conducted in the manner prescribed in the Rules (section 11(1) of the Act).

9.2 **Notice to the Respondent:** the Internal Committee will send one copy of the complaint to the Respondent within 7 working days of receiving it (Rule 7(2)).

9.3 **Reply:** the Respondent will file a reply to the complaint, together with a list of documents and the names and addresses of witnesses, within 10 working days of receiving the copy of the complaint (Rule 7(3)). The Internal Committee will share the reply with the Complainant.

9.4 **Natural justice:** the Internal Committee will conduct the inquiry in accordance with the principles of natural justice. Each party will be given:

(a) a fair opportunity to be heard;

(b) copies of the evidence relied on;

(c) an opportunity to present witnesses and documents; and

(d) an opportunity to put questions to the other party and the witnesses, through the Internal Committee, in writing or otherwise as the Internal Committee directs.

The Internal Committee may arrange for evidence to be given in a way that avoids direct confrontation between the Complainant and the Respondent.

9.5 **No legal practitioners:** the parties are not allowed to bring any legal practitioner to represent them at any stage of the proceedings before the Internal Committee (Rule 7(6)).

9.6 **Non-attendance:** the Internal Committee may terminate the inquiry or give an ex parte decision on the complaint if the Complainant or the Respondent fails, without sufficient cause, to be present at 3 consecutive hearings convened by the Presiding Officer. Before doing so, the Internal Committee must give 15 days' notice in writing to the party concerned (Rule 7(5)).

9.7 **Powers:** for the purpose of the inquiry, the Internal Committee has the same powers as a civil court under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 when trying a suit, in respect of (section 11(3) of the Act):

(a) summoning and enforcing the attendance of any person and examining him or her on oath;

(b) requiring the discovery and production of documents; and

(c) any other matter that may be prescribed.

9.8 **Time limit:** the inquiry will be completed within 90 days of the receipt of the complaint (section 11(4) of the Act).

9.9 **Cooperation:** every Employee will cooperate with the Internal Committee, attend when called, and provide truthful information. Failure to cooperate, interference with witnesses or evidence, and retaliation are misconduct.

9.10 **Video-conferencing:** the Internal Committee may hold hearings through video-conferencing where the parties are in different locations, provided that the confidentiality and fairness of the proceedings are maintained.

## 10. Interim Relief

10.1 During the pendency of an inquiry, on a written request by the Aggrieved Woman, the Internal Committee may recommend that the Company (section 12 of the Act and Rule 8):

(a) transfer the Aggrieved Woman or the Respondent to any other Workplace;

(b) grant leave to the Aggrieved Woman for up to 3 months, in addition to the leave to which she is otherwise entitled;

(c) restrain the Respondent from reporting on the work performance of the Aggrieved Woman or writing her confidential report, and assign that duty to another officer; or

(d) grant such other relief as may be prescribed.

10.2 The Company will implement the recommendation of the Internal Committee and send a report of the implementation to the Internal Committee.

10.3 The Company may also, on its own initiative and without any finding of guilt, take reasonable precautionary measures, such as changing reporting lines or seating, or moving the Respondent to remote work. These measures will not disadvantage the Complainant.

## 11. Inquiry Report and Action

11.1 **Report:** on completion of the inquiry, the Internal Committee will provide a report of its findings to the Company within 10 days of completing the inquiry. The report will also be made available to the parties concerned (section 13(1) of the Act).

11.2 **Allegation not proved:** where the Internal Committee concludes that the allegation against the Respondent has not been proved, it will recommend to the Company that no action is required to be taken in the matter (section 13(2) of the Act).

11.3 **Allegation proved:** where the Internal Committee concludes that the allegation has been proved, it will recommend to the Company (section 13(3) of the Act and Rule 9):

(a) to take action for Sexual Harassment as misconduct in accordance with the Company's service rules or, where none exist, in accordance with the Rules. Depending on the gravity of the conduct, the action may include:

(i) a written apology;

(ii) a warning, reprimand or censure;

(iii) withholding of promotion, pay rise or increments;

(iv) termination of employment;

(v) undergoing a counselling session; or

(vi) carrying out community service; and

(b) to deduct from the salary or wages of the Respondent such sum as it considers appropriate to be paid to the Aggrieved Woman or her legal heirs, determined in accordance with paragraph 12. Where the Company is unable to make the deduction because the Respondent is absent from duty or has ceased to be employed, the Internal Committee may direct the Respondent to pay the sum to the Aggrieved Woman. If the Respondent fails to pay, the Internal Committee may forward the order for recovery of the sum as arrears of land revenue to the District Officer concerned.

11.4 **Implementation:** the Company will act on the recommendation of the Internal Committee within 60 days of its receipt (section 13(4) of the Act), and will inform the Internal Committee and the parties of the action taken.

11.5 **Respondent who is not an Employee:** where the Respondent is not an Employee, the Company will forward the report to the Respondent's employer or principal, and take such action as is available to it. This may include banning access to the Workplace and terminating or suspending the relevant contract.

## 12. Compensation

12.1 In determining the sum to be paid to the Aggrieved Woman, the Internal Committee will have regard to (section 15 of the Act):

(a) the mental trauma, pain, suffering and emotional distress caused to the Aggrieved Woman;

(b) the loss in career opportunity due to the incident;

(c) medical expenses incurred by the Aggrieved Woman for physical or psychiatric treatment;

(d) the income and financial status of the Respondent; and

(e) the feasibility of paying the sum in a lump sum or in instalments.

## 13. False or Malicious Complaints and False Evidence

13.1 Where the Internal Committee concludes that an allegation was made with malicious intent, or that the Complainant (or the person making the complaint) has made the complaint knowing it to be false, or has produced any forged or misleading document, it may recommend that the Company take action against that person in accordance with the Company's service rules or the Rules (section 14(1) of the Act).

13.2 **A mere inability to substantiate a complaint, or to provide adequate proof, will not attract action under paragraph 13.1.** The malicious intent of the Complainant must be established after an inquiry in accordance with the procedure prescribed before any action is recommended (proviso to section 14(1) of the Act).

13.3 Where the Internal Committee concludes that a witness has given false evidence or produced any forged or misleading document during the inquiry, it may recommend that the Company take action against that witness in accordance with the service rules (section 14(2) of the Act).

## 14. Confidentiality

14.1 Notwithstanding anything in the Right to Information Act, 2005, the following will not be published, communicated or made known to the public, the press or the media in any manner (section 16 of the Act):

(a) the contents of the complaint;

(b) the identity and addresses of the Aggrieved Woman, the Respondent and the witnesses;

(c) any information relating to conciliation and inquiry proceedings;

(d) the recommendations of the Internal Committee; and

(e) the action taken by the Company.

14.2 Information may be disseminated about the justice secured to any victim of Sexual Harassment under the Act, without disclosing the name, address, identity or any other particulars calculated to lead to the identification of the Aggrieved Woman or the witnesses.

14.3 Any person entrusted with the duty to handle or deal with a complaint, inquiry, recommendation or action who breaches paragraph 14.1 will be liable to a penalty in accordance with the service rules or, where none exist, as prescribed in the Rules (section 17 of the Act and Rule 12). This may include recovery of a sum of ₹ 5,000 [verify]. The breach will also be treated as misconduct.

14.4 Personal data collected in connection with a complaint will be processed only for the purposes of this Policy, kept securely with access restricted to the Internal Committee and those who need to know, and retained in accordance with the Company's records-retention schedule and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.

> **Drafting note:** The DPDP Rules, 2025 are being brought into force in phases, so check which obligations apply at the time of adoption. Inquiry records are needed for appeals and for the annual report, so set a retention period long enough to cover limitation for appeals and any proceedings (for example, until the later of 3 years after closure and the end of any proceedings) [verify against your retention policy].

## 15. Appeal

15.1 Any person aggrieved by the recommendations of the Internal Committee under section 13(2), 13(3)(i) or (ii), 14(1) or 14(2) of the Act, or by the non-implementation of those recommendations, may prefer an appeal to the court or tribunal in accordance with the service rules applicable to that person. Where no service rules exist, the appeal lies in the manner prescribed in the Rules (section 18 of the Act and Rule 11).

15.2 The appeal must be preferred within 90 days of the recommendations (section 18(2) of the Act).

15.3 For the purposes of this Policy, the appellate authority under the Company's service rules is {{appellate_authority}}.

> **Drafting note:** Rule 11 provides that, where there are no service rules, the appeal lies to the appellate authority notified under the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946. That Act has now been subsumed in the Industrial Relations Code, 2020 [verify how Rule 11 applies under the Code]. Courts have also entertained writ petitions and labour-court challenges. Name a senior appellate authority who has had no involvement in the case.

## 16. Protection against Retaliation

16.1 The Company will not tolerate any retaliation, victimisation or adverse action against a person who has, in good faith, made a complaint, assisted a complainant, given evidence, or served on the Internal Committee. Retaliation includes adverse changes to work, appraisal, pay or opportunities, threats, intimidation, ostracism and online harassment.

16.2 Retaliation is serious misconduct, and complaints of retaliation may be made to the Internal Committee under this Policy.

## 17. Awareness, Training and Annual Report

17.1 **Awareness and training:** the Company will:

(a) conduct mandatory awareness sessions on this Policy for all Employees at induction and at least once a year;

(b) conduct specialised training for managers and for members of the Internal Committee;

(c) display at every Workplace, in English and in the local language, the composition and contact details of the Internal Committee and the penal consequences of Sexual Harassment; and

(d) make this Policy available on its intranet.

17.2 **Annual report:** the Internal Committee will prepare, in each calendar year, an annual report and submit it to the Company and to the District Officer (section 21 of the Act and Rule 14). The report will set out:

(a) the number of complaints of Sexual Harassment received in the year;

(b) the number of complaints disposed of during the year;

(c) the number of cases pending for more than 90 days;

(d) the number of workshops or awareness programmes against Sexual Harassment carried out; and

(e) the nature of action taken by the Company.

17.3 **Disclosure in annual report:** the Company will include in its annual report the number of cases filed and their disposal under the Act or, where it is not required to prepare an annual report, inform the District Officer of the number of such cases (section 22 of the Act).

## 18. Local Committee

18.1 A Local Committee is constituted by the District Officer in every district. It receives complaints of Sexual Harassment from establishments where an Internal Committee has not been constituted because there are fewer than 10 workers, and complaints made against the employer himself or herself (section 6(1) of the Act).

18.2 Where a complaint is against the head of the Company, or against the employer in person, the Complainant may make it to the Local Committee for the district, through the nodal officer for the relevant block, taluka, tehsil, ward or municipality, as notified. The Company will cooperate fully with the Local Committee. The contact details of the District Officer and Local Committee are: {{local_committee_details}}.

> **Drafting note:** If your business has fewer than 10 workers at all its offices, you are not required to constitute an Internal Committee, but you are still bound by the employer duties in section 19. Complaints from your workforce will then go to the Local Committee. Many small companies choose to constitute an Internal Committee voluntarily. Count all workers, including contract and part-time workers, at each office or unit when applying the 10-worker threshold.

## 19. Penalties for Non-compliance

19.1 Under section 26 of the Act, if the Company fails to constitute an Internal Committee, fails to act on the recommendations of the Internal Committee, or contravenes any other provision of the Act or the Rules, it is punishable with a fine that may extend to ₹ 50,000. On a repeat contravention, the Company is liable to twice the punishment that might have been imposed for the first conviction (subject to any higher punishment prescribed for the same offence), and to cancellation of its licence or withdrawal or non-renewal of its registration, or of any approval required for carrying on its business or activity.

> **Drafting note:** The penalty amounts in paragraph 19.1 are taken from section 26 of the Act as originally enacted [verify any amendment]. Separately, an employer that fails to protect employees, or that obstructs complaints, faces civil liability and serious reputational and diligence risk. Investors routinely check POSH compliance during due diligence, so keep the Internal Committee order, training records and annual reports ready.
