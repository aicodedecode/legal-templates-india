# Advisor Agreement

**Category:** Startup Core · **Type:** agreement · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

This agreement engages an external startup advisor: an industry expert, an experienced operator or a technical specialist who gives guidance and introductions for a few hours a month in return for equity-linked or cash compensation. It covers:

- the services and time commitment, and expenses
- compensation with vesting
- assignment of IP in the advisory work product (compliant with s.19 of the Copyright Act)
- confidentiality, including use of AI tools
- conflicts of interest and independent status
- term and termination, liability, and arbitration

Indian law restricts who can receive stock options, so the template offers three compensation structures rather than assuming the US "advisor option grant" model:

- **Alternative A:** an ESOP grant, only where the advisor is eligible.
- **Alternative B:** a direct issue of shares in tranches as they vest.
- **Alternative C:** phantom units paid in cash on a liquidity event, optionally with a cash fee.

## When to use it

- You are engaging a strategic, sector or fundraising advisor who will not be an employee.
- You want a written record of equity promised to an advisor, which investors will expect to see in due diligence.
- You need to make sure that IP arising from the advisor's input belongs to the company.

## When not to use it

- The person will deliver defined work for fees. Use the **Consultancy Agreement**.
- The person will work under your direction as part of the team. Use the **Employment Agreement**: the person may in substance be an employee (see the misclassification note in cl. 10).
- The person is joining the board. Use the director appointment process and board resolutions; this agreement can supplement them, but director duties under the Companies Act, 2013 apply independently.

## Key provisions

- **Services and time commitment** (cl. 2–3, Sch. 1). Advisory capacity only, with no authority to bind the company. Describing the person as an advisor requires their approval.
- **Compensation** (cl. 5, Sch. 2). Choose one alternative:
  - A: ESOPs with at least one year between grant and vesting.
  - B: tranche issues, avoiding US-style reverse vesting, which a company's buy-back restrictions make hard to implement.
  - C: phantom units.
  - In every case, vesting stops when the agreement ends.
- **IP assignment** (cl. 6). A present assignment of advisory work product, for the full term, worldwide, covering all s.19/s.14 rights, with no lapse under s.19(4). Moral rights are handled by a waiver plus an undertaking not to assert them. The advisor's background IP is licensed, not assigned.
- **Confidentiality** (cl. 7). Includes an AI-tool restriction and insider-trading compliance.
- **Conflicts** (cl. 8). Disclosed interests are listed in Sch. 3. There is no competing activity during the term only, with no post-term non-compete. The advisor warrants that no obligations to an employer or university conflict with the agreement.
- **Non-solicit** (cl. 9). Narrow and limited to 12 months.
- **Liability and indemnity** (cl. 12). The advisor's liability is capped, with carve-outs. A narrow indemnity covers third-party claims arising from the advisor's prior obligations.
- **Arbitration** (cl. 13). Seat and venue are separate and appointment is mutual.
- **DPDP notice** (cl. 15). Covers the advisor's personal data.
- **Anti-bribery and POSH compliance** (cl. 16).

## India-specific notes

**ESOP eligibility.** Under s.62(1)(b) of the Companies Act, 2013 and Rule 12 of the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014, stock options can be granted only to "employees" as defined:

- Permanent employees and directors are eligible, as are employees and directors of a holding, subsidiary or associate company.
- Independent directors are excluded.
- Promoters and holders of more than 10% are excluded, subject to a relaxation for eligible DPIIT startups.

An external advisor therefore usually **cannot** receive ESOPs. Do not appoint someone as a director just to get around this.

**Sweat equity.** Sweat equity (s.54) is also limited to directors and employees. It requires a special resolution and a valuation, and is subject to lock-in and caps.

**Direct issue of shares.** A direct issue requires either a preferential allotment (s.62(1)(c)) or a private placement (s.42), with:

- a special resolution
- a registered valuer's report
- an offer letter
- a return of allotment

Investor consent rights under your shareholders' agreement will usually apply as well.

**Non-resident advisors.** FEMA, 1999 and the Non-debt Instruments Rules, 2019 apply. Check:

- pricing guidelines
- sectoral caps
- FC-GPR reporting
- Press Note 3 (2020) approval, if the advisor or their beneficial owner is from a country sharing a land border with India

**Tax.** Equity or phantom payouts received for services are generally taxable as income. Refer to applicable tax laws; the Income-tax Act, 2025 now applies, and the template does not cite section numbers.

**Stamp duty.** Stamp the agreement under the stamp law of the State of execution, usually under the "agreement" article; check the article and rate for your State. E-stamp before or at signing. An unstamped agreement is not void, but it is inadmissible until the duty and penalty are paid (*In re: Interplay*, 2023). Share issues attract their own stamp duty on the share certificates, or on issue in demat form.

**Restraint of trade.** Under s.27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, post-term non-competes are void. The template restricts competing roles only during the term.

**Misclassification.** An "advisor" with fixed hours, a salary-like fee and managerial duties may be treated as an employee under the Labour Codes and the applicable State Shops and Establishments Act.

**E-signature.** E-signing is valid under the Information Technology Act, 2000. Keep the audit trail to support a certificate under s.63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Choose compensation Alternative A, B or C, and delete the others in cl. 5.2 and Sch. 2.
- [ ] Confirm the number of shares, options or units against the fully diluted cap table and any investor consents needed.
- [ ] Set the vesting period. Decide whether to include acceleration on a liquidity event.
- [ ] If Alternative B: plan how to batch tranche allotments, and line up valuations.
- [ ] If the advisor is non-resident: complete the FEMA review before any equity.
- [ ] Set the time commitment, reporting line and expense limit.
- [ ] Complete Sch. 3 (disclosed interests). Obtain the employer's or university's consent if needed.
- [ ] Decide the term and the confidentiality survival period.
- [ ] Choose the seat and venue, and who bears stamp duty.
