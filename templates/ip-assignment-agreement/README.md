# Intellectual Property Assignment Agreement

**Category:** Startup Core · **Type:** agreement · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

A standalone agreement under which a founder, employee, consultant or advisor assigns to the company all IP they have created, or will create, for the business. It also confirms any earlier assignments that may have been defective. It is the standard fix for chain-of-title gaps that investors and acquirers find in due diligence: a founder's code or brand created before incorporation, a contractor who never signed an assignment, or an employment agreement whose IP clause did not satisfy s.19 of the Copyright Act, 1957.

The agreement:

- assigns copyright, patents and the right to apply for them, designs, trade marks and domain names, and know-how
- assigns past rights of action
- schedules excluded prior inventions
- deals with moral rights
- requires delivery of repositories and credentials
- includes a further-assurance covenant and, as a separate wet-ink instrument, a power of attorney

## When to use it

- At or shortly after incorporation, for each founder's pre-incorporation work.
- Before a fundraise or acquisition, as a clean-up across founders, early employees and contractors.
- When an employee or contractor agreement lacks a valid IP assignment.
- When the company needs a document to record with the Patent Office or Trade Marks Registry, or to support a patent application as assignee.

## When not to use it

- As the only document for a new hire or contractor. Include IP terms in the **Employment Agreement** or **Consultancy Agreement**, and use this agreement as a supplement.
- Acquiring IP from a third-party company. Use an asset purchase or technology transfer agreement with full commercial warranties.
- Licensing rather than transferring IP. A licence is a different instrument.

## Key provisions

- **Consideration** (cl. 2). Stated expressly, as required by s.25 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 and s.19(3) of the Copyright Act, 1957. It may be nominal cash, cash plus the continuing engagement, or shares (see the Companies Act notes below).
- **Present assignment of all Assigned IP** (cl. 3):
  - includes goodwill, rights of action for past infringement, priority rights and records
  - copyright particulars under s.19 (works, s.14 rights, full term, worldwide, consideration)
  - future works vest automatically under the proviso to s.18(1)
  - no lapse under s.19(4)
  - patents (s.68 writing requirement; s.7(2) proof of right), designs, trade marks and domain names, and know-how
  - ratification of earlier use
  - a fallback exclusive licence and a bare trust
- **Excluded Inventions** (cl. 4, Sch. 2). A licence applies if they are incorporated into company products.
- **Moral rights** (cl. 5). A waiver, an undertaking not to assert them, and consent.
- **Delivery of records** (cl. 6). Repositories, accounts and credentials, plus disclosure of open-source and AI-tool use.
- **Further assurance and power of attorney** (cl. 7, Sch. 3). The power of attorney is a separate wet-ink instrument, intended to be irrevocable as coupled with an interest (s.202 of the Indian Contract Act).
- **Warranties of authorship and title, and a capped indemnity** (cl. 8–10).
- **Specific performance and injunction** (cl. 11).
- **Effect on other agreements** (cl. 12). Supplements existing agreements, and the assignment is irrevocable even if a dispute arises over the consideration.
- **Arbitration** (cl. 14). Separate seat and venue, with mutual appointment.

## India-specific notes

**Who owns what by default.**
- Section 17 of the Copyright Act makes the employer the first owner of works employees create in the course of employment.
- It does not cover contractors, advisors or founders' pre-incorporation work.
- India has no statutory employer-ownership rule for inventions.
- Express written assignments are therefore essential.

**Formal validity.**
- A copyright assignment must be in writing and must identify the works, the rights, the duration and the territory. Otherwise it defaults to 5 years and India only (s.19(5)–(6)).
- A patent assignment is valid only if it is in writing and duly executed (s.68 of the Patents Act, 1970).

**Registration.** After signing:
- Register title to patents with the Controller (s.69).
- Record design and trade mark assignments (Designs Act, 2000; s.45 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999).
- Check current forms and fees.

**Moral rights.** Section 57 rights survive assignment, and whether they can be waived is unsettled (*Amar Nath Sehgal*, Delhi High Court, 2005).

**Stamp duty.**
- An IP assignment may be charged as a simple agreement or, in some States, as a **conveyance** with ad valorem duty on the consideration or market value. Check the State article, particularly where shares are the consideration.
- The power of attorney carries its own duty.
- E-stamp before or at signing.
- An under-stamped assignment is not void, but it cannot be used in evidence or before registries until the duty and penalty are paid (*In re: Interplay*, 2023).

**E-signature.**
- The agreement itself may be e-signed (ss.3A and 10A of the Information Technology Act, 2000).
- The **power of attorney cannot** be e-signed, because powers of attorney are excluded by the First Schedule to the IT Act. It must be signed in wet ink, stamped and usually notarised.
- For documents to be recorded at IP offices, wet ink before two witnesses is safer.
- Keep e-sign audit trails to support a certificate under s.63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023.

**Companies Act.**
- Shares issued for IP are a non-cash allotment, which needs a registered valuer's report and the related filings.
- Buying IP from a director may be a related party transaction (s.188), requiring disclosure of interest (s.184) and board approval.
- Record both in board resolutions.

**Tax.** Cash consideration may require TDS under applicable tax laws, and the Assignor may be taxed on the consideration. Take advice.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Describe the Assignor's relationship with the company and the date their involvement began.
- [ ] Decide the consideration. If it is shares, plan the valuation, approvals and filings.
- [ ] Complete Sch. 1 specifically: works, repositories, patent and design filings, marks, domains, co-creators, and any third-party interests.
- [ ] Have the Assignor complete and initial Sch. 2 (Excluded Inventions), or write "None".
- [ ] Set the indemnity cap (lower for employees; founders are usually uncapped or capped high).
- [ ] Decide whether a power of attorney is needed. If so, execute Sch. 3 separately in wet ink, stamped and notarised.
- [ ] Choose the seat and venue, and decide who bears stamp duty. Confirm the State article.
- [ ] Obtain board approval, including under s.188 where the Assignor is a director.
- [ ] Plan recordals with IP offices after signing.
