# One-Way Non-Disclosure Agreement

**Category:** Startup Core · **Type:** agreement · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

A confidentiality agreement for when only your company will share confidential information. It is drafted in the discloser's favour. The recipient takes on strict use and disclosure limits, an outright ban on using AI tools with the information, security and audit undertakings, prompt return or destruction, and an indemnity. The discloser makes no warranty about the information and has no obligation to go ahead with any deal. The agreement is governed by Indian law, with arbitration seated in India.

## When to use it

- Sharing specifications, designs or source code with a prospective vendor, developer, manufacturer or system integrator before they bid.
- Briefing a channel partner, distributor or reseller before appointing them.
- Giving a prospective acquirer or strategic partner early access to business information.
- Sharing information with an individual, such as a freelancer or job candidate, during evaluation. Adjust the signature block for an individual.

## When not to use it

- Both sides will share material information. Use the **Mutual NDA**; a recipient with its own secrets will usually insist on reciprocity.
- The recipient will create work product for you. Use the **Consultancy Agreement** or **Master Services Agreement**, which assign IP.
- Pitching to venture investors. Most will not sign, so share only what you are comfortable sharing.
- The recipient is a large platform whose standard terms will govern anyway. Negotiate precedence (see cl. 15.5) rather than signing two inconsistent documents.

## Key provisions

- **Very broad Confidential Information** (cl. 1.1(f)). It includes information observed on site or through system access, and derived materials and AI outputs.
- **Recipient obligations** (cl. 2). Purpose-limited use, a highest-standard duty of care, no reverse engineering, no competing use, no patent filings, a register of Authorised Persons, 24-hour breach notice, and **no residuals clause**.
- **Outright AI ban** (cl. 3). The Company may approve specific tools in writing.
- **Exclusions** (cl. 4). The recipient bears the burden of proving them.
- **Personal data** (cl. 6). Processing on instructions only, security safeguards, no transfer outside India without consent, and breach assistance with notifications to the Data Protection Board of India.
- **Return and destruction within five Business Days, with an officer's certificate** (cl. 7).
- **Feedback assignment** (cl. 8.2). Anything the recipient suggests can be used freely, and copyright in it is assigned worldwide for the full term.
- **Non-solicit and non-circumvention** (cl. 9). Non-circumvention applies during the term only, in line with s.27.
- **Security undertakings and audit** (cl. 11).
- **Specific performance, injunctions and an indemnity, with no cap** (cl. 12).
- **Arbitration** (cl. 13), with a separate seat and venue and pre-institution mediation for court matters.

## India-specific notes

- **Stamp duty.** Stamp the agreement under the State stamp law where it is executed, usually under the residuary "agreement" article; check your State's article and rate. E-stamp before or at signing. An unstamped agreement is not void, but it is inadmissible until the duty and penalty are paid (*In re: Interplay*, Supreme Court, 2023). Delay causes trouble just when you need urgent relief, so stamp first. If the recipient executes in another State, consider whether differential duty will be payable where you would enforce.
- **Indemnity.** Indemnities are recognised under ss.124–125 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. Recipients often push back on an uncapped indemnity. A sensible fallback is to keep it for third-party and regulatory claims and rely on s.73 damages for direct loss.
- **No penalty clause.** Section 74 limits any stipulated sum to reasonable compensation. The template therefore relies on actual loss and injunctions.
- **Restraint of trade.** The non-circumvention clause operates only during the term, and the employee non-solicit is narrow. Post-term restraints risk being void under s.27.
- **Specific performance and injunctions.** Since the 2018 amendment to the Specific Relief Act, 1963, specific performance is the general rule. For an agreement terminable on notice, the injunction route (ss.38 and 42) and s.9 interim relief are the practical remedies.
- **Arbitration.** Do not reserve a unilateral right for the Company to appoint the arbitrator. The Supreme Court has held such clauses invalid (*Perkins Eastman*, 2019; *CORE*, 2024).
- **E-signature and evidence.** E-signing is valid under the Information Technology Act, 2000. Keep the audit trail to support the certificate required under s.63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023.
- **Data protection.** The DPDP Act and Rules commence in phases; check which obligations are in force when you sign. If the recipient is outside India, also check whether any cross-border transfer restrictions apply.
- **Insider trading.** If listed securities are involved, cl. 15.6 imposes compliance with the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Describe the Purpose precisely, since it limits the recipient's use.
- [ ] Set the disclosure period and the survival period.
- [ ] Decide whether to approve any AI tools in advance.
- [ ] Decide whether the indemnity stays uncapped or what fallback you will accept.
- [ ] Decide whether to keep the non-solicit and non-circumvention clauses.
- [ ] Choose the seat, the venue and any arbitral institution.
- [ ] Decide who pays stamp duty (usually the Company as discloser, for speed), and confirm the State article and rate.
- [ ] If the recipient is an individual, adapt the description and the signature block.
