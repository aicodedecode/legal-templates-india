## 1. About this Handbook

1.1 **Purpose.** This Handbook sets out the standards of conduct expected of everyone who works at {{company_name}} (the **"Company"**) and the policies that govern day-to-day employment. It is designed to be short enough to read in one sitting and complete enough to answer most questions.

1.2 **Application.** This Handbook applies to all employees of the Company, whether permanent, fixed-term or on probation, at all locations and when working remotely. Sections 3, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15 also apply, with any necessary changes, to interns, trainees, consultants and contractors who work with the Company, and their contracts will require compliance.

1.3 **Status.** This Handbook does not form part of any employee's contract of employment, except where an employment agreement expressly incorporates a provision of it. The Company may amend, replace or withdraw any part of it by publishing a revised version and notifying employees, but no amendment will reduce any contractual or statutory entitlement.

1.4 **Precedence.** If there is a conflict: (a) Applicable Law (including any certified or model standing orders that apply) prevails; then (b) the employee's employment agreement; then (c) this Handbook; then (d) any team-level guideline.

1.5 **Questions.** Questions about this Handbook should be sent to {{company_email}}. Questions on ethics and compliance should be sent to {{compliance_officer_email}}.

## 2. Definitions

In this Handbook:

(a) **"Applicable Law"** means all laws in force in India from time to time, including the Labour Codes, the {{shops_establishments_act}} and the rules made under them.

(b) **"Company Systems"** means all computers, devices, networks, email, messaging, cloud services, software, accounts and data provided or controlled by the Company.

(c) **"Confidential Information"** has the meaning given in the employee's employment agreement and includes all non-public information about the Company, its customers, suppliers, employees and investors.

(d) **"Employee"** means any person to whom this Handbook applies under Section 1.2.

(e) **"Internal Committee"** means the internal committee constituted under the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 (the **"POSH Act"**).

(f) **"Labour Codes"** means the Code on Wages, 2019, the Industrial Relations Code, 2020, the Code on Social Security, 2020 and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020, and the rules made under them.

(g) **"Manager"** means the person to whom an Employee reports.

## 3. Code of Conduct

3.1 **Core standards.** Every Employee shall:

(a) act honestly, with integrity and in the best interests of the Company;

(b) comply with Applicable Law and the Company's policies;

(c) treat colleagues, customers, suppliers and members of the public with dignity and respect;

(d) protect the Company's assets, Confidential Information and reputation;

(e) avoid conflicts of interest and disclose them when they arise;

(f) keep accurate and complete records, and never falsify any document, timesheet, expense claim or report;

(g) speak up about conduct that appears to breach this Handbook or the law; and

(h) cooperate fully and truthfully with any internal investigation or audit.

3.2 **Workplace behaviour.** The following are prohibited: harassment, bullying, intimidation, discrimination, violence or threats of violence; possession of weapons at the workplace; being under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs while working; gambling on Company premises; and any conduct that endangers the health or safety of others. Alcohol may be served at Company events only with prior approval and in moderation, and Employees remain responsible for their conduct at such events.

3.3 **Dress and presentation.** Employees should dress appropriately for their role and for the people they will meet, respecting cultural and religious diversity.

3.4 **Compliance with law.** Employees shall not engage in any unlawful activity in connection with their work, including insider trading in the securities of any listed company using information obtained through their work, and shall notify the Company promptly if they are arrested for or charged with any offence that may affect their ability to perform their role.

## 4. Equal Opportunity, Diversity and Inclusion

4.1 The Company is an equal opportunity employer. It does not discriminate in recruitment, remuneration, training, promotion, transfer, discipline or termination on the basis of sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, caste, religion, race, place of birth, descent, disability, HIV status, age, marital status, pregnancy, or any other characteristic protected by Applicable Law.

4.2 In particular, the Company: (a) pays equal remuneration for the same work or work of a similar nature, without discrimination on the ground of gender, as required by the Code on Wages, 2019; (b) provides reasonable accommodation to persons with disabilities in accordance with the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016; (c) does not discriminate against transgender persons, in accordance with the Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019; and (d) does not require HIV testing as a condition of employment, in accordance with the Human Immunodeficiency Virus and Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome (Prevention and Control) Act, 2017.

4.3 Any Employee who believes they have been discriminated against may raise a grievance under Section 18. Complaints of sexual harassment are dealt with under Section 15.

> **Drafting note:** The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 and the Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019 require establishments to publish an equal opportunity policy and, in some cases, designate a complaint officer [verify the precise obligations and thresholds for private establishments under the rules]. This Section can serve as that policy if it is published and the officer is named.

5.5 **Night work and women Employees.** Women Employees may work night shifts only with their consent and subject to the safeguards required by Applicable Law, including safe transport and adequate security.

## 7. Remote and Hybrid Work

7.1 **Eligibility.** Employees may work remotely for up to {{remote_days_per_week}} days a week, or as otherwise agreed in writing with their Manager, where the nature of their role permits. Remote working is a flexibility, not an entitlement, and may be varied or withdrawn on reasonable notice.

7.2 **Location.** Employees shall work remotely only from their registered residential address in India, or another location in India approved in advance. Working from outside India requires prior written approval because of tax, immigration, data transfer and permanent establishment implications.

7.3 **Workspace and security.** Employees working remotely shall: (a) maintain a safe and suitable workspace; (b) use only Company-approved devices and secure networks (and a VPN where required); (c) prevent others, including family members, from viewing or hearing Confidential Information; (d) lock devices when unattended; and (e) not print Confidential Information at home unless authorised.

7.4 **Availability.** Employees shall be reachable during core hours and attend scheduled meetings. Working hours and leave rules apply equally to remote work.

7.5 **Equipment and expenses.** The Company will provide the equipment necessary for remote work and reimburse approved expenses in accordance with the expense policy. Equipment remains Company property.

7.6 **Right to disconnect.** Outside working hours, Employees are not expected to respond to non-urgent communications, except where they are on an agreed on-call rotation.

## 8. IT and Acceptable Use

8.1 **Business use.** Company Systems are provided for business purposes. Reasonable, occasional personal use is permitted if it does not interfere with work, consume excessive resources, or breach this Handbook.

8.2 **Prohibited use.** Employees shall not use Company Systems to: (a) access, store or distribute unlawful, obscene, defamatory, discriminatory or harassing material; (b) infringe copyright or other intellectual property, including by installing unlicensed software; (c) circumvent security controls or access systems or data without authorisation; (d) install software, browser extensions or applications that have not been approved; (e) use personal email, messaging or file-sharing accounts to store or transmit Confidential Information; (f) mine cryptocurrency or conduct any personal business; or (g) do anything that is an offence under the Information Technology Act, 2000.

8.3 **Passwords and access.** Employees shall use strong, unique passwords and multi-factor authentication, never share credentials, and report any suspected compromise immediately.

8.4 **Generative AI and automated tools.** Employees may use only those generative artificial intelligence tools approved by the Company, and only in accordance with the Company's AI acceptable use policy. Employees shall not enter Confidential Information, personal data, source code or customer data into any tool that is not approved for that category of data, and remain responsible for reviewing and verifying any AI-generated output used in their work.

8.5 **Monitoring.** The Company may, to the extent proportionate and in accordance with Applicable Law, monitor and log the use of Company Systems for security, compliance, the protection of Confidential Information and the investigation of suspected misconduct. Monitoring will be carried out by authorised personnel only. The Company will not access an Employee's personal accounts or personal devices, except with consent or as required by law.

8.6 **Bring your own device.** Personal devices may be used to access Company email or applications only if enrolled in the Company's device management solution, which may allow the Company to wipe Company data remotely.

8.7 **Security incidents.** Employees shall immediately report to the IT team any actual or suspected security incident, including lost or stolen devices, phishing, malware or unauthorised access. Prompt reporting enables the Company to comply with its obligation under the CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022 to report specified cyber security incidents within six hours of noticing them.

## 9. Data Protection

9.1 **The Company's role.** The Company is a Data Fiduciary under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (the **"DPDP Act"**) in respect of the personal data of its customers, users, Employees and others. Employees who handle personal data in the course of their work act on the Company's behalf and must help the Company meet its obligations.

9.2 **Employee obligations.** Employees shall:

(a) process personal data only for the purpose for which it was collected and as authorised in their role;

(b) access only the personal data they need for their work;

(c) not share personal data with any person, inside or outside the Company, who is not authorised to receive it;

(d) keep personal data accurate and up to date, and dispose of it securely in accordance with the Company's retention schedule;

(e) not transfer personal data to any third party or outside India except through Company-approved processes and contracts; and

(f) report any actual or suspected personal data breach immediately to {{grievance_officer_email}}, so that the Company can notify the Data Protection Board of India and affected Data Principals as required.

9.3 **Employees' own data.** The Company processes Employees' personal data for the purposes of employment, which is a legitimate use under the DPDP Act, as described in the employee privacy notice. Employees may contact the {{grievance_officer_name}} at {{grievance_officer_email}} to exercise their rights as Data Principals or to raise a grievance.

## 10. Confidentiality and Intellectual Property

10.1 Employees shall keep Confidential Information confidential during and after their employment, use it only for the Company's business, and comply with the confidentiality and intellectual property provisions of their employment agreement.

10.2 All work product created by Employees in the course of their employment belongs to the Company. Employees shall not use open-source or third-party material in Company products except in accordance with the Company's open-source policy.

10.4 Employees shall not make public statements, publish papers or speak at events about the Company's technology or business without prior approval under Section 14.

## 11. Conflicts of Interest

11.1 A conflict of interest arises when an Employee's personal interests (or those of a relative or close associate) interfere, or appear to interfere, with their duty to act in the Company's best interests.

11.2 Examples include: (a) holding a financial interest in, or a role with, a competitor, customer or supplier; (b) participating in a decision to hire, promote or evaluate a relative or a person with whom the Employee has a close personal relationship; (c) taking for oneself a business opportunity that belongs to the Company; (d) outside employment or consulting; and (e) accepting gifts or hospitality outside Section 13.

11.3 Employees shall disclose any actual or potential conflict in writing to their Manager and to {{compliance_officer_email}} as soon as they become aware of it, and at the time of joining. The Company will decide how the conflict should be managed, which may include recusal, reassignment or divestment.

11.4 Outside employment, directorships and paid engagements require prior written approval, as set out in the employment agreement.

## 12. Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption

12.1 **Zero tolerance.** The Company prohibits bribery and corruption in all forms, whether involving public servants or private persons, and whether committed directly or through a third party.

12.2 **The law.** Under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, as amended in 2018, it is an offence to give or promise an undue advantage to a public servant to induce or reward the improper performance of a public duty. Where a person associated with a commercial organisation (including an employee, agent or service provider) gives or promises an undue advantage to a public servant to obtain or retain business or an advantage for the organisation, the organisation itself commits an offence under section 9 of that Act, and its directors and officers may also be liable. The only defence available to the organisation is to prove that it had adequate procedures in place, in compliance with the guidelines prescribed under that Act, to prevent such conduct.

12.3 **Prohibited conduct.** Employees shall not, directly or indirectly:

(a) offer, promise, give, request or accept any bribe, kickback or other undue advantage;

(b) make facilitation or "speed" payments to any public servant to expedite routine actions (such as licences, inspections, permits or customs clearance), even if small or customary;

(c) use agents, consultants, distributors or other third parties to do anything that the Employee is prohibited from doing;

(d) make any political contribution on behalf of the Company, except as approved by the Board in accordance with the Companies Act, 2013; or

(e) make any charitable donation or sponsorship on behalf of the Company that is intended to, or could appear to, secure an improper advantage.

12.4 **Third parties.** Before engaging any intermediary that will interact with public servants on the Company's behalf, the engaging team shall carry out risk-based due diligence, obtain a contractual anti-bribery undertaking and record the business rationale for its fees.

12.5 **Extortion and safety.** If an Employee is faced with a demand for payment under threat to their personal safety, they may make the payment to protect themselves, but must report it immediately to {{compliance_officer_email}}.

12.6 **Records.** All payments shall be accurately recorded in the Company's books. No off-book account or false entry shall be created for any purpose.

12.7 **Reporting.** Any request for, or offer of, a bribe, and any suspected breach of this Section, shall be reported immediately to {{compliance_officer_email}} or through the whistleblower channel in Section 17.

> **Drafting note:** The "adequate procedures" defence in s.9 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 depends on guidelines to be prescribed by the Central Government; check whether such guidelines have been notified [verify]. Regardless, a documented policy, training, third-party due diligence, gift registers and a reporting channel are the core of any adequate-procedures defence. If the Company has a group entity in the United Kingdom or the United States, the UK Bribery Act, 2010 or the US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act may also apply.

## 15. Prevention of Sexual Harassment

15.1 The Company has zero tolerance for sexual harassment. ## 17. Whistleblower Policy (Speak Up)

17.1 **Purpose.** The Company encourages Employees, and other persons dealing with the Company, to report genuine concerns about unethical behaviour, actual or suspected fraud, breach of law, breach of this Handbook, financial irregularities, bribery, data misuse or danger to health, safety or the environment (**"Protected Disclosures"**).

17.2 **How to report.** Protected Disclosures may be made in writing to {{whistleblower_email}}, or, where the concern involves senior management or the person who would ordinarily handle the report, directly to {{audit_committee_chair_email}}. Disclosures may be made anonymously, although anonymous reports can be harder to investigate.

17.3 **Investigation.** Each Protected Disclosure will be acknowledged, assessed and, where appropriate, investigated promptly, fairly and confidentially by a person independent of the matter. The whistleblower will be informed of the outcome to the extent appropriate.

17.4 **Protection.** No Employee who makes a Protected Disclosure in good faith will suffer dismissal, demotion, harassment or any other detriment as a result, even if the concern is not substantiated. Retaliation is misconduct. Knowingly making a false disclosure is also misconduct.

17.5 **Confidentiality.** The identity of the whistleblower will be kept confidential, except to the extent required by law or to conduct an effective investigation.

> **Drafting note:** Under s.177(9) of the Companies Act, 2013 and the Companies (Meetings of Board and its Powers) Rules, 2014, a vigil mechanism is mandatory for listed companies and for companies that accept deposits from the public or have borrowed from banks and public financial institutions above a prescribed amount [verify current threshold]; the mechanism must provide for direct access to the chairperson of the Audit Committee (or a director nominated by the Board, where there is no Audit Committee) in appropriate cases. Even where it is not mandatory, a speak-up channel is part of an adequate-procedures defence under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988. Disclose the mechanism on the website and in the Board's report where required.

## 18. Grievance Redressal

18.1 Employees with a grievance relating to their work, working conditions, pay, treatment by colleagues or the application of any policy should first raise it informally with their Manager.

18.2 If the grievance is not resolved within seven days, or if it concerns the Manager, the Employee may submit a written grievance to {{company_email}}. The Company will acknowledge it within three working days, may meet the Employee (who may be accompanied by a colleague), and will communicate a written decision within 30 days.

18.3 The Employee may appeal against the decision in writing within 15 days to a senior leader not previously involved, whose decision will be communicated within a further 30 days.

18.4 Where the Company has a Grievance Redressal Committee constituted under the Industrial Relations Code, 2020, grievances of "workers" shall be dealt with by that Committee in accordance with the Code, and nothing in this Section limits any statutory remedy.

> **Drafting note:** The Industrial Relations Code, 2020 requires every industrial establishment employing 20 or more workers to constitute a Grievance Redressal Committee with equal representation of the employer and workers and proportionate representation of women [verify the threshold, composition and timelines under the Code and the relevant State rules]. Sexual harassment complaints go to the Internal Committee under Section 15, not to the grievance process.

## 19. Disciplinary Procedure

19.1 **Principles.** The Company will deal with suspected misconduct fairly and consistently, in accordance with the principles of natural justice: the Employee will be told the allegations and the evidence against them, will have a reasonable opportunity to respond and to be heard, and the decision will be taken by a person who is unbiased and will be reasoned and proportionate. Where certified or model standing orders apply to the Employee, the procedure in those standing orders prevails.

19.2 **Misconduct.** Misconduct includes, without limitation: breach of this Handbook or any Company policy; dishonesty, fraud or theft; falsification of records; wilful insubordination; habitual absence or lateness; negligence causing loss; harassment, bullying, discrimination or violence; sexual harassment (as found by the Internal Committee); breach of confidentiality, data protection or IT policies; bribery; working under the influence of alcohol or drugs; and conduct that brings the Company into disrepute.

19.3 **Minor matters.** Minor performance or conduct issues will ordinarily be addressed by informal discussion and coaching, followed if necessary by a documented performance improvement plan with clear objectives and a reasonable timeline.

19.4 **Formal procedure.** For serious or repeated misconduct, the following steps will apply:

(a) **Preliminary inquiry.** The Company may carry out a preliminary fact-finding inquiry and, where necessary, suspend the Employee pending inquiry, with subsistence allowance as required by Applicable Law.

(b) **Show-cause notice.** The Employee will be given a written notice setting out the specific allegations and the material relied on, and a reasonable period (ordinarily not less than seven days) to submit a written explanation.

(c) **Inquiry.** If the explanation is not satisfactory, or the allegations are disputed and serious, an inquiry officer who is not involved in the matter will hold an inquiry. The Employee may inspect the documents relied on, cross-examine witnesses, present evidence and witnesses in their defence, and be accompanied by a co-employee.

(d) **Findings.** The inquiry officer will record written findings with reasons, which will be shared with the Employee, who may make a representation.

(e) **Decision.** The disciplinary authority will decide on the action, considering the findings, the Employee's representation, the gravity of the misconduct, the Employee's past record and any mitigating circumstances.

(f) **Appeal.** The Employee may appeal in writing within 15 days of the decision to an appellate authority more senior than the disciplinary authority, whose decision will be final within the Company.

19.5 **Penalties.** Depending on the seriousness of the misconduct, the Company may impose: a written warning; a final written warning; withholding of increment or variable pay; demotion; recovery of loss caused by the Employee (within the limits on deductions in the Code on Wages, 2019); or termination of employment, including termination for Cause.

19.6 **Ex parte proceedings.** If the Employee does not participate in the inquiry despite reasonable notice, it may proceed in their absence, and the reasons will be recorded.

> **Drafting note:** Natural justice in workplace inquiries (notice of charges, disclosure of evidence, opportunity to be heard and to cross-examine, an unbiased inquiry officer and a reasoned decision) is required by standing orders for "workers" and is also the standard courts expect of any employer whose policies promise a fair process. Skipping the show-cause and inquiry steps is the most common reason dismissals are set aside. Sexual harassment findings must come from the Internal Committee under the POSH Act; the disciplinary authority then acts on its recommendations within the statutory time.

## 21. Exit Process

21.1 **Resignation.** An Employee who wishes to resign shall submit their resignation in writing (including by email) to their Manager and to {{company_email}}, and serve the notice period in their employment agreement (generally {{notice_period_days}} days after confirmation). The Company may accept an earlier release or place the Employee on garden leave in accordance with the employment agreement.

21.2 **Handover.** During the notice period, the Employee shall complete a documented handover of their work, files, credentials and pending matters as directed by their Manager.

21.3 **Return of property.** On or before the last working day, the Employee shall return all Company property (including laptops, access cards, documents and data) and delete Company data from personal devices after providing a copy to the Company, and confirm in writing that they have done so.

21.4 **Access.** Access to Company Systems will be withdrawn at the end of the last working day, or earlier at the Company's discretion.

21.5 **Final settlement.** The Company will pay wages due within the time required by the Code on Wages, 2019 and other final dues (including leave encashment, reimbursements and gratuity where applicable) within the time required by Applicable Law, after setting off amounts lawfully recoverable.

21.6 **Documents.** The Company will issue a relieving letter and a service or experience certificate on completion of the exit formalities, and will provide Form 16 and assistance with provident fund transfer or withdrawal.

21.7 **Exit interview.** Departing Employees are invited to give feedback in a confidential exit interview.

21.8 **Continuing obligations.** Confidentiality, intellectual property and non-solicitation obligations continue after exit as set out in the employment agreement. Stock options are dealt with under the Employee Stock Option Plan.

21.9 **Personal data after exit.** The Company will retain a former Employee's personal data only for as long as necessary for legal, tax and record-keeping obligations or the establishment or defence of legal claims, and will then delete it.
