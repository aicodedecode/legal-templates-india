# Employee Handbook

**Category:** HR Policies · **Type:** policy · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

This is a concise but complete employee handbook for an Indian company. It sets out the standards of conduct and the day-to-day employment policies in one document, covering:

- code of conduct and equal opportunity;
- working hours and attendance, and leave (with State Shops and Establishments Act placeholders);
- remote and hybrid work, IT and acceptable use (including generative AI tools), and data protection under the DPDP Act;
- confidentiality and conflicts of interest;
- anti-bribery under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (including the 2018 offence for commercial organisations), and gifts and hospitality;
- social media;
- POSH, a whistleblower (speak-up) channel and grievance redressal;
- a disciplinary procedure built on the principles of natural justice;
- the exit process.

It ends with an employee acknowledgement.

The Handbook is expressly **non-contractual**, so the company can update it without each employee's consent. Contractual terms belong in the employment agreement.

## When to use it

- When a startup reaches its first 10–20 employees and needs written policies. This is also the stage at which the POSH Internal Committee becomes mandatory and investors start asking for policies in due diligence.
- When consolidating scattered policies into a single document that employees acknowledge.
- When preparing an adequate-procedures defence under s.9 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988.

## When not to use it

- **As a substitute for standing orders.** An industrial establishment above the Industrial Relations Code threshold (300 or more workers under the Code [verify]) must adopt certified standing orders, or follow the model standing orders, for its "workers". Those prevail over this Handbook.
- **As the POSH policy itself.** Use `posh-policy` for the full policy, Internal Committee and inquiry procedure. The Handbook summarises it and cross-refers to it.
- **For contractors and gig workers' engagement terms.** Their contracts should require compliance with the relevant sections without making them employees.

## Key provisions

- **Status and precedence (s.1):** the order is law and standing orders, then the employment agreement, then the Handbook, then team guidelines.
- **Equal opportunity (s.4):** equal remuneration (Code on Wages), the RPwD Act, the Transgender Persons Act and the HIV/AIDS Act.
- **Hours and leave (ss.5–6):** a leave table that uses the State Act as a floor. Paternity leave is a policy benefit, not a statutory one.
- **Remote work (s.7):** work only from within India unless approved, security rules, and a right to disconnect.
- **IT and AI (s.8):** generative AI tools are restricted to approved ones, monitoring is proportionate, and incidents must be reported fast enough for the CERT-In six-hour window.
- **Data protection (s.9):** employees' duties when handling personal data, and their own rights as Data Principals.
- **Anti-bribery and gifts (ss.12–13):** zero tolerance, no facilitation payments, third-party due diligence, a gift value cap and a register.
- **POSH (s.15):** the Internal Committee, the three-month time limit for complaints (extendable by three months), confidentiality, anti-retaliation, and coverage of all genders.
- **Speak-up (s.17):** an anonymous channel, with direct access to the Audit Committee chair, consistent with s.177(9) of the Companies Act, 2013.
- **Grievances (s.18):** a two-tier process, with a link to the IR Code Grievance Redressal Committee for "workers".
- **Discipline (s.19):** a show-cause notice, an inquiry with cross-examination, reasoned findings, proportionate penalties and an appeal.
- **Exit (s.21):** handover, return of property, final dues within the Code on Wages timelines, relieving letter, and data retention.

## India-specific notes

- **State law drives leave and hours.** Each State's Shops and Establishments Act, and its holidays law, sets the minimum annual leave, casual and sick leave, carry-forward, compulsory holidays, daily and weekly hours, and night-work rules. Fill in the placeholders for each State, or add a State-wise annexure.
- **Standing orders and committees.**
  - Standing orders are needed above the IR Code worker threshold.
  - A Grievance Redressal Committee is needed in establishments with 20 or more workers.
  - An Internal Committee is needed under the POSH Act where there are 10 or more employees.

  Verify the current thresholds and any State variations.
- **Anti-bribery.** Under s.9 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (as amended in 2018), a commercial organisation is guilty if an associated person bribes a public servant for its benefit. The only defence is adequate procedures. Check whether government guidelines on those procedures have been notified [verify].
- **Vigil mechanism.** This is mandatory for listed companies and for certain borrowing or deposit-taking companies under s.177(9) and the related Rules. It is recommended for everyone else.
- **DPDP.** Employment is a legitimate use, but employees who handle customer data are the front line of compliance. The DPDP Rules are being phased in.
- **Stamp duty.** None. The Handbook is a policy, not an instrument, and the acknowledgement is not stamped.
- **E-acknowledgement.** Collect it through the HR system and preserve the audit trail. Proving it in court needs a certificate under s.63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023.
- **Display obligations.** The Labour Codes, State Acts and the POSH Act require certain notices and abstracts to be displayed at the workplace. A handbook does not satisfy those requirements.
