# DISTRIBUTION AGREEMENT

> **Drafting note:** This Agreement appoints a "buy-sell" distributor: the Distributor buys Products from the Supplier on its own account and resells them in its own name and at its own risk. It is not a commercial agency, in which the agent sells in the principal's name, is paid commission and may bind the principal (ss.182 to 238 Indian Contract Act, 1872). The distinction affects tax, product liability, competition-law treatment and termination. The draft leans modestly towards the Supplier (typically a manufacturer or brand owner) but includes the safeguards a reputable distributor will expect. It is drafted for an Indian Supplier; if the Supplier is foreign, see the drafting note to Clause 22. Delete all drafting notes before signing.

**BETWEEN**

**{{company_name}}**, a company incorporated under the laws of India with corporate identity number {{company_cin}} and its registered office at {{company_address}} (the **"Supplier"**, which expression shall, unless repugnant to the context or meaning thereof, include its successors and permitted assigns) of the ONE PART;

**AND**

**{{counterparty_name}}**, having its registered office or principal place of business at {{counterparty_address}} and GSTIN {{counterparty_gstin}} (the **"Distributor"**, which expression shall, unless repugnant to the context or meaning thereof, include its successors and permitted assigns) of the OTHER PART.

The Supplier and the Distributor are each a **"Party"** and together the **"Parties"**.

**WHEREAS:**

(A) The Supplier manufactures and/or markets the products described in Schedule 1 under the trade marks described in Schedule 2.

(B) The Distributor carries on the business of distributing products of that kind in the Territory, and has represented to the Supplier that it has the infrastructure, personnel, financial standing and market access required to distribute the Products effectively.

(C) Relying on those representations, the Supplier wishes to appoint the Distributor, and the Distributor wishes to be appointed, as a distributor of the Products in the Territory on the terms of this Agreement.

## 1. Definitions and Interpretation

**"Business Day"** means a day other than a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday on which banks are open for general business in the city of the Supplier's registered office.

**"Change of Control"** means, in relation to the Distributor, any person or persons acting in concert (who did not control the Distributor on the Effective Date) acquiring control of the Distributor, where "control" has the meaning given in the definition of Affiliate.

**"Confidential Information"** has the meaning given in Clause 16.1.

**"Contract Year"** means each period of twelve (12) months beginning on the Effective Date or an anniversary of it.

**"Customer"** means any person in the Territory who purchases Products from the Distributor, including sub-distributors, dealers, retailers, institutional buyers and end consumers.

**"Defective Product"** means a Product that does not conform to its Specifications or the warranty in Clause 10, or that is "defective" within the meaning of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.

**"Intellectual Property Rights"** means patents, trade marks, service marks, trade names, trade dress, copyright, rights in designs, rights in get-up, domain names, rights in confidential information (including know-how) and all other intellectual property rights, whether registered or unregistered, anywhere in the world.

## 2. Appointment

2.1 **Appointment.** Subject to the terms of this Agreement, the Supplier appoints the Distributor as its [OPTION A: exclusive / OPTION B: non-exclusive] distributor for the resale of the Products in the Territory, and the Distributor accepts the appointment.

2.2 **Exclusivity (Option A only).** If the appointment is exclusive, then during the term of this Agreement, and subject to Clauses 2.3, 2.4 and 4.4, the Supplier shall not appoint any other distributor for the Products in the Territory, and shall not itself actively sell the Products to Customers in the Territory.

> **Drafting note:** Exclusive distribution is one of the vertical arrangements listed in s.3(4) of the Competition Act, 2002 (together with tie-in arrangements, exclusive supply agreements, refusal to deal and resale price maintenance). Such agreements are void only if they cause or are likely to cause an appreciable adverse effect on competition (**"AAEC"**) in India, assessed against the factors in s.19(3) (for example, entry barriers, foreclosure, benefits to consumers and efficiencies). Exclusive territories are usually defensible where the Parties' market shares are modest and inter-brand competition is strong, but take advice where either Party has a significant market position, where the restriction is combined with resale price controls, or where the Supplier may be dominant (s.4). The Competition (Amendment) Act, 2023 widened s.3(4) to cover agreements between enterprises that are not in a direct vertical relationship, and introduced a settlement and commitment mechanism [verify scope and commencement of the amended provisions].

2.3 **Reserved rights.** Notwithstanding Clause 2.2, the Supplier reserves the right, in the Territory, to: (a) sell Products directly to the key and institutional accounts listed in Schedule 5 (the **"Reserved Accounts"**) and to government and public sector buyers through public procurement (including the Government e-Marketplace); (b) sell Products through its own website and its own official stores on online marketplaces; (c) supply Products to other distributors appointed outside the Territory, who may make passive sales into the Territory; and (d) sell Products for export from India.

2.4 **Sales outside the Territory.** The Distributor shall not actively market or solicit sales of the Products outside the Territory, or establish any branch, warehouse or sub-distributor for the Products outside the Territory, without the Supplier's prior written consent. The Distributor may, however, respond to unsolicited orders from customers outside the Territory (passive sales), and nothing in this Agreement prohibits Customers from reselling Products anywhere.

> **Drafting note:** A complete ban on sales outside the Territory, or on Customers' onward resale, increases competition-law risk, because it partitions markets. The CCI has scrutinised territorial restrictions and online-sales restrictions in distribution systems (including in its 2020 market study on e-commerce, and in enforcement against automobile manufacturers) [verify citations before relying]. The active/passive sales distinction used here follows international practice and is not codified in India; it is a risk-reduction measure, not a safe harbour.

2.5 **Sub-distributors.** The Distributor may appoint sub-distributors, dealers and retailers in the Territory, provided that: (a) it notifies the Supplier of each sub-distributor before the appointment; (b) the Supplier does not object on reasonable grounds (such as the sub-distributor's poor reputation, insolvency, or involvement in counterfeit or grey-market trade) within ten (10) Business Days; and (c) the Distributor remains responsible for compliance by its sub-distributors with the provisions of this Agreement that are relevant to them. The Distributor shall not appoint any agent with authority to bind the Supplier.

2.6 **Changes to Products.** The Supplier may, on not less than sixty (60) days' written notice, discontinue any Product, change its Specifications or packaging, or add new products to Schedule 1 (with the Distributor's consent where the new products are to be subject to the Minimum Purchase Target). The Supplier shall fulfil all accepted Orders for discontinued Products and shall, at the Distributor's request, repurchase unsold stock of discontinued Products in saleable condition at the price paid by the Distributor.

2.7 **Relationship.** The Distributor purchases Products from the Supplier and resells them on its own account, in its own name and at its own risk. The Distributor is an independent contractor and is not the agent, partner, franchisee or employee of the Supplier. The Distributor has no authority to make any contract, representation, warranty or commitment on behalf of the Supplier, and shall not hold itself out as having such authority. The Distributor may describe itself as an "authorised distributor" of the Supplier for the Products in the Territory.

## 3. Distributor's Obligations

3.1 **Promotion.** The Distributor shall use its best efforts to promote, market and sell the Products throughout the Territory and to maximise their sales, and shall conduct its business in a manner that reflects favourably on the Products and the Supplier's reputation.

3.2 **Infrastructure.** The Distributor shall, at its own cost: (a) maintain adequate warehousing (meeting the storage conditions in the Specifications), logistics and sales infrastructure throughout the Territory; (b) employ a sufficient number of trained sales and service personnel; (c) maintain stocks of Products sufficient to meet Customer demand, in the quantities set out in Schedule 3 or as otherwise reasonably required by the Supplier; and (d) maintain the licences, registrations and approvals required under Applicable Law to store and sell the Products in the Territory (including GST registration, trade licences and any product-specific licence).

3.3 **Sales network.** The Distributor shall establish and maintain a network of dealers and retailers covering the Territory, in accordance with the coverage plan agreed with the Supplier each Contract Year.

3.4 **Customer service.** The Distributor shall deal promptly and courteously with Customer enquiries and complaints, forward to the Supplier any warranty claim or complaint relating to the quality or safety of the Products within two (2) Business Days of receipt (and any complaint alleging death, injury or a safety hazard within twenty-four (24) hours), and assist the Supplier in handling warranty claims in accordance with the Supplier's warranty policy.

3.5 **Product integrity.** The Distributor shall not: (a) alter, repackage, relabel, re-sticker, obliterate or remove any packaging, label, declaration, serial number, batch code, maximum retail price or Trade Mark on the Products, except as expressly authorised by the Supplier in writing and permitted by Applicable Law; (b) sell any Product that it knows or suspects to be a Defective Product, counterfeit, expired or damaged; or (c) make any statement or claim about the Products that is inconsistent with the Supplier's labelling, Specifications or marketing materials.

> **Drafting note:** Under the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011, pre-packaged commodities sold at retail must carry prescribed declarations, including the name and address of the manufacturer, packer or importer, the common or generic name, net quantity, month and year of manufacture or pre-packing, the retail sale price as a maximum retail price (MRP) inclusive of all taxes, unit sale price for specified commodities, country of origin for imports, and consumer-care details [verify current list and any recent amendments]. A Distributor that repacks or re-labels Products may itself become a "packer" requiring registration under the Rules [verify]. Selling above the printed MRP is an offence; selling below it is lawful. Some Products (for example, food, cosmetics, drugs, medical devices, electrical and electronic goods) are also subject to FSSAI, CDSCO, BIS or e-waste regulation; add product-specific obligations to Schedule 1.

3.6 **No Competing Products during the term.** During the term of this Agreement, the Distributor shall not, without the Supplier's prior written consent, directly or indirectly manufacture, distribute, sell or promote any Competing Products in the Territory, other than those listed in Schedule 5 as existing lines that the Distributor may continue to carry.

> **Drafting note:** An in-term exclusivity or non-compete obligation on a distributor is generally enforceable, since s.27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 is directed at restraints that operate after the relationship ends; Indian courts have distinguished negative covenants operating during the term of a contract (Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning, Supreme Court, 1967). It is, however, an exclusive supply/single-branding obligation that may be examined under s.3(4) of the Competition Act, 2002. Keep it limited to genuinely competing products, and do not extend it beyond termination. This Agreement deliberately contains no post-termination non-compete.

3.7 **Compliance.** The Distributor shall comply with all Applicable Law in the storage, marketing, promotion, sale and delivery of the Products, including laws relating to consumer protection, advertising, packaging and labelling, e-commerce, data protection, anti-bribery and taxation.

3.8 **Online sales.** The Distributor may sell Products online (including through its own website and through online marketplaces), provided that: (a) it complies with the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 and any guidelines of the Central Consumer Protection Authority, including on the display of mandatory product information, country of origin, seller details, return and refund policies and the prohibition of dark patterns; (b) it uses the Trade Marks and product images only in accordance with Clause 8 and the Supplier's brand guidelines; and (c) its online storefronts meet the reasonable quality standards notified by the Supplier, applied in a non-discriminatory way to all of the Supplier's distributors.

> **Drafting note:** An outright ban on online sales by an authorised distributor may be viewed as a restriction on competition. Requiring qualitative standards for online sales is a lower-risk approach. If the Distributor will operate a marketplace or inventory-based e-commerce entity, check the FDI policy on e-commerce if either Party has foreign investment [verify].

## 4. Minimum Purchase Targets

4.1 **Targets.** The Distributor shall purchase from the Supplier Products with a total invoice value (excluding GST) of not less than the Minimum Purchase Target for each Contract Year. The Parties shall agree in good faith the Minimum Purchase Target for each Contract Year after the first not later than sixty (60) days before the start of that Contract Year, taking into account market conditions and the Distributor's past performance. If the Parties fail to agree, the Minimum Purchase Target for the previous Contract Year, increased by the percentage specified in Schedule 3, shall apply.

4.2 **Review.** The Parties shall review the Distributor's performance against the Minimum Purchase Target at the end of each quarter, and the Distributor shall present a written action plan to address any shortfall.

4.3 **Shortfall.** If, in any Contract Year, the Distributor purchases less than {{target_shortfall_percent}} per cent of the Minimum Purchase Target for that Contract Year, other than by reason of: (a) the Supplier's failure to supply Products ordered in accordance with this Agreement; (b) a Force Majeure Event; or (c) a Recall or regulatory restriction affecting the Products, then the Supplier may, by written notice given within ninety (90) days after the end of that Contract Year, do one or more of the following:

(a) convert the Distributor's appointment from exclusive to non-exclusive (where it is exclusive), with effect from the date of the notice;

(b) remove from the Territory any part of it in which the Distributor has failed to achieve reasonable coverage, as specified in the notice; or

(c) terminate this Agreement on not less than sixty (60) days' written notice.

4.4 **Sole remedies.** The remedies in Clause 4.3 are the Supplier's sole remedies for a failure to meet the Minimum Purchase Target. No damages or other compensation shall be payable by the Distributor for a shortfall.

> **Drafting note:** Treating targets as a condition of exclusivity (with conversion, reduction of territory or termination as the consequences) rather than as a debt obligation avoids an argument that a shortfall payment is a penalty unenforceable beyond reasonable compensation under s.74 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. If the Supplier wants a "take-or-pay" commitment, it must be a genuine pre-estimate of loss (for example, linked to dedicated production capacity) and should be drafted with care.

## 5. Prices and Resale Prices

5.1 **Supply prices.** The Supplier shall sell the Products to the Distributor at the prices in the Price List in force on the date the Supplier accepts the relevant Order, less any discounts set out in Schedule 4. Prices are exclusive of GST, which shall be charged in addition, and [OPTION A: include / OPTION B: exclude] freight and transit insurance to the delivery point under Clause 6.4.

5.2 **Price changes.** The Supplier may amend the Price List by giving the Distributor not less than thirty (30) days' written notice. Amended prices shall not apply to Orders accepted before the effective date of the amendment.

5.3 **Resale prices.** The Distributor is free to determine its own resale prices for the Products, including the price at which it sells to sub-distributors, dealers and retailers. The Supplier may communicate recommended or suggested resale prices, provided that they are clearly non-binding and the Supplier does not, directly or indirectly, enforce them by any means, including by threats, penalties, delay or suspension of supplies, withdrawal of discounts or incentives, or termination.

5.4 **Maximum retail price.** The Distributor and its Customers shall not sell any Product to an end consumer at a price exceeding the maximum retail price printed on the Product as required by the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011. Nothing in this Clause 5.4 restricts the Distributor or any Customer from selling at a price below the maximum retail price.

> **Drafting note:** Resale price maintenance, which the Explanation to s.3(4) of the Competition Act, 2002 defines to include any agreement to sell goods on condition that the prices to be charged on resale shall be the prices stipulated by the seller unless it is clearly stated that prices lower than those may be charged, is one of the listed vertical restraints and has been the subject of CCI enforcement (for example, in relation to discount-control policies of automobile manufacturers; Hyundai Motor India Ltd, CCI, 2019 and Maruti Suzuki India Ltd, CCI, 2021 [verify case references]). Minimum resale prices, "discount control" policies, and monitoring of dealers' discounts backed by penalties are high-risk. Recommending prices or setting a maximum price (including the statutory MRP) is generally lower-risk. Train the Supplier's sales team accordingly; most RPM cases turn on emails and WhatsApp messages, not the contract.

5.5 **Promotional schemes.** The Supplier may offer trade promotions, rebates or schemes from time to time, on written terms that state the eligibility conditions, which shall be applied consistently and shall not be conditioned on the Distributor's resale prices.

## 6. Orders, Delivery, Title and Risk

6.1 **Forecasts.** By the tenth (10th) day of each month, the Distributor shall provide the Supplier with a non-binding rolling forecast of its requirements for each Product for the following three (3) months.

6.2 **Orders.** The Distributor shall place Orders in writing (including through the Supplier's dealer management system or by email), specifying the Products, quantities, requested delivery date and delivery location. No Order shall be binding on the Supplier until accepted by it in writing. The Supplier shall accept or reject each Order within {{order_acceptance_days}} Business Days of receipt, and shall not unreasonably reject an Order that is consistent with the Distributor's forecast and within its credit limit. The Supplier may allocate available Products among its distributors fairly and on objective criteria in the event of a shortage.

6.3 **Terms of sale.** Each accepted Order shall be governed exclusively by this Agreement. Any terms in the Distributor's purchase order or other documents shall not apply.

6.4 **Delivery.** Unless otherwise agreed in an accepted Order, delivery shall be made on the following terms: {{delivery_terms}}. The Supplier shall use reasonable efforts to deliver by the requested delivery date, but delivery dates are estimates and time of delivery shall not be of the essence. Each delivery shall be accompanied by a tax invoice, delivery challan and e-way bill as required by GST law.

6.5 **Inspection.** The Distributor shall inspect each delivery on receipt and notify the Supplier in writing of any shortage, damage or visible defect within {{inspection_days}} days of delivery, with supporting evidence (including photographs and the transporter's acknowledgement where applicable). The Supplier shall, at its option, make good any shortage or replace or credit any damaged or defective Products so notified. Products for which no notice is given within that period shall be deemed accepted, without prejudice to the warranty in Clause 10 for latent defects.

6.6 **Risk.** Risk in the Products shall pass to the Distributor on delivery in accordance with Clause 6.4.

6.7 **Title.** Title to the Products shall pass to the Distributor on [OPTION A: delivery / OPTION B: receipt by the Supplier of payment in full for them]. Until title passes under Option B, the Distributor shall store the Products separately and identify them as the Supplier's property, but may resell them in the ordinary course of business.

> **Drafting note:** A retention-of-title clause (Option B) is permitted under s.25 of the Sale of Goods Act, 1930, but its practical value in India is limited once the goods are resold or mixed, and it may be difficult to enforce against an insolvent Distributor without clear identification of goods. A bank guarantee or security deposit (Clause 7.5) is usually more effective protection.

## 7. Payment and Credit

7.1 **Invoicing.** The Supplier shall invoice the Distributor on dispatch of the Products. Each invoice shall comply with GST law, including e-invoicing where applicable.

7.2 **Payment.** The Distributor shall pay each invoice in full within {{payment_days}} days of the invoice date, by electronic funds transfer to the account designated by the Supplier, without set-off, deduction or counterclaim (other than any tax required to be deducted at source under applicable tax laws, for which the Distributor shall furnish the certificate within the prescribed time).

> **Drafting note:** If the Supplier is a micro or small enterprise registered under the MSMED Act, the Distributor (as buyer) must pay within the agreed period, not exceeding 45 days from acceptance or deemed acceptance of the Products, failing which compound interest at three times the bank rate notified by the Reserve Bank of India is payable under s.16 and the Supplier may approach the Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council. Late payment to an MSE may also affect the Distributor's income-tax deduction for the purchase cost under applicable tax laws [verify the corresponding provision of the Income-tax Act, 2025]. Applicable tax laws may also require tax to be deducted or collected on the purchase or sale of goods above specified thresholds; confirm with a tax adviser which applies and do not hard-code section numbers.

7.3 **Credit limit.** The total amount outstanding from the Distributor at any time (whether or not due) shall not exceed {{credit_limit}}, or such other amount as the Supplier may set by written notice, acting reasonably and having regard to the Distributor's payment record. The Supplier may refuse to accept, or suspend delivery of, Orders that would cause the credit limit to be exceeded.

7.4 **Late payment.** If the Distributor fails to pay any amount when due, the Supplier may, without prejudice to its other rights: (a) charge simple interest on the overdue amount at {{late_interest_rate}} per cent per annum from the due date until payment; (b) suspend deliveries until all overdue amounts are paid; and (c) withdraw any credit and require payment in advance for future Orders. The Parties agree that the interest rate is a genuine pre-estimate of the Supplier's financing cost.

7.5 **Security.** Within thirty (30) days of the Effective Date, the Distributor shall provide the Supplier with [OPTION A: an unconditional and irrevocable bank guarantee from a scheduled commercial bank acceptable to the Supplier / OPTION B: an interest-free refundable security deposit] for {{security_amount}}, valid for the term of this Agreement plus ninety (90) days. The Supplier may invoke the bank guarantee or appropriate the deposit to recover any amount due from the Distributor that remains unpaid for more than thirty (30) days after its due date, after giving seven (7) days' written notice. The Distributor shall restore the security to its full amount within fifteen (15) days of any invocation or appropriation. The Supplier shall return the security (less any amounts properly appropriated) within thirty (30) days after termination and settlement of all amounts due.

## 8. Trade Marks and Intellectual Property

8.1 **Licence.** The Supplier grants to the Distributor, for the term of this Agreement, a non-exclusive, non-transferable, royalty-free licence to use the Trade Marks in the Territory solely to identify, advertise, promote and resell the Products in accordance with this Agreement and the Supplier's brand guidelines. The Distributor may sub-license the Trade Marks to its approved sub-distributors and dealers solely for the same purpose.

8.2 **Quality control.** The Distributor shall use the Trade Marks only in the form and manner approved by the Supplier, and shall submit to the Supplier for prior written approval (not to be unreasonably withheld or delayed, and deemed given if no response is received within ten (10) Business Days) all advertising, promotional and point-of-sale materials, signage, websites, social-media handles and online listings that use the Trade Marks.
