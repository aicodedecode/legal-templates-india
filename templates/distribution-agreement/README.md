# Distribution Agreement

**Category:** Commercial & Enterprise · **Type:** agreement · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

An agreement appointing a "buy-sell" distributor. The distributor buys products from a manufacturer or brand owner (the Supplier) and resells them in a defined Indian territory, in its own name and at its own risk. It covers:

- exclusive or non-exclusive appointment, with rights reserved to the Supplier
- minimum purchase targets
- supply pricing, with the distributor free to set its own resale prices
- orders, credit and security
- a licence of the Supplier's trade marks
- marketing and reporting
- product warranty, product liability and recalls
- termination, sell-off and repurchase of stock

The draft leans modestly towards the Supplier, but builds in the safeguards needed under the Competition Act, 2002, the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and the Legal Metrology rules.

## When to use it

- You are a manufacturer, brand owner or importer appointing a regional or national distributor in India for consumer, industrial or B2B products.
- You want territorial exclusivity tied to performance, with clear rules on resale pricing, online sales, recalls and exit.
- You are a distributor reviewing a supplier's paper, and want a balanced benchmark.

## When not to use it

- **Commission agents or sales representatives** who sell in your name and do not buy stock. Use an agency agreement; agency is governed by ss.182–238 of the Indian Contract Act.
- **Franchises**, where the operator pays fees for a business system and brand. Franchises raise different GST, royalty and trade-mark issues.
- **Marketplace or quick-commerce listings.** These are governed by platform terms and the E-Commerce Rules.
- **Regulated products with special distribution licensing**, such as drugs, medical devices, alcohol, fertilisers or arms. Use this template only as a base, and add the regulatory licensing chain.
- **Buying goods for your own use.** Use the vendor-agreement.

## Key provisions

- **Appointment (cl. 2).**
  - Exclusive or non-exclusive appointment, with the Supplier keeping reserved accounts, its own online channels and the right to sell through GeM.
  - The distributor may not actively sell outside the territory, but passive sales are allowed and customers can resell freely.
  - Sub-distributors require approval, and the relationship is expressly principal-to-principal.
- **Distributor obligations (cl. 3).**
  - Best-efforts promotion, infrastructure, stock and service obligations.
  - Product integrity: no relabelling or MRP tampering.
  - A non-compete that applies only during the term.
  - Online sales are permitted, subject to E-Commerce Rules compliance and non-discriminatory quality standards.
- **Minimum purchase targets (cl. 4).** If the distributor misses its targets, its exclusivity can be removed, its territory reduced or the agreement terminated. These are the sole remedies; there are no shortfall damages.
- **Prices (cl. 5).** The distributor is free to set its resale prices. Recommended prices must be genuinely non-binding. Selling above MRP is prohibited, but selling below MRP is always permitted. Promotional schemes may not be tied to resale prices.
- **Orders, delivery and credit (cl. 6–7).** Orders bind only once accepted, and supplies are allocated fairly in a shortage. The clause covers inspection, risk, optional retention of title, a credit limit and late interest, and requires a bank guarantee or security deposit.
- **Trade marks (cl. 8).** A non-exclusive licence with quality control. The distributor may not register the marks or use them in domain names or social-media handles. Goodwill belongs to the Supplier.
- **Reporting and traceability (cl. 9).** Monthly sales and stock reports, with a warning against exchanging competitively sensitive information. Batch traceability supports recalls, and the Supplier has audit rights.
- **Product warranty, liability and recalls (cl. 10).** The Supplier gives warranties, including legal metrology compliance. Consumer warranties are honoured through the Supplier's service network. The clause acknowledges product liability under Chapter VI of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, sets out a recall procedure (including directions from the CCPA) and allocates recall costs.
- **Indemnities and liability (cl. 12).** Each party indemnifies the other for the risks it controls. The claims procedure lets the Supplier control the regulatory response. A mutual annual cap applies, with carve-outs for product liability, IP, fraud and payment.
- **Termination (cl. 14–15).**
  - Long-notice convenience termination, termination for cause, and further Supplier rights (non-payment, change of control, challenge to the trade marks).
  - An express statement that no goodwill compensation is payable.
  - A sell-off period, a repurchase option and customer transition.
  - No restraint after termination.
- **Disputes (cl. 19–20).** Arbitration with the seat and venue stated separately and no unilateral appointment. MSEFC rights are preserved, and s.12A mediation applies to court suits.
- **Foreign supplier (cl. 22).** Covers the importer of record, FEMA payments and permanent-establishment points.

## India-specific notes

- **Competition Act, 2002.** Exclusive distribution, exclusive supply (single-branding), tie-ins, refusal to deal and resale price maintenance are listed under s.3(4). They are void only if they cause an appreciable adverse effect on competition. The CCI has penalised discount-control and RPM practices in the automobile sector. The real risk usually lies in day-to-day conduct (emails, WhatsApp messages and penalties for discounting), not in the contract itself. The 2023 amendments widened s.3(4) and addressed hub-and-spoke cartels; check their current scope.
- **Consumer Protection Act, 2019.** A distributor can be a "product seller" and directly liable to consumers. The CCPA can order recalls. Consumer rights cannot be excluded; the contract only allocates the cost between the parties.
- **Legal Metrology.** Pre-packaged goods must carry the prescribed declarations, including MRP inclusive of all taxes. Re-labelling or repacking may require registration as a packer. Imported goods need importer details.
- **Section 27, Contract Act.** Exclusivity during the term is generally permissible. A non-compete that applies after termination is void (*Percept D'Mark v. Zaheer Khan*, 2006). Indian law provides no statutory goodwill compensation for distributors, but give reasonable notice of termination.
- **Stamp duty.** The agreement is chargeable under the Stamp Act of the State where it is signed. Rates vary, and some States charge ad valorem duty. Bank guarantees attract separate duty. Stamp at or before signing by e-stamping. Unstamped documents are inadmissible until duty and penalty are paid, but are not void (*In re Interplay*, 2023).
- **GST and tax.** Each sale is a separate supply, so invoices, e-way bills and e-invoicing apply. Applicable tax laws may require tax to be deducted or collected on purchases and sales of goods. Confirm the position without citing section numbers.
- **Evidence.** Orders, approvals and price communications are often electronic. Preserve them, because a certificate under s.63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 may be needed to prove them.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Choose exclusive or non-exclusive appointment, and define the territory precisely (States, districts, PIN codes or channels).
- [ ] List the reserved accounts and any existing competing lines the distributor may keep (Sch. 5).
- [ ] Set the minimum purchase targets, the shortfall threshold and the default annual increase (Sch. 3).
- [ ] Complete the products, trade marks and price list (Sch. 1, 2 and 4), including HSN codes and MRP.
- [ ] Choose the freight option, the title option (delivery, or retention of title) and the security type (bank guarantee or deposit).
- [ ] Set the credit limit, payment days and late-payment interest rate.
- [ ] Set the consumer warranty period and confirm the after-sales service network.
- [ ] Set the term, renewal, convenience notice and sell-off periods.
- [ ] Brief the sales team on resale-price and information-exchange rules.
- [ ] Confirm the arbitration seat, venue and appointment route.
- [ ] Decide who pays stamp duty.
- [ ] Delete cl. 22 if the Supplier is Indian.
