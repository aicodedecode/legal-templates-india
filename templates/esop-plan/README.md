# Employee Stock Option Plan

**Category:** Fundraising & Corporate · **Type:** plan · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

This is a complete Employee Stock Option Plan (ESOP) for an unlisted Indian company that issues new equity shares to employees. It is built around s.62(1)(b) of the Companies Act, 2013 and Rule 12 of the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014. It comes with three schedules:

- **Schedule 1, Grant Letter:** includes a vesting table and the employee's acceptance.
- **Schedule 2, Exercise Notice:** includes payment, demat and tax details.
- **Schedule 3, special resolution:** includes the Rule 12 explanatory-statement disclosure checklist.

The Plan is adopted by the Board and approved by shareholders. It is not signed as a contract, so `parties` is 0. Each Grant Letter is signed by the company and the employee.

## When to use it

- When setting up an option pool for the first time, often before or alongside a seed or Series A round. Investors will usually require a pool of a stated size, sized on a fully diluted basis before the money comes in.
- When replacing an informal "ESOP promise" in offer letters with a compliant plan.
- For DPIIT-recognised startups that want to grant options to founders or significant shareholders within the ten-year exception window.

## When not to use it

- **Listed companies, or companies about to list.** The SEBI (Share Based Employee Benefits and Sweat Equity) Regulations, 2021 apply instead.
- **LLPs.** An LLP cannot issue shares or options. Consider a profit-linked incentive plan.
- **Advisors, consultants and independent directors.** They are not "employees" under Rule 12. Use sweat equity (s.54), phantom stock or a cash-settled stock appreciation right (SAR).
- **Cash-only incentives** with no intention to issue shares. A phantom stock or SAR plan is simpler.

## Key provisions

- **Eligibility (cl. 6):**
  - tracks Rule 12 word for word: permanent employees, directors (excluding independent directors) and group-company employees;
  - excludes promoters and directors holding more than 10%;
  - lifts both exclusions for DPIIT startups for ten years from incorporation.
- **Pool (cl. 4):** lapsed options are recycled. Any increase needs a special resolution.
- **Administration (cl. 5):** the Board or the Nomination and Remuneration Committee (NRC), with conflicted members recused.
- **Vesting (cl. 8):** a mandatory minimum of one year from grant to vesting. The default is four years with a one-year cliff and quarterly vesting after that. Performance conditions are optional.
- **Exercise price (cl. 9):** set freely, but never below face value.
- **Exercise (cl. 10):** exercise windows, allotment within 60 days, and handling of a liquidity event, including cash-out of intrinsic value.
- **Leavers (cl. 13):**
  - unvested options lapse;
  - a good leaver has an exercise window (or, optionally, until a liquidity event);
  - termination for Cause forfeits everything;
  - on death or permanent incapacity, all options vest, as Rule 12 requires.
- **Corporate actions (cl. 14):** adjustments, assumption or substitution, acceleration, cash-out, and conversion to the SEBI regime on listing.
- **Tax (cl. 15):** perquisite tax at exercise, TDS mechanics, and the startup deferral. No section numbers are cited.
- **Non-residents (cl. 17):** FEMA pricing, Press Note 3 approval, and Form ESOP reporting.
- **Records (cl. 19):** Form SH-6 register and Board report disclosures.
- **Disputes (cl. 22):** a jointly appointed sole arbitrator, with seat and venue stated separately.

## India-specific notes

- **Shareholder approval.** Pass a special resolution under s.62(1)(b), with a Rule 12-compliant explanatory statement, and file Form MGT-14. A 2015 MCA exemption allows private companies to use an ordinary resolution under s.62(1)(b) [verify], but most companies and investors still use a special resolution because Rule 12 refers to one.
- **Separate resolutions.** These are needed for grants to employees of a subsidiary or holding company, and for grants to any one employee in a year equal to or above 1% of issued capital.
- **One-year minimum vesting.** This runs from the grant date. Back-dated vesting commencement gives economic credit for earlier service, but nothing can vest within twelve months of grant (except on death or permanent incapacity).
- **DPIIT exception.** Confirm the company's DPIIT recognition and its incorporation date before granting to a founder. Diarise the date on which the ten-year window closes.
- **Tax.** The perquisite equals FMV at exercise minus the exercise price, and is taxed as salary with TDS. Eligible startups (those holding the inter-ministerial board certificate) can defer the tax to the earliest of a fixed period, sale of the shares, or leaving employment. On sale, capital gains apply. Use the Income-tax Act, 2025 framework and get a merchant-banker valuation where required.
- **FEMA.** Non-resident grantees need the NDI Rules checked, and Form ESOP must be filed with the RBI through the AD bank. Employees from land-border countries need government approval.
- **Stamp duty.** The Plan itself is generally not stamped. Shares allotted in demat form attract stamp duty on issue, which is collected through the depository system. Grant letters are commonly unstamped; check your State. Any deed of adherence to a shareholders' agreement is stampable. Remember that an unstamped instrument is inadmissible until the duty is paid (*In re Interplay*, 2023).
- **E-signature.** Valid for grant letters and exercise notices. Retain the audit trails for the certificate required by s.63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023.
- **Labour law.** Options are expressly excluded from wages, so they do not inflate gratuity or PF.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Decide the plan name and year, the Administrator (Board or NRC), and the plan term.
- [ ] Set the pool size and the percentage on a fully diluted basis, aligned with the term sheet or investor requirements.
- [ ] Set the per-employee annual cap.
- [ ] Choose the default vesting schedule (keep the cliff at 12 months or more) and decide whether to use performance conditions.
- [ ] Decide the exercise price policy (face value or FMV) and its tax messaging.
- [ ] Set the leaver exercise window, or allow exercise until a liquidity event.
- [ ] Decide on any lock-in on exercised shares (usually zero, relying on SHA transfer restrictions).
- [ ] Choose the direct route or the trust route (cl. 18).
- [ ] Confirm DPIIT status before any grant to a promoter or a holder of more than 10%.
- [ ] Board resolution, then general meeting, then special resolution, then MGT-14.
- [ ] Set up the SH-6 register, the grant letter workflow and the payroll TDS process.
- [ ] Check the FEMA position for any non-resident employees.
