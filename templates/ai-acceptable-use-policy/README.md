# Generative AI Acceptable Use Policy

**Category:** Privacy & Web · **Type:** policy · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

An internal policy that governs how employees, contractors and other personnel use generative AI tools such as chatbots, coding assistants, image, voice and video generators, meeting note-takers and AI agents. It does not ban AI. It sets out:

- which tools are approved;
- what information may go into each tool;
- how outputs must be reviewed;
- how intellectual property, personal data, labelling, bias, security, procurement and incidents are handled.

The policy is drafted against the Indian legal framework: the IT Act and IT Rules (including the rules on synthetically generated information), the MeitY AI advisories, the DPDP Act, 2023, the Copyright Act, 1957 and the CERT-In Directions.

## When to use it

- Your staff already use ChatGPT-style tools, coding assistants or AI features built into productivity software, whether or not you have sanctioned them.
- You are rolling out enterprise AI licences and need rules on what may be input into them.
- A client, investor or auditor has asked about your AI governance controls.
- You handle client confidential information or personal data at scale (IT services, BPO/KPO, professional services, fintech, health-tech).

## When not to use it

- **AI products you sell to customers.** This policy does not govern AI products you build or offer. Those need product-level AI governance, customer-facing terms (see `terms-of-use`) and, if you are an intermediary, compliance with the IT Rules' synthetic-media duties.
- **Hiring or lending decisions by AI.** A formal AI risk-management framework is needed where AI makes or materially informs decisions such as hiring or lending. Clause 11.2 requires a separate assessment for those uses.

## Key provisions

- **AI Tools Register (cl. 5).** Only approved tools and use cases are allowed. Built-in AI features count as new tools. AI agents are subject to authority limits and logging, because of attribution under s.11 of the IT Act.
- **Information classification matrix (cl. 6).** Different rules for public and enterprise tools. Credentials, Aadhaar, card data and UPSI are never to be input. Stricter client contract restrictions prevail.
- **Human review (cl. 7).** Required before any external, decision-making, production or legal use. Citations must be verified, and AI may not be the sole basis for decisions about people.
- **IP (cl. 8).** The Company owns work product. The policy notes the uncertainty under s.2(d)(vi) of the Copyright Act, requires prompts and edits to be recorded, and addresses third-party rights, open-source licences and vendor indemnities.
- **Data protection (cl. 9).** A lawful ground under the DPDP Act and a contract with the processor under s.8(2) are required. New purposes need review. Children's data, cross-border transfer and meeting note-takers are covered.
- **Labelling and deepfakes (cl. 10).** Labelling and metadata for external synthetic content, and a ban on unconsented depictions of real people.
- **Bias (cl. 11), security including prompt injection and deepfake fraud (cl. 12), vendor procurement checklist (cl. 13).**
- **AI incidents (cl. 14).** A 2-hour internal reporting window so that the DPDP breach timelines and the 6-hour CERT-In timeline can be met. Good-faith reporters are protected from retaliation.
- **Training, monitoring, roles, enforcement and an acknowledgement form (cl. 15–20).**

## India-specific notes

- **No AI statute yet.** India has no AI-specific statute. The relevant sources are:
  - MeitY's advisories of 26 December 2023 and 15 March 2024 (the latter superseding the 1 March 2024 advisory and dropping its requirement of government permission for under-tested models);
  - the amendments to the IT Rules on synthetically generated information (reported to be in force from 20 February 2026);
  - the Government's AI governance guidelines.

  Most of these duties fall on **intermediaries**. The policy adopts them as good practice for every company. Verify the current text before relying on it.
- **DPDP Act.** There is no "legitimate interests" basis. Putting customer personal data into an AI tool for a new purpose may need fresh notice and consent. The AI vendor is a Data Processor and needs a contract (s.8(2)). Children's data carries the s.9 restrictions.
- **Copyright.** India has no text-and-data-mining exception. Litigation over AI training (*ANI v OpenAI*, Delhi High Court) is pending [verify]. Whether AI output is protected by copyright depends on human creative input, so record prompts and edits.
- **Employment law.** To make the policy binding, incorporate it by reference into appointment letters or the employee handbook. For establishments covered by standing orders under the Industrial Relations Code, 2020, align the misconduct and disciplinary provisions with the certified or model standing orders.
- **Monitoring employees.** Monitoring personnel's use of AI tools involves processing their personal data. Rely on the employment legitimate use (s.7(i) DPDP Act) or on consent, and describe the monitoring in your employee privacy notice.
- **Sector rules.** Regulated entities should check sector expectations on model risk, outsourcing and AI use, for example RBI guidance on AI and outsourcing, SEBI circulars on AI/ML use by intermediaries [verify], and NMC/CDSCO rules for clinical AI.
- **Stamp duty.** Not applicable. Employee acknowledgements may be signed electronically under s.3A of the IT Act. Keep the audit trail.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Name the policy owner and set up the AI governance committee.
- [ ] Build the initial AI Tools Register: tools, plans, permitted data classes, use cases, configuration and processing locations.
- [ ] Check client contracts for restrictions on AI use and flag them to engagement leads.
- [ ] Adjust the classification matrix in cl. 6 to your data-classification scheme.
- [ ] Decide which tools may handle personal data, and sign data-processing agreements with those vendors.
- [ ] Set the incident reporting window and connect it to your breach-response plan.
- [ ] Set the training frequency and the policy review interval.
- [ ] Incorporate the policy into employment and contractor terms, and collect acknowledgements.
