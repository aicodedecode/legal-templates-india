# Master Services Agreement

**Category:** Commercial & Enterprise · **Type:** agreement · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

A framework agreement between a provider of technology or IT services and a business customer. The Master Services Agreement (MSA) holds the legal terms: IP, confidentiality, data protection, warranties, indemnities, liability, termination and disputes. Each project or engagement is then signed as a short Statement of Work (SOW) that covers scope, deliverables, fees and timelines. Once the MSA is agreed, new work can start by signing an SOW, without renegotiating the legal terms.

The draft leans towards the provider but stays balanced. It gives a customer the protections a reasonable enterprise procurement team will expect, so it can be used as an opening draft without being rejected outright.

## When to use it

- You are an IT services company, software development agency, systems integrator, managed services provider or technology consultancy, and you expect to do more than one project for the same customer.
- You are a customer and want a single set of legal terms for several projects with one vendor. Check the provider-leaning points listed below before you use it this way.
- The work involves custom deliverables (code, configurations, reports) that the customer will own after paying for them.

## When not to use it

- **Subscription software** where the customer gets access to a hosted platform rather than custom work. Use the saas-subscription-agreement template.
- **Purchase of goods**, or procurement where the customer holds the bargaining power and wants customer-friendly terms. Use the vendor-agreement template.
- **Individual consultants or freelancers.** Use the consultancy-agreement template. It deals with the risk that an individual is treated as an employee, and with individual tax issues.
- **One-off, low-value work**, where a single short agreement is simpler.
- **Regulated outsourcing** (for example, a bank's material outsourcing under RBI directions). The MSA can be used as a base, but it will need the regulator-mandated clauses on audit, inspection by the regulator, business continuity and exit.

## Key provisions

- **SOW mechanism and order of precedence (cl. 2).** Each SOW is a separate contract that incorporates the MSA. The MSA prevails over an SOW unless the SOW expressly identifies the clause it varies. Terms printed on purchase orders and vendor portals are excluded.
- **Change control (cl. 4, Sch. 2).** Changes take effect only through a signed Change Request. The provider gets time and cost relief for customer delays.
- **Acceptance (cl. 6).** Each deliverable has a fixed testing window, and rejection notices must give reasons. A deliverable is deemed accepted when the window lapses or when the customer uses it in production. After three failed attempts the customer has set remedies.
- **Fees, GST and TDS (cl. 7–8).** GST is charged in addition to the fees. TDS is drafted generically to match the Income-tax Act, 2025. The clauses cover late-payment interest, disputed invoices, suspension for non-payment and an MSMED Act override.
- **Personnel and subcontracting (cl. 9).** The provider is responsible for compliance with the Labour Codes. Key personnel are protected, and the customer may ask for removal on reasonable grounds. Subcontracting is permitted, but the customer can object to sub-processors.
- **IP (cl. 10).** The provider keeps its pre-existing IP and tools. Deliverables are assigned on payment, and the assignment wording meets s.19 of the Copyright Act (work, rights, full term, worldwide, no lapse under s.19(4)). The customer gets a licence to any provider tools embedded in the deliverables. Moral rights are waived under s.57.
- **Data protection (cl. 12).** The roles follow the DPDP Act: the customer is the Data Fiduciary and the provider is the Data Processor. A separate DPA is required (data-processing-agreement template), with minimum processor terms applying until it is signed. The clause covers breach notification, CERT-In compliance, cross-border transfer limits and audit.
- **Indemnities (cl. 14).** The provider indemnifies for third-party IP claims, personal injury and property damage, and labour claims by its personnel. The customer indemnifies for claims arising from its own materials. Both are limited to third-party claims.
- **Limitation of liability (cl. 15).** General cap of 12 months' fees. A separate, non-eroding super-cap of a chosen multiple (market range 2x to 5x) of annual contract value for confidentiality, data protection and IP indemnity claims. Fraud, gross negligence, wilful misconduct and fees owed are uncapped. Indirect losses are excluded, and the claim period is aligned with the Limitation Act.
- **Term and termination (cl. 17–18).** Only the customer can terminate an SOW for convenience, and it must pay for work done and committed costs. Either party can terminate for uncured breach or insolvency. The clauses also cover transition assistance and data return or deletion.
- **Non-solicit (cl. 20).** Limited to employees directly involved in the services, with standard exceptions.
- **Disputes and boilerplate (cl. 22–24).** Tiered escalation, then arbitration before a sole arbitrator appointed jointly or under s.11 or by an institution, never unilaterally. Seat and venue are stated separately. Section 12A mediation applies to non-arbitrable court matters. The boilerplate covers email notices, a change-of-control termination right, FEMA and export-control compliance for cross-border deals, e-signature with s.63 BSA evidence, and stamping at execution.
- **Transition assistance (cl. 18.4).** Up to 90 days of paid exit support, including an exit plan, handover of deliverables and runbooks, and continued service.

## India-specific notes

- **Stamp duty.** The MSA is an "agreement" under the stamp law of the state where it is signed, or where it is first brought into that state. Some states charge a fixed amount; others charge an ad valorem rate on the contract value. Check whether each SOW is stamped separately in your state. Pay duty through e-stamping or franking before or at the time of signing. An unstamped or under-stamped agreement cannot be admitted in evidence until duty and penalty are paid. In 2023 a seven-judge bench of the Supreme Court held that an unstamped arbitration agreement is not void, and that stamping is a curable defect. Do not rely on that ruling: stamp the agreement properly.
- **E-signature.** Electronic signatures and Aadhaar e-sign are valid under the Information Technology Act, 2000. This agreement is not a document listed in the Act's First Schedule. When you sign electronically, pay stamp duty through e-stamping in the state concerned.
- **MSMED Act.** If the provider is a registered micro or small enterprise, the customer must pay within 45 days of acceptance, and statutory compound interest applies to late payment. The provider can also bring a claim before the Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council. Cl. 8.5 reflects this.
- **Copyright assignment.** Without the s.19 particulars, an assignment of copyright may be limited to five years and to India. The template states the work, the rights, the full term of protection and worldwide territory.
- **Arbitrator appointment.** Clauses that let one party appoint the sole arbitrator, or choose from a panel it curated itself, are invalid. See *Perkins Eastman* (2019) and *CORE* (Constitution Bench, 2024). Cl. 22.2(b) excludes them.
- **Electronic evidence.** To prove an e-signed agreement, you may need a certificate under s.63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023. Keep the platform's audit trail.
- **MSE tax point.** If a buyer pays a micro or small enterprise late, it may lose its tax deduction for that amount until it actually pays, under applicable tax laws. Confirm the provision in the Income-tax Act, 2025.
- **Non-solicitation and s.27.** Restraints that operate after the agreement ends are interpreted narrowly. Keep the non-solicit period short.
- **Limitation clauses.** Under s.28 of the Indian Contract Act, a clause that shortens the statutory limitation period is void. Do not cut the three-year claim period.
- **DPDP Act.** Obligations under the DPDP Act and the DPDP Rules, 2025 are commencing in phases. Check which obligations are in force when you sign. Regulated customers (banks, NBFCs, insurers, listed entities) will add their sector's outsourcing and localisation requirements.
- **Labour Codes.** If provider personnel work on the customer's premises, the customer may be treated as a principal employer. Expect requests for evidence of compliance.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Confirm the legal names, CIN/LLPIN and registered addresses of both parties.
- [ ] Decide the payment period, late-interest rate, and whether the provider is an MSE (if so, obtain its Udyam certificate).
- [ ] Set the acceptance period, and decide whether production use counts as deemed acceptance.
- [ ] Decide whether deliverables are assigned on payment or on creation, and list any deliverables that will be licensed instead.
- [ ] Choose the super-cap multiple (2x to 5x annual contract value), and match it to your cyber and PI insurance.
- [ ] Set the insurance limits, or delete policies you do not hold.
- [ ] Set the initial term, convenience notice period, cure period and non-solicit period.
- [ ] Choose the arbitral seat and venue, and the appointment route (court or an institution). Use the same city for court jurisdiction.
- [ ] For cross-border deals, confirm the FEMA documentation, withholding tax under the DTAA, and any export-control classification.
- [ ] Decide who pays stamp duty, and check the rate in the state where you will sign.
- [ ] Sign a DPA before any personal data is shared.
- [ ] Fill in the governance contacts in Schedule 3.
- [ ] Prepare the first SOW using the statement-of-work template.
