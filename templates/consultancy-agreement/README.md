# Consultancy Agreement

**Category:** Startup Core · **Type:** agreement · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

A client-friendly agreement for engaging an independent consultant, freelancer or consulting firm in India. It covers:

- services defined in a schedule, with change control and acceptance testing
- fees, GST and TDS, and invoicing
- the MSMED Act 45-day payment rule
- a full IP assignment that complies with s.19 of the Copyright Act, 1957
- moral rights and background-IP licensing
- open-source and AI-use controls
- confidentiality and DPDP processor obligations
- independent-contractor safeguards against misclassification under the Labour Codes
- in-term restrictions and narrow post-term non-solicits
- an indemnity, a liability cap with carve-outs, termination, and arbitration

## When to use it

- Engaging a developer, designer, marketer, data scientist or other specialist to deliver defined work.
- A short or medium-term engagement with one primary scope. Add more scope through Statements of Work.
- Any engagement where the company must own the output, which is the case for all contractors, because s.17 of the Copyright Act does not give the company ownership of a contractor's work.

## When not to use it

- The individual will work fixed hours under your supervision as part of the team. Use the **Employment Agreement**: labels do not change status.
- A large vendor relationship with multiple projects and SLAs. Use the **Master Services Agreement** with **Statements of Work** and a **Service Level Agreement**.
- Pure advisory roles paid in equity. Use the **Advisor Agreement**.
- Supply of manpower to work at your establishment. Contract-labour rules under the OSH Code may apply, so take specific advice.

## Key provisions

- **Services, change control, acceptance** (cl. 2–4, Sch. 1). Time is of the essence only for named critical milestones. Deliverables can be rejected twice before the client has termination and refund options.
- **Fees and payment** (cl. 5, Sch. 2):
  - GST-compliant invoices
  - payment within a set number of days, with disputed amounts handled separately
  - Udyam disclosure, and payment within 45 days for micro and small enterprises
  - late-payment interest for consultants that are not MSEs
  - set-off
- **Taxes** (cl. 6). GST is charged on top of the fees, and the consultant must protect the client's input tax credit. TDS is deducted under "applicable tax laws", with no section numbers cited because of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Lower-deduction certificates are honoured.
- **IP** (cl. 7):
  - a present assignment of all work product, identifying the works and the s.14 rights, for the full term and worldwide, with consideration stated
  - no lapse under s.19(4)
  - s.68 Patents Act recordal
  - moral-rights waiver and undertaking not to assert
  - background-IP licence
  - open-source approval, with copyleft blocked
  - no embedded power of attorney
- **AI use** (cl. 3.9). No confidential data in tools that retain inputs or train on them. Material AI-generated content must be disclosed.
- **Data protection** (cl. 9). DPDP processor terms, 24-hour breach notice, and support for the CERT-In six-hour report and 180-day log retention.
- **Independent contractor** (cl. 10). No benefits under the Labour Codes. The consultant is responsible for its own personnel and indemnifies the client against misclassification claims.
- **Restrictive covenants** (cl. 11). A competitor restriction during the term only, and a narrow post-term non-solicit.
- **Warranties, indemnity and cap** (cl. 12–14). Covers IP, confidentiality, data, misclassification and tax. The cap does not apply to the key risks.
- **Termination** (cl. 16–17). For convenience or cause, including on insolvency under the IBC. Handover assistance and pro-rata fees on termination.
- **Force majeure; arbitration** (cl. 18–19). Seat and venue are separate, appointment is mutual or under s.11, and s.12A Commercial Courts Act mediation applies to court matters.

## India-specific notes

- **Stamp duty.** Stamp duty is payable under the stamp law of the State of execution, usually under the "agreement" article. Some States have specific entries for service or work contracts, so check the article and rate. E-stamp before or at signing. An unstamped agreement is not void, but it is inadmissible until the duty and penalty are paid; its arbitration clause remains enforceable (*In re: Interplay*, 2023).
- **MSMED Act.** If the consultant is a registered micro or small enterprise:
  - Payment is due within the agreed period, which cannot exceed 45 days from acceptance (s.15).
  - Delay attracts compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate (s.16).
  - The consultant can refer a dispute to the MSE Facilitation Council (s.18).
  - Your tax deduction for the payment may also be affected.
- **Misclassification.** Authorities and courts look at the substance of the relationship: control, integration, exclusivity and economic dependence. Getting it wrong exposes the company to PF, ESI, gratuity and wage claims under the Code on Wages, 2019 and the Code on Social Security, 2020.
- **Restraint of trade.** Under s.27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, post-term non-competes are void (*Superintendence Co. v. Krishan Murgai*; *Percept D'Mark v. Zaheer Khan*). Courts are divided on non-solicits, so keep them narrow.
- **Copyright.** Without a stated duration and territory, an assignment defaults to 5 years and India only (s.19(5)–(6)). Moral rights under s.57 survive assignment.
- **Limitation.** Clauses shortening the time to sue are void under s.28 of the Indian Contract Act. The template preserves statutory limitation.
- **Cross-border consultants.** For a consultant outside India:
  - GST is payable under reverse charge on imported services.
  - Withholding tax applies, subject to DTAA relief.
  - Remittance must be made through an authorised dealer bank under FEMA.
- **E-signature.** E-signing is valid under ss.3A and 10A of the Information Technology Act, 2000. Keep the audit trail to support a certificate under s.63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Complete Sch. 1 (scope, deliverables, critical milestones, restricted competitors) and Sch. 2 (fees, schedule, expenses).
- [ ] Confirm whether the consultant is an MSE. If so, keep payment at 45 days or less.
- [ ] Confirm the GST registration and the TDS category with your tax adviser.
- [ ] List the Key Personnel and any Background IP (Sch. 3). Avoid blanket background-IP listings.
- [ ] Set the liability cap. Decide whether a super-cap should apply for data breaches.
- [ ] Set the non-solicit period (6–12 months) and the confidentiality survival period.
- [ ] Decide insurance and background-verification requirements.
- [ ] Choose the seat, venue and any institution, and who bears stamp duty.
- [ ] Check that the working arrangements really reflect independence.
