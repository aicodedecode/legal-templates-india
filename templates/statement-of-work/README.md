# Statement of Work

**Category:** Commercial & Enterprise · **Type:** agreement · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

A project-level Statement of Work (SOW) issued under the master-services-agreement template. The legal terms live in the MSA: IP, confidentiality, data protection, liability and disputes. The SOW records the commercial and operational detail of a single engagement:

- what will be done, and what will not
- what will be delivered, when, and in what format
- how deliverables will be accepted
- how and when the provider will be paid
- what the customer must provide
- who is on each team
- how the project will be governed and changed

The SOW cross-refers to specific MSA clauses (acceptance in cl. 6, fees and the MSMED Act in cl. 7–8, IP in cl. 10, data protection in cl. 12), so the two documents work as one contract.

## When to use it

- You have signed (or are signing at the same time) the master-services-agreement, and you need to document a specific project, phase or work package.
- The work is fixed-price, milestone-based or time-and-materials software development, implementation, integration or consulting.
- You need a clear scope baseline against which to measure change requests and acceptance.

## When not to use it

- **No MSA is in place.** An SOW on its own lacks the essential legal terms. Sign the MSA first, or convert the SOW into a standalone services agreement.
- **Ongoing managed services or support** measured by availability and response times. Use an SOW together with a service-level-agreement schedule, or a dedicated managed-services SOW.
- **SaaS subscriptions.** Use an Order Form under the saas-subscription-agreement.
- **Engagements with individual freelancers.** Use the consultancy-agreement.

## Key provisions

- **Incorporation and precedence (Part 1, Part 12).** The MSA prevails over the SOW. An SOW can vary an MSA clause only by naming it in Part 12.
- **Scope and out-of-scope (Part 3).** Phased delivery (discovery, build, testing, deployment, hypercare), a Project Plan mechanism, and an explicit out-of-scope list with general exclusions.
- **Deliverables (Part 4).** A table recording the format, due date, whether each deliverable is subject to acceptance, and its IP treatment. Software deliverables must come with source code, build instructions and an open-source component list. Provider Tools are listed up front.
- **Acceptance criteria (Part 5).** Objective, severity-based criteria for software. The customer must share its test cases in advance, and go-live counts as acceptance.
- **Fees (Part 6).** A milestone payment table tied to acceptance and go-live, a rate card for change requests, the expenses option, and cross-references to the GST, TDS and MSMED Act clauses in the MSA. If a milestone is delayed by the customer, the provider can invoice pro rata.
- **Assumptions and dependencies (Part 7).** Assumptions that underpin the price, and customer dependencies. Failure to meet a dependency is a "Customer Default" under the MSA.
- **Team and governance (Parts 8 and 10).** Key personnel, a weekly status meeting, a monthly steering committee, a RAID log and an escalation path aligned with the MSA.
- **Data protection (Part 9).** Records the personal data involved and the DPDP roles, restricts use of production data in test environments, and removes access at the end of the project.
- **Change Request form (Schedule A).** Request, impact assessment and decision, all on one form.

## India-specific notes

- **Stamp duty.** Some States may treat an SOW as a separate instrument, chargeable on its own (sometimes ad valorem, on its contract value). Others may treat it as part of the stamped MSA. Check the stamp law of the State where you sign, and stamp at or before signing. An unstamped SOW cannot be admitted in evidence until duty and penalty are paid, but it is not void (*In re Interplay*, 2023).
- **E-signature and evidence.** SOWs and Change Requests may be signed electronically under the Information Technology Act, 2000. Keep the e-sign audit trail, because a certificate under s.63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 may be needed to prove an electronic record. Approvals given by email are binding only if the MSA or SOW says email is enough. This template requires signed Change Requests for anything that affects scope, price or timelines.
- **MSMED Act.** If the provider is a micro or small enterprise, the 45-day payment limit runs from acceptance or deemed acceptance. If you tie milestones to acceptance, the deemed-acceptance mechanism in the MSA matters to both sides. A buyer who pays late also faces a tax consequence under applicable tax laws.
- **Copyright.** Section 19 of the Copyright Act, 1957 requires an assignment to identify the work. The Deliverables table, read with MSA cl. 10.3, does that. Keep it accurate.
- **GST.** Milestone invoices must be issued when the milestone is achieved, in line with the GST time-of-supply rules for continuous or milestone-based supplies. Check the rules with your tax adviser.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Confirm the date of the MSA, and allocate the SOW number.
- [ ] Write a specific scope and out-of-scope list. Avoid "including but not limited to" in scope descriptions.
- [ ] Complete the Deliverables table, including the IP treatment, and list the Provider Tools.
- [ ] Agree objective acceptance criteria and the severity definitions.
- [ ] Choose a pricing model. Complete the milestone table, or replace it with time-and-materials terms.
- [ ] Choose the expenses option.
- [ ] List the assumptions that drive the price, and the customer dependencies.
- [ ] Name the key personnel and the governance attendees.
- [ ] Describe the personal data involved, or state "None". Make sure a DPA is in place.
- [ ] Record any MSA variations in Part 12, citing the clause numbers.
- [ ] Check the stamp duty position for this SOW.
