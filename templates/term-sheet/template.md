# TERM SHEET – {{round_name}} INVESTMENT IN {{company_name}}

> **Drafting note:** This term sheet records the agreed commercial terms before the definitive documents are negotiated. Those documents are the share subscription agreement (**SSA**), the shareholders' agreement (**SHA**) and the restated Articles of Association (**AoA**). Only the Binding Provisions listed in paragraph 29 create legal obligations: confidentiality, exclusivity, costs, and governing law and dispute resolution, plus the mechanical clauses needed to make them work. Everything else is written in descriptive language ("the Transaction Documents will provide...") on purpose. Indian courts decide whether a preliminary document is binding from the language used and from the parties' conduct, so do not convert this into promissory language. Defined terms here match those in the shareholders' agreement and iSAFE templates in this repository, so the term sheet can be carried into the SSA and SHA without re-definition.

This term sheet (the **"Term Sheet"**) dated {{effective_date}} sets out the principal terms on which the Investors propose to subscribe to securities of {{company_name}}, a private company incorporated under the Companies Act, 2013 with corporate identity number {{company_cin}} and having its registered office at {{company_address}} (the **"Company"**).

## 1. Definitions and Interpretation

1.1 In this Term Sheet, unless the context requires otherwise:

**"Act"** means the Companies Act, 2013 and the rules made under it, each as amended from time to time.

**"Affiliate"** means, in relation to a person, any other person that directly or indirectly Controls, is Controlled by or is under common Control with that person. In relation to an Investor that is a fund, it includes its general partner, investment manager or adviser, and any fund managed or advised by any of them. **"Control"** has the meaning given in section 2(27) of the Act.

**"Articles"** means the articles of association of the Company.

**"Board"** means the board of directors of the Company.

**"Business Day"** means a day, other than a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday, on which scheduled commercial banks are open for business in the city in which the Company's registered office is situated.

**"CCPS"** means the {{ccps_series_name}}, being fully paid-up, non-cumulative, compulsorily convertible preference shares of the Company of face value {{ccps_face_value}} each, carrying the rights set out in this Term Sheet.

**"Closing"** means the allotment of the CCPS to the Investors against receipt of the Subscription Amount. **"Closing Date"** means the date on which Closing occurs.

**"Competitor"** means a person engaged in a business that competes with the business of the Company, as listed in a schedule to the SHA and updated by the Board (with the consent of the Investor Director) not more than once in each financial year.

**"Equity Shares"** means equity shares of the Company of face value {{equity_face_value}} each.

**"ESOP Plan"** means the employee stock option plan of the Company adopted under section 62(1)(b) of the Act, and **"ESOP Pool"** means the Equity Shares reserved for issue under it.

**"Exempted Issuance"** has the meaning given in paragraph 7.3.

**"FEMA"** means the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 and the rules, regulations, master directions and press notes made or issued under it, including the NDI Rules and the consolidated foreign direct investment policy of the Government of India.

**"Fully Diluted Basis"** means, in relation to a computation of share capital, a computation that assumes the conversion of all outstanding convertible securities, the exercise of all outstanding options and warrants (whether vested or unvested), and the issue of all Equity Shares reserved under the ESOP Pool (whether granted or ungranted).

**"Investor Majority"** means Investors holding more than 50% of the CCPS then outstanding.

**"Liquidation Event"** has the meaning given in paragraph 8.2.

**"NDI Rules"** means the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019.

**"Qualified IPO"** means an initial public offering of Equity Shares and listing on a recognised stock exchange in India (or, if permitted by law, outside India) at a pre-money valuation and on terms approved by the Investor Majority.

**"Reserved Matters"** means the matters set out in Schedule 1.

**"Securities"** means Equity Shares, preference shares, debentures, options, warrants or any other instrument convertible into or exchangeable for Equity Shares.

**"Subscription Amount"** means the aggregate amount payable by the Investors for the CCPS under paragraph 3.

**"Transaction Documents"** means the SSA, the SHA, the restated Articles and every other document the parties agree to designate as a Transaction Document.

1.2 Headings are for convenience only. "Including" means "including without limitation". References to a statute include its amendments and re-enactments. References to paragraphs and Schedules are to paragraphs of and Schedules to this Term Sheet.

## 3. Investment

3.1 **Round size:** an aggregate Subscription Amount of up to {{round_size}}. Of this, the Lead Investor will subscribe {{lead_investor_commitment}}, and the balance will be subscribed by the other Investors in the proportions agreed with the Lead Investor.

3.2 **Instrument:** CCPS, issued by private placement on a preferential basis under sections 42 and 62(1)(c) of the Act. If the Lead Investor requires, the Investors may also be issued a nominal number of Equity Shares so that they hold voting Securities.

3.3 **Pre-money valuation:** {{pre_money_valuation}}, on a Fully Diluted Basis. This includes the ESOP Pool as expanded under paragraph 3.5.

3.4 **Issue price per CCPS:** the pre-money valuation divided by the number of Equity Shares on a Fully Diluted Basis immediately before Closing. The price will be rounded to two decimal places and will not be lower than the fair value floor in paragraph 4.

3.5 **ESOP Pool:** before Closing, the Company will expand the ESOP Pool so that the ungranted portion equals {{esop_pool_percentage}}% of the share capital of the Company on a Fully Diluted Basis immediately after Closing.

3.6 **Use of proceeds:** the Subscription Amount will be used for the business of the Company in accordance with the business plan approved by the Board. It will not be used for any activity in which foreign investment is prohibited or for any purpose restricted by FEMA. Until Closing, it will not be used at all, in accordance with section 42 of the Act.

> **Drafting note:** If the post-money ESOP Pool is included in the pre-money valuation, as paragraph 3.3 provides, the Founders and Existing Investors bear all of the dilution from the pool expansion. Founders should model the cap table under both conventions before agreeing to this. An instrument that is only optionally convertible, or is redeemable, is treated as debt under FEMA and has to comply with the external commercial borrowing framework. Keep the CCPS fully, compulsorily and mandatorily convertible.

## 4. Regulatory and Pricing Compliance

4.1 **FEMA:** the issue of CCPS to any Investor who is a person resident outside India will comply with FEMA, including entry route, sectoral caps and conditions, pricing guidelines, mode of payment, and reporting.

4.2 **Land-border investors:** each Investor will confirm to the Company whether it, or any beneficial owner of its investment, is an entity of, is situated in, or is a citizen of a country that shares a land border with India. Where this applies, the investment will be made only after government approval is obtained under the NDI Rules (as amended following Press Note 3 (2020 Series)). Obtaining that approval will be a Condition Precedent.

4.3 **Pricing floor:** where an Investor is a person resident outside India, the issue price of the CCPS will be not less than the fair value of the Equity Shares. The fair value will be determined by an internationally accepted pricing methodology on an arm's-length basis and certified by a chartered accountant or a SEBI-registered merchant banker, or by such other valuer as FEMA permits. The conversion formula of the CCPS will be fixed at the time of issue, and the price per Equity Share on conversion will not be lower than the fair value determined at the time of issue.

4.4 **Valuation under the Act:** the Company will also obtain a valuation report from a registered valuer, as required for a preferential allotment under the Act and the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014.

4.5 **Receipt and reporting:** the Subscription Amount will be received by inward remittance through banking channels or from a permitted Indian account of the Investor, into a separate bank account opened by the Company for this private placement. The Company will allot the CCPS within 60 days of receipt of the Subscription Amount. If it does not, the Company will refund the amount within the time and in the manner required by section 42 of the Act and FEMA. The Company will report the issue in Form FC-GPR through the FIRMS portal of the Reserve Bank of India within 30 days of allotment.

4.6 **Tax:** the Investors acknowledge that the levy formerly charged on share premium received by closely held companies in excess of fair market value (commonly called "angel tax") was abolished with effect from assessment year 2025-26. The Company will nevertheless obtain any valuation report that applicable tax laws require for the issue.

> **Drafting note:** Verify each of the following against the current RBI Master Direction on Foreign Investment in India and the NDI Rules before signing [verify]: (a) the class of professional who may certify fair value (chartered accountant, SEBI-registered merchant banker or practising cost accountant); (b) the 30-day period for FC-GPR; (c) the 60-day allotment period and the refund window after it; and (d) the sector's entry route and cap, and whether any performance conditions apply. Do not hard-code a sectoral cap into this document. For a resident-only round, paragraphs 4.1 to 4.3 and 4.5 (FEMA reporting) can be deleted, but the registered valuer's report in paragraph 4.4 is still required. Paragraph 4.6 reflects the abolition of angel tax by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024, which took effect before the Income-tax Act, 2025 came into force. No section of either Act is cited, in line with this repository's standard.

## 5. Terms of the CCPS

5.1 **Dividend:** each CCPS will carry a preferential, non-cumulative dividend of {{ccps_dividend_rate}} per annum on its face value, payable only when declared by the Company. If the Company declares a dividend on Equity Shares, each CCPS will also participate pari passu with the Equity Shares on an as-if-converted basis.

5.2 **Conversion ratio:** each CCPS will initially convert into one Equity Share. The ratio will be adjusted for anti-dilution under paragraph 7 and for share splits, consolidations, bonus issues and similar corporate actions.

5.3 **Optional conversion:** a holder may convert any or all of its CCPS at any time by written notice to the Company.

5.4 **Mandatory conversion:** each CCPS will convert automatically on the earliest of:

(a) the latest date permitted by applicable law for conversion before the filing of an offer document for a Qualified IPO; and

(b) the date falling {{conversion_long_stop_years}} years from the date of allotment.

5.5 **Voting:** until conversion, the CCPS will carry voting rights as provided in section 47 of the Act. The Transaction Documents will provide that the Investors are entitled, to the extent permitted by law, to vote on an as-if-converted basis. To that end, the Company and the Founders will procure that the Investors' views are given effect on any shareholder resolution.

5.6 **Rank:** the CCPS will rank senior to Equity Shares on dividend and on return of capital, and pari passu with any other series of preference shares unless the Investor Majority agrees otherwise.

> **Drafting note:** Under section 47(2) of the Act, preference shareholders vote only on resolutions that directly affect the rights attached to their shares (and on all resolutions if preferential dividend remains unpaid for the prescribed period). As-converted voting is therefore contractual. Investors often also take a few Equity Shares (paragraph 3.2). Under section 55 of the Act, preference shares must be redeemed or converted within 20 years of issue [verify], so keep the long-stop in paragraph 5.4(b) inside that limit.

## 6. Pre-emptive Rights

6.1 Each Investor will have a right, but not an obligation, to subscribe to any further issue of Securities in proportion to its shareholding on a Fully Diluted Basis, on the same terms as those offered to other subscribers. Unsubscribed entitlements will be re-offered to the Investors who took up their full entitlement. This right does not apply to an Exempted Issuance.

6.2 The Company will make any such issue by private placement or by a rights issue under section 62(1)(a) of the Act, as the Investor Majority may require.

## 7. Anti-dilution Protection

7.1 If the Company issues any Securities (other than in an Exempted Issuance) at a price per Equity Share, on an as-if-converted basis, that is lower than the conversion price of the CCPS then in effect (a **"Dilutive Issuance"**), the conversion price of the CCPS will be reduced on a broad-based weighted average basis using the formula:

CP2 = CP1 × (A + B) ÷ (A + C)

where:

(a) **CP2** is the conversion price in effect immediately after the Dilutive Issuance;

(b) **CP1** is the conversion price in effect immediately before the Dilutive Issuance;

(c) **A** is the number of Equity Shares outstanding on a Fully Diluted Basis immediately before the Dilutive Issuance;

(d) **B** is the number of Equity Shares that the aggregate consideration received by the Company for the Dilutive Issuance would purchase at CP1; and

(e) **C** is the number of Equity Shares issued, or issuable on conversion or exercise of the Securities issued, in the Dilutive Issuance.

7.2 The conversion ratio of each CCPS will then be adjusted to the issue price of that CCPS divided by CP2.

7.3 **Exempted Issuance** means any issue of:

(a) Equity Shares or options under the ESOP Plan within the ESOP Pool;

(b) Equity Shares on conversion of the CCPS or of any other Security outstanding on the Closing Date;

(c) Securities in a bonus issue, split or consolidation made proportionately to all shareholders;

(d) Securities in a Qualified IPO; and

(e) Securities that the Investor Majority approves in writing as an Exempted Issuance.

7.4 If the adjustment cannot be given effect through the conversion ratio, the Company and the Founders will take any other step permitted by applicable law to put the Investors in the position they would have been in had the adjustment been made. This may include a fresh issue, a bonus issue or a transfer by the Founders.

> **Drafting note:** Broad-based weighted average is the market default in India. Resist "full ratchet". For non-resident Investors, any adjustment must respect paragraph 4.3: the effective conversion price cannot go below the fair value determined when the CCPS were issued. Under paragraph 7.4, a fresh issue to a non-resident must meet the pricing floor at that time, and a transfer by a Founder to a non-resident must meet the transfer pricing guidelines in the NDI Rules. These constraints often limit how far paragraph 7.4 can operate.

## 8. Liquidation Preference

8.1 On the occurrence of a Liquidation Event, the proceeds available for distribution to the shareholders (the **"Proceeds"**) will be distributed as follows:

(a) **first,** to each holder of CCPS, in priority to all other shareholders, the higher of (i) one time (1x) the subscription amount paid for its CCPS plus any declared but unpaid dividends, and (ii) the amount it would receive if all CCPS had been converted into Equity Shares immediately before the Liquidation Event;

(b) **second,** the balance to the holders of Equity Shares, pro rata to their shareholding. A holder of CCPS that has received an amount under paragraph 8.1(a)(i) will not participate further.

If the Proceeds are insufficient to pay the amounts in paragraph 8.1(a) in full, they will be distributed among the holders of CCPS pro rata to the amounts due to them.

8.2 **Liquidation Event** means any of the following:

(a) a voluntary or compulsory winding up or dissolution of the Company;

(b) a sale, lease, exclusive licence or other disposal of all or substantially all of the assets, undertaking or intellectual property of the Company;

(c) a merger, demerger, amalgamation, scheme of arrangement or acquisition of Securities as a result of which the shareholders of the Company immediately before that transaction hold less than 50% of the voting share capital of the surviving or acquiring entity; and

(d) any transaction or series of related transactions resulting in a change of Control of the Company.

8.3 The Founders and the Company will do everything required, including voting, executing transfer instruments and amending the Articles, to give effect to this paragraph. This includes reallocating the consideration received by shareholders on a Liquidation Event that takes the form of a sale of Securities.

> **Drafting note:** In a statutory winding up, distribution is governed by the Act and, where applicable, the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016. Preference shareholders rank ahead of equity only for return of paid-up capital, so a preference over the full subscription amount (including premium) operates contractually between shareholders. For a non-resident Investor, the waterfall must not guarantee a return. Any reallocation from residents to non-residents on a share sale must comply with the NDI Rules pricing guidelines.

## 9. Board and Management

9.1 **Board composition:** the Board will consist of not more than {{board_size}} directors. Of these:

(a) {{founder_director_count}} will be nominated by the Founders, for so long as the Founders remain in full-time employment of the Company;

(b) {{investor_director_count}} will be nominated by the Lead Investor (the **"Investor Director"**), for so long as the Lead Investor holds at least {{investor_rights_threshold}} of the share capital on a Fully Diluted Basis; and

(c) any remaining directors will be independent directors appointed by the Board, with the consent of the Founders and the Investor Director.

9.2 **Observer:** the Lead Investor may appoint one non-voting observer, who will receive all notices and papers given to directors and may attend all Board and committee meetings.

9.3 **Meetings:** the Board will meet at least once every calendar quarter, with no more than 120 days between two consecutive meetings (section 173 of the Act). Directors may participate by video-conferencing or other audio-visual means as permitted by the Act.

9.4 **Quorum:** the quorum for a Board meeting will be as required by section 174 of the Act and must include the Investor Director, unless the Investor Director has waived this requirement in writing. If no quorum is present within 30 minutes, the meeting will stand adjourned to the same day and time in the following week. At the adjourned meeting, the directors present will form a quorum (subject to the Act) for business specified in the original notice, provided that no Reserved Matter is taken up without the prior written consent of the Investor Director.

9.5 **Committees:** the Investor Director may be appointed to every committee of the Board.

9.6 **Non-executive status:** the Investor Director will be a non-executive director. The Investor Director will not be identified as an officer in default or as a person responsible for statutory compliance, and the Company will nominate other persons for that purpose.

9.7 **Insurance and indemnity:** within 90 days of Closing, the Company will obtain directors' and officers' liability insurance on terms acceptable to the Investor Director. The Company will indemnify the Investor Director and the observer to the maximum extent permitted by law.

9.8 **Expenses:** the Company will reimburse the reasonable expenses incurred by the Investor Director and the observer in attending meetings.

> **Drafting note:** The appointment of the Investor Director needs a Director Identification Number, consent in Form DIR-2, and a declaration of non-disqualification. It must be filed in Form DIR-12 within 30 days. A private company must have at least two directors, and at least one director must have stayed in India for at least 182 days in the previous calendar year (section 149(3) of the Act). Always keep the adjourned-meeting fall-back in paragraph 9.4, so that the Company cannot be paralysed by a nominee's absence.

## 10. Reserved Matters

10.1 The Company will not take any action listed in Schedule 1 (a **"Reserved Matter"**) without the prior written consent of the Investor Majority, which must include the Lead Investor for so long as it holds at least {{investor_rights_threshold}} of the share capital on a Fully Diluted Basis. This applies whether the action is taken at a Board meeting, a committee meeting or a general meeting, and whether by resolution or by circulation. The Founders will exercise their voting rights to ensure compliance with this paragraph.

10.2 Consent may be given by the Investor Director at a Board meeting, or by email from an authorised representative of the Lead Investor. If the Lead Investor does not respond within 10 Business Days of a written request containing all material information, and then a further 5 Business Days after a reminder, consent will be deemed given. This deemed consent does not apply to the matters in paragraphs (a) to (f) of Schedule 1.

## 11. Information and Inspection Rights

11.1 For so long as an Investor holds at least {{investor_rights_threshold}} of the share capital on a Fully Diluted Basis, the Company will deliver to it:

(a) monthly management information, including revenue, burn, cash balance, headcount and key operating metrics, within {{mis_days}} days of the end of each month;

(b) unaudited quarterly financial statements within {{quarterly_days}} days of the end of each quarter;

(c) audited annual financial statements, prepared under the applicable accounting standards, within {{audited_days}} days of the end of each financial year;

(d) the annual business plan and budget, approved by the Board, at least {{budget_days}} days before the start of each financial year;

(e) copies of all filings with the Registrar of Companies and the Reserve Bank of India, within 7 days of filing;

(f) prompt written notice of any litigation, regulatory notice, personal data breach, material cyber incident or other event that has or could reasonably be expected to have a material adverse effect on the Company; and

(g) any other information that the Investor reasonably requests, including for its own regulatory or limited-partner reporting.

11.2 Each such Investor and its authorised representatives may, on 7 Business Days' notice, inspect the books, records and premises of the Company, and discuss its affairs with its directors, officers and statutory auditors.

## 12. Founder Commitments

12.1 **Full-time engagement:** each Founder will devote his or her full working time and attention to the Company and will hold no other employment, directorship or office (other than as disclosed and approved by the Investor Director).

12.2 **Employment agreement:** each Founder will enter into an employment agreement with the Company on terms acceptable to the Lead Investor. The agreement will include confidentiality, IP assignment, and non-compete and non-solicitation provisions.

12.3 **Non-compete:** while a Founder is employed by or is a director of the Company, the Founder will not directly or indirectly engage in, invest in or advise any business that competes with the Company. For 12 months after ceasing to be so employed, the Founder will not solicit or entice away any employee, customer or supplier of the Company.

12.4 **Intellectual property:** each Founder will assign to the Company all intellectual property relating to the business of the Company created by the Founder before or after Closing, for the full term of protection and throughout the world, and will waive or agree not to assert moral rights to the extent permitted by law.

12.5 **Lock-in:** no Founder will transfer, or create any encumbrance over, any Securities for {{founder_lockin_months}} months from the Closing Date without the prior written consent of the Lead Investor. This does not apply to transfers to a trust or entity wholly owned and controlled by the Founder for estate-planning purposes, provided the transferee executes a deed of adherence and the Founder remains jointly and severally liable.

12.6 **Reverse vesting:** {{founder_vesting_percentage}} of each Founder's Securities held at Closing will vest in equal monthly instalments over {{founder_vesting_months}} months from the Closing Date, with credit for service before Closing as agreed. All unvested Securities will vest on a Liquidation Event, or on the Founder's termination without cause within 12 months after one. If a Founder ceases to be employed before full vesting:

(a) as a **good leaver** (death, permanent incapacity, termination without cause), the Founder will retain the vested Securities and the unvested Securities will be subject to transfer; and

(b) as a **bad leaver** (resignation within the vesting period, or termination for cause, being fraud, wilful misconduct, conviction for an offence involving moral turpitude, or material uncured breach of the Transaction Documents), the Founder will be required to transfer all unvested Securities, and the Investor Majority may require the Founder to transfer up to 50% of the vested Securities.

In each case, the transfer will be to a person nominated by the Board (including an employee welfare trust), at the lowest price permitted by applicable law.

> **Drafting note:** Restraints on trade after a Founder's employment ends are void under section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. The sale-of-goodwill exception rarely applies to a Founder who is not selling shares. Paragraph 12.3 therefore restricts competition only during the Founder's tenure, and relies on non-solicitation and confidentiality afterwards. India has no simple "repurchase at cost" mechanism. A buy-back has to meet section 68 of the Act, and a transfer to a non-resident has to meet the NDI Rules pricing floor, so reverse vesting is implemented as an obligation to transfer to a Board nominee. Check the tax position of both transferor and transferee on any transfer below fair value.

## 13. Transfer of Securities

13.1 **Right of first refusal:** after the lock-in period, if a Founder proposes to transfer any Securities to a third party, the Investors will have a right of first refusal, pro rata to their shareholding, on the same price and terms.

13.2 **Tag-along:** if the Investors do not exercise their right of first refusal, each Investor may require the transferee to buy, on the same price and terms, a proportionate number of the Investor's Securities. If the transfer would result in a change of Control of the Company, each Investor may require the transferee to buy all of the Investor's Securities.

13.3 **Investor transfers:** each Investor may transfer its Securities freely to any person other than a Competitor, subject to FEMA and to the transferee executing a deed of adherence. Rights linked to shareholding thresholds will be transferred with the Securities. Transfers to an Affiliate will carry all of the Investor's rights.

13.4 **Pricing and reporting:** any transfer between a person resident in India and a person resident outside India will be made at a price that complies with the NDI Rules pricing guidelines, and will be reported in Form FC-TRS within the time prescribed.

> **Drafting note:** Under FEMA, a transfer from a resident to a non-resident must be at or above fair value, and a transfer from a non-resident to a resident must be at or below fair value [verify current guidelines]. Form FC-TRS is generally due within 60 days of receipt of consideration [verify]. The restrictions in this paragraph bind the Company only if they are written into the Articles: see paragraph 18 and the drafting note on *V.B. Rangaraj* in the shareholders' agreement template.

## 14. Exit

14.1 **Exit period:** the Company and the Founders will use their best efforts to provide the Investors with a full exit within {{exit_years}} years from the Closing Date (the **"Exit Period"**), by:

The drag-along will be subject to the following conditions:

(a) the Founders will first have a right of first offer, exercisable within 60 days, to purchase the Investors' Securities;

(b) the implied equity value of the Company in the drag sale must be at least {{drag_valuation_floor}}, unless the Founders consent;

(d) the Founders will give only warranties of title and capacity, and their liability will be capped at the proceeds they receive.

> **Drafting note:** Put options, buy-back obligations and other optionality in favour of a non-resident Investor are permitted under the NDI Rules only where there is no assured return and the Securities have been held for a minimum lock-in period of one year [verify], at a price not exceeding fair value at the time of exit. For that reason this term sheet relies on best-efforts exit obligations and a drag-along rather than a put. If a resident Investor insists on a put option, check whether it would make the Securities a form of debt or deposit, and whether the Company's other non-resident shareholders would object.
