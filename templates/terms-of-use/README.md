# Terms of Use (Website and App)

**Category:** Privacy & Web · **Type:** agreement · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

Click-wrap terms of use for an Indian website or mobile app. The terms are built around three regimes that every Indian consumer platform has to reconcile:

- the **Indian Contract Act, 1872**, covering capacity, consideration, indemnity and the limits on damages and restraint;
- the **IT Act, 2000 and the IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021**, covering the safe harbour under s.79, the prohibited-content categories in rule 3(1)(b), takedown, the Grievance Officer and the Grievance Appellate Committee, plus the amendments on synthetically generated information;
- the **Consumer Protection Act, 2019, the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 and the CCPA Dark Patterns Guidelines, 2023**, under which unfair terms can be struck down and consumer fora cannot be ousted.

## When to use it

- Consumer-facing or prosumer websites and apps: marketplaces, content platforms, community apps, freemium SaaS with self-serve sign-up, ed-tech and similar services.
- Platforms that host user-generated content and want to rely on the intermediary safe harbour.
- Services that charge subscriptions or one-off fees through an online checkout.

## When not to use it

- **Negotiated B2B SaaS sold through order forms.** Use `saas-subscription-agreement` with a `service-level-agreement` and a `data-processing-agreement`.
- **Regulated activities.** Payments, lending, insurance distribution, securities, online gaming involving stakes, telemedicine and similar activities need sector-specific terms required by the RBI, IRDAI, SEBI, MeitY or other regulators.
- **Significant social media intermediaries.** This template only points to the extra rule 4 duties of a significant social media intermediary. Platforms above the user threshold notified by MeitY need specialist drafting.

## Key provisions

- **Eligibility (cl. 2).** Users must have capacity to contract. Minors may use the platform only through a parent or guardian, which ties in with the verifiable parental consent requirement in s.9 of the DPDP Act.
- **Prohibited content (cl. 5).** A paraphrase of the rule 3(1)(b) categories, plus platform-conduct rules.
- **Synthetically generated information (cl. 5A).** Covers user declarations, labels and metadata, and a prohibition on removing identifiers.
- **User content licence (cl. 6).** A narrow, operational licence, an AI-training option and a moral-rights undertaking under s.57.
- **Intermediary mechanics (cl. 7).** Takedown on court or government order, a published Grievance Officer, the 24-hour and 15-day timelines, the 24-hour timeline for non-consensual intimate imagery, and the right of appeal to the Grievance Appellate Committee.
- **Fees (cl. 9).** Auto-renewal, free trials, the RBI e-mandate rules and price-change notice, all drafted to avoid dark patterns.
- **Liability cap and indemnity (cl. 13–14).** Proportionate, with express preservation of consumer-law rights.
- **Disputes (cl. 17).** Courts or arbitration (with seat and venue stated separately and no unilateral appointment), with an express carve-out preserving consumer commissions.
- **E-commerce add-on (cl. 21).** Seller disclosures, cancellation symmetry, rules on reviews, and a consumer grievance officer.

## India-specific notes

- **Safe harbour depends on due diligence.** Under s.79(2)–(3) of the IT Act, the safe harbour is lost if you fail to observe the IT Rules. These include publishing these terms and the privacy policy, informing users of the rules at least once a year, appointing a Grievance Officer and meeting the takedown timelines. The terms alone are not enough; your processes must actually meet these requirements.
- **The IT Rules keep changing.** Amendments were made in 2022 and 2023, the fact-check-unit provision was struck down by the Bombay High Court in 2024, and synthetic-media amendments followed. Check the current consolidated text before each release.
- **Online gaming.** The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 prohibits online money games and their advertising [verify current status and rules]. Clause 5.1(i) must be read with it.
- **Consumer law overrides.** Under s.2(46) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, the State and National Commissions can declare a term "unfair". An arbitration clause does not bar a consumer complaint (*Emaar MGF v Aftab Singh*, SC 2018).
- **Minors.** A minor's agreement is void (*Mohori Bibee*). Get a parent or guardian to accept on the minor's behalf.
- **Click-wrap evidence.** Keep acceptance logs. In proceedings, electronic records need a certificate under s.63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023.
- **Stamp duty.** Treated as not applicable. Online click-wrap terms are not executed as instruments, and stamp duty is not paid on them in practice. If a particular State treats electronic agreements as instruments, an unstamped instrument is inadmissible in evidence until the duty and penalty are paid, but it is not void (*In re Interplay*, 2023). Check the position if your terms will be enforced against business users.
- **Taxes.** Prices are stated exclusive of GST unless you say otherwise.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Describe the Services and decide whether minors may use them (Option A or B).
- [ ] Decide whether Users can post content, and delete clauses 5A, 6 and 7 if they cannot.
- [ ] Choose an AI-training position for User Content (cl. 6.4) that matches your privacy notice.
- [ ] Name the Grievance Officer and publish their contact details. If you are an e-commerce entity, confirm the E-Commerce Rules timelines.
- [ ] Set the liability cap at a figure that is proportionate for consumers.
- [ ] Choose courts or arbitration and fill in the seat or city, keeping cl. 17.4 in place.
- [ ] Include cl. 21 if you sell goods or services, and publish a refund and cancellation policy.
- [ ] Audit the checkout and cancellation flows against the Dark Patterns Guidelines.
- [ ] Set up the annual user reminder required by the IT Rules.
