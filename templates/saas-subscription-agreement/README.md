# SaaS Subscription Agreement

**Category:** Commercial & Enterprise · **Type:** agreement · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

A business-to-business agreement for a subscription to hosted software (software as a service). It is signed between a SaaS provider and an enterprise customer, and each purchase is recorded in an Order Form. The draft is a balanced provider paper. The provider keeps its platform IP, limits its liability and protects its subscription revenue. The customer gets the commitments that Indian enterprise procurement, information-security and data-protection teams routinely ask for:

- data ownership
- no training of AI models on its data
- DPDP processor terms
- CERT-In-aligned incident handling
- service levels
- an orderly exit

## When to use it

- You sell a cloud product to businesses on annual or multi-year subscriptions, under a negotiated, signed contract.
- Your product includes AI features (copilots, summarisation, generation, classification), and customers want clear rules on training, output ownership and use of third-party models.
- You want one master agreement with repeatable Order Forms for renewals, upsells and customer affiliates.

## When not to use it

- **Self-serve sign-up or click-through** for small businesses or consumers. Use terms-of-use. Make sure consumer protection law is respected; consumer fora cannot be excluded.
- **On-premise software licences**, where the customer installs the software itself. These need a licence of copyright, delivery and acceptance mechanics, and escrow.
- **Significant custom development or implementation**, where the customer will own the deliverables. Add a master-services-agreement and a statement-of-work.
- **Material outsourcing by a regulated entity** (bank, NBFC, insurer, market intermediary). You can use this template as the base, but the regulator's outsourcing directions will require extra audit, inspection, localisation, business continuity and exit terms.

## Key provisions

- **Order Forms and precedence (cl. 2, Sch. 1).** Order Forms govern the commercial terms. They can vary the legal terms only by naming the clause they change. Purchase-order and procurement-portal terms are excluded.
- **Subscription rights (cl. 3).** The customer gets a non-exclusive right to access and use the service, not a licence of the software. Usage limits are enforced through a true-up mechanism. The restrictions preserve interoperability decompilation rights under s.52(1)(ab) of the Copyright Act.
- **Service levels and support (cl. 4).** These are set by reference to the Service Level Schedule (service-level-agreement template). The provider cannot materially degrade functionality during a term, and must give notice before deprecating a feature.
- **Customer data (cl. 6).** The customer owns all its data, including AI input and output. The provider gets a narrow licence to provide the service. Aggregated and de-identified data is permitted, subject to safeguards against re-identification. The provider may not sell the data or use it for advertising.
- **AI features (cl. 7).**
  - The customer's data is never used to train shared models without its express written opt-in, which it can withdraw.
  - Third-party model providers must be bound to zero training and limited retention.
  - The provider assigns to the customer whatever rights it has in the output, without warranting that copyright subsists.
  - Both parties take on human-review and labelling responsibilities.
- **Data protection (cl. 8).** The DPDP roles are set out, with a separate DPA. Processing is on instructions only. The clause covers sub-processor notice and objection, cross-border transfer limits under s.16 DPDP and sector localisation rules. Breach notice to the customer is due within a short fixed window, so the customer can meet the Board's timeline.
- **Security (cl. 9, Sch. 2).** Minimum technical measures, certifications, penetration testing, CERT-In 6-hour reporting and 180-day log retention, and audit rights (including a regulator's audit rights where required).
- **Fees (cl. 10).**
  - GST is charged in addition to the fees, and TDS is handled generically.
  - The MSMED Act 45-day override applies, with a note on the tax deduction a buyer loses by paying late.
  - Renewal price increases are capped, and the customer gets a walk-away right if it rejects an increase.
- **Suspension (cl. 11).** Allowed only after notice for non-payment, and never for amounts disputed in good faith. Suspension for security or legal reasons must be narrowly limited.
- **Liability (cl. 16).** The general cap is 12 months' fees. A separate super-cap, set as a multiple of annual contract value, covers confidentiality, data and security breaches, breach of the no-training promise, and IP indemnities.
- **Exit (cl. 19).** A free data export window in machine-readable formats, paid transition assistance, and certified deletion. Deletion is reconciled with CERT-In and regulatory retention requirements.
- **Disputes (cl. 22–23).** Arbitration with the seat and venue stated separately. The arbitrator is appointed jointly, or through a court or institution, never by one party alone. Section 12A mediation applies to non-arbitrable court matters.

## India-specific notes

- **Stamp duty.** The agreement is chargeable as an "agreement" (or under a residuary article) of the Stamp Act of the State where it is signed or first received. Several States levy ad valorem duty on agreements with a stated value. Check whether Order Forms attract separate duty. Use e-stamping at or before signing. An unstamped agreement is inadmissible until duty and penalty are paid, but it is not void (*In re Interplay*, 2023).
- **E-signature and evidence.** Electronic signatures are valid under the IT Act, 2000. To prove the signed contract, or system records such as usage logs and notices, you may need a certificate under s.63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023. Preserve audit trails.
- **DPDP phasing.** The DPDP Rules, 2025 bring obligations into force in phases. Check which obligations apply on the date of signing. The template is drafted to the full standard, so it will not need amending on commencement.
- **CERT-In vs DPDP.** These are separate clocks: CERT-In requires reporting within 6 hours, while the DPDP Rules require Board intimation and a detailed report (72 hours). The contract's notice period to the customer must sit inside both.
- **AI regulation.** The IT Rules amendments on synthetically generated information, and MeitY advisories, are evolving. Review the AI clause and the Acceptable Use Policy at every renewal.
- **Tax.** Whether SaaS fees are treated as a service or as a royalty affects TDS, especially for non-resident providers. Take tax advice. Do not hard-code section numbers of the Income-tax Act, 2025.
- **MSME suppliers.** If the provider is a micro or small enterprise, the statutory 45-day payment limit and compound interest apply whatever the contract says.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Confirm the legal names, CIN and registered offices, and the signatories' authority.
- [ ] Complete the first Order Form: products, AI features, usage limits, term, fees, support tier and certifications.
- [ ] Decide the AI training position: the default is no training. Record any opt-in specifically.
- [ ] Set the hosting location and confirm the sub-processor list, including third-party AI providers.
- [ ] Set the breach notification hours. Recommend 24 hours or less.
- [ ] Choose the super-cap multiple (2x to 5x ACV), and check it against your insurance.
- [ ] Choose the convenience-termination option.
- [ ] Set the renewal notice period, the renewal price cap and the price-increase notice period.
- [ ] Set the export and deletion periods. Extend them if the customer is regulated.
- [ ] Choose the arbitral seat, the venue and the appointment route.
- [ ] Decide who pays stamp duty.
- [ ] Execute the DPA and attach or link the Service Level Schedule.
