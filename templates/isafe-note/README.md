# iSAFE Note – CCPS Subscription Agreement with Valuation Cap and Discount

**Category:** Fundraising & Corporate · **Type:** agreement · **Jurisdiction:** India · **Status:** Draft

## Overview

The "iSAFE" is an Indian adaptation of the US Simple Agreement for Future Equity. A US SAFE cannot be used as-is in India, because:

- money received against a mere right to future shares risks being a **deposit** under the Companies Act, 2013 and the Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules, 2014;
- a SAFE is not a permitted instrument for foreign investment under FEMA.

The iSAFE solves this by issuing **compulsorily convertible preference shares (CCPS)** immediately. The CCPS carry a nominal dividend and convert into equity at the next priced round (the Qualified Financing), at the lower of a **valuation-cap price** and a **discounted price**.

The template has four parts:

- a subscription agreement covering conditions precedent, closing, the private placement process, FEMA and Press Note 3, representations, an MFN clause, a capped indemnity and arbitration;
- the CCPS Terms (Schedule 1);
- a CP and Closing Checklist (Schedule 3);
- a conversion notice (Schedule 4).

Defined terms match the term sheet and shareholders' agreement templates in this repository.

## When to use it

- Pre-seed, seed or bridge rounds where the founders and investors do not want to fix a valuation now.
- Angel syndicates and early-stage funds investing small amounts in an Indian private limited company.
- Rounds that include non-resident investors. A priced CCPS issue with a fixed conversion formula is the FEMA-compliant route.

## When not to use it

- **The company is not ready to run a private placement.** It needs a special resolution, PAS-4, a separate bank account, valuation reports and PAS-3. If it cannot do this, consider a convertible note, but only if the company is DPIIT-recognised and the note meets the size and tenure conditions [verify].
- **A priced round with a lead investor.** Use the term sheet and the shareholders' agreement instead.
- **The company is an LLP.** An LLP cannot issue preference shares.
- **The investor needs governance rights.** The iSAFE gives only light information rights and class protection. Board seats and reserved matters belong in a shareholders' agreement.

## Key provisions

- **Subscription and private placement (clauses 2–4):** money is paid from the investor's own account into a separate account, and is not used until allotment and PAS-3. Allotment must happen within 60 days, failing which there is a refund with interest.
- **Regulatory (clause 6):**
  - Residential status and land-border (Press Note 3) declarations.
  - For non-residents, FEMA fair value certified by a chartered accountant or a SEBI-registered merchant banker.
  - The conversion formula is fixed at issue, with a **Floor Price**.
  - Records that angel tax was abolished with effect from AY 2025-26.
- **Covenants until conversion (clause 9):** class consent under section 48, notice of financing or exit, sufficient authorised capital, the CCPS Terms embodied in the Articles, and an MFN with a FEMA-aware drafting note.
- **CCPS Terms (Schedule 1):**
  - Nominal non-cumulative dividend.
  - Automatic conversion at a Qualified Financing at the lower of the Cap Price and the Discount Price, subject to the Floor Price.
  - On a Liquidation Event, the investor chooses between conversion at the cap and a 1x return of the Issue Price.
  - Conversion before an IPO, mandatory long-stop conversion and optional conversion.
  - No redemption.
- **Dispute resolution (clause 16):** the seat and the venue are stated separately, with mutual or institutional appointment of the arbitrator. The clause also preserves NCLT oppression remedies.

## India-specific notes

- **Why CCPS:** fully and mandatorily convertible preference shares are "equity instruments" under the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019. Optionally convertible or redeemable preference shares are treated as debt.
- **Private placement:** follow section 42 and Rule 14 of the PAS Rules:
  - offer to no more than 200 persons in a financial year;
  - an offer letter in Form PAS-4 and a record of offers in Form PAS-5;
  - a separate bank account;
  - allotment within 60 days;
  - Form PAS-3 within 15 days [verify];
  - a special resolution, with Form MGT-14 filed within 30 days.
- **Valuation:**
  - A registered valuer's report is needed for a preferential issue (Rule 13 of the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014).
  - For non-residents, a fair value certificate is also needed.
  - Because of the FEMA floor, each CCPS issued at fair value converts into at most one equity share. The cap and discount only bite if the next round is priced above that floor. Model this before fixing the Issue Price.
- **FEMA reporting:** report the allotment in Form FC-GPR within 30 days. Check how the later conversion must be reported.
- **Press Note 3 (2020):** an investor (or beneficial owner) from a country sharing a land border with India needs government approval before allotment.
- **Stamp duty:**
  - *On the agreement:* duty is payable as an agreement under the law of the State where it is executed. E-stamp it before or at signing.
  - *On the issue of the CCPS:* duty is levied at the uniform rate introduced by the 2020 amendments to the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 [verify the rate]. It is collected through the depository for demat shares.
  - An unstamped agreement is inadmissible in evidence until the duty and penalty are paid, but it is not void (*In re Interplay*, 2023).
- **Dematerialisation:** private companies that are not small companies must issue Securities in demat form (Rule 9B of the PAS Rules) [verify the deadlines]. Obtain an ISIN for the CCPS early.
- **Convertible notes as an alternative:** available to DPIIT-recognised startups. The current conditions are at least INR 25 lakh in a single tranche and conversion or repayment within 10 years [verify]. They are simpler at issue but rank as debt.
- **Enforceability:** the CCPS Terms bind the company only if they are in the Articles (clause 17.2). Specific performance of the obligation to allot is available under the Specific Relief Act, 1963, as amended in 2018.

## Customisation checklist

- [ ] Choose cap only, discount only, or cap and discount; set the cap (post-money or pre-money) and the discount
- [ ] Qualified Financing threshold
- [ ] Issue Price, supported by the valuation reports on the same valuation date
- [ ] Investor's residential status and land-border declaration completed in Schedule 3
- [ ] Dividend rate and long-stop period (within the section 55 limit)
- [ ] Information-rights threshold and MFN scope (consider excluding it for non-residents)
- [ ] Indemnity cap and claim window
- [ ] Arbitration: Option A (mutual appointment) or Option B (institutional); seat; venue
- [ ] Who bears stamp duty; demat and ISIN arrangements
- [ ] Owners and dates completed in the CP and Closing Checklist
